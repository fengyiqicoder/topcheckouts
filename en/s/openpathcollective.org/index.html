Paul Fugelsang, a private-practice therapist in Asheville, North Carolina, had to turn away middle-class clients every day who could not pay full price. In 2012 he proposed on his Huffington Post (HuffPost) blog "an online network of therapists, each setting aside a few slots to see low-income clients at $30–50 per session" and publicly asked for help, parked the project under a mental health foundation as an incubated program, and launched in spring 2013; therapists join for free, clients pay a one-time lifetime membership fee ($49 → $59 → $65), and the fee funds the organization. The first therapists came via the HuffPost blog and local media; in 2015–2016 it built a landing page for each of several hundred US cities to capture "affordable therapy + city name" searches, and therapists put Open Path links on their own sites and Psychology Today profiles. More supply meant more cities covered meant more clients, snowballing to 40K therapists, 175K cumulative clients and about $1.9M in revenue in 2023.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a nonprofit directory of low-cost therapy. Clients pay a one-time lifetime membership fee (now $65 / Canada CAD $89) and can see in-network therapists at $40–70 per session (couples/family $40–80, student interns $30), in person or online; it also sells online mental health courses. Sources: site homepage, profile file.
  • Eligibility: household income under $100K and insurance that does not cover therapy (Wikipedia).
  • Founder: Paul Fugelsang (MA, LPC), a private-practice therapist in Asheville, working in mental health since 1996, now Executive Director; in 2015 the team also included Annalisa Birdsall (LMFT) and foundation liaison Virgil Stucker. Source: 2015 Wayback "Who We Are" page.
  • Organization: initially an incubated project of the Foundation for Excellence in Mental Health Care (using its 501(c)3 status), later an independent 501(c)(3), EIN 47-1657042 (established 2014).
  • Domain: registered 2013-01-07 (RDAP); first Wayback snapshot 2013-04-15.
  • Scale (site's "Our Impact in Numbers"): 40K+ therapists, 175K+ cumulative clients, 80K+ course students, operating for 14 years.
  • Traffic (profile): United States 82%; direct 53%, search 42% (brand keywords 95%); referrals from psychologytoday.com 9%, crisistextline.org 7.8%, therapists' personal sites; the site's "Featured In" lists ABC News, Forbes, NYT, NPR.

Where the first users came from: on the supply side (therapists), the founder's public recruitment on his HuffPost blog plus Asheville's local therapist circle and local media; on the demand side, mainly people searching for "can't afford therapy" and therapist referrals (therapists with full caseloads sending clients who cannot pay to Open Path). A specific number for the first 1,000 was not found.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Supply-driven: therapists join for free in exchange for a steady flow of low-cost clients; 40K therapists each have a public profile page, creating a large number of searchable pages.
  • City landing page SEO: /cities/ pages built in bulk in 2015–2016 (several hundred visible in Wayback CDX).
  • Referral network: Psychology Today (9%), Crisis Text Line (7.8%) and therapists' personal sites link to Open Path (profile file).
  • Press: HuffPost, Mountain Xpress, PsychCentral, later NYT, NPR, ABC, Forbes (site's Featured In); "affordable therapy recommendation" articles and TikTok creators (e.g. a 2025-12 @kellymengg video "Huge proponent of Open Path", summary only).

Speculation:

  • Search traffic is now 95% brand keywords, meaning the name has spread through word of mouth and "affordable therapy" listicles, and the city-page long tail now plays a secondary role.
  • Therapists refer clients who cannot pay to Open Path while also listing themselves on Open Path for client flow, so both sides pull each other in.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 2023 revenue $1.919M (membership fees $1.493M ≈ 23K memberships at $65; courses $289K); Executive Director compensation about $280K (Form 990, as relayed by givefreely).
  • 40K+ therapists, 175K+ cumulative clients (site).
  • No venture funding, nonprofit; incubated early on by the Foundation for Excellence in Mental Health Care.

Confidence and gaps
