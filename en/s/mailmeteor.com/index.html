Two founders out of Paris's STATION F (Corentin Brossault, Jean Dubrulle) built a Gmail mail-merge add-on for the G Suite (now Google Workspace) Marketplace in 2019, going head-to-head with YAMM and GMass: double the free quota (100 emails/day), a privacy pitch, and faster performance using Google's newest APIs. The marketplace ranks by rating and installs, so Mailmeteor rode high ratings to "best-rated add-on" and people searching "mail merge" in the marketplace installed it naturally; in 2019-12 it took #1 of the day and week on Product Hunt. In 2020 remote work and remote school made Gmail bulk-sending demand explode, and review count went from 500 to 3,000. It then used a batch of free mini-tool pages (spam word checker, email permutator, email verifier, Gmail export, etc.) and a blog to rank for non-brand search terms, pulling users in from outside the marketplace too; by 2026 the add-on had over 7 million installs.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • App marketplace: the only button on the 2019 product page was "Install from the G Suite Marketplace"; the homepage has always led with "highest-rated add-on in the marketplace" and "X reviews"; review count 500 → 3,000 → 6,500 → 8,000 → 10,000+ → 11,648, installs 7M+.
  • Free quota as acquisition: the free tier sends 100 emails/day (already billed as "double the existing options" in 2019), with paid plans unlocking more volume.
  • Search ranking: a large set of free tool pages (email checker, spam checker, email permutator, Gmail export, behind the email, etc.) plus a blog; our data shows non-brand search at about 24.9%, driven by tool pages; the founder has publicly written about his SEO methods.
  • Multiple Product Hunt launches (7), with the first taking #1 of the day/week.

Speculation:

  • The first 1,000 users came mainly from organic "mail merge" searches inside the G Suite Marketplace and from people arriving via YAMM/GMass comparison pages; the Product Hunt launch (2019-12) was the second push.
  • During the 2020 pandemic, schools, associations and small businesses used Gmail for bulk notices, which likely explains the 6x review growth in 9 months (from memory, unverified: teachers made up a large share of users then).
  • One person installs the add-on and recommends it to colleagues in their team/school, which is B2B word of mouth; the emails themselves carry no branding (privacy pitch), so the output is unbranded.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 7M+ installs; the site says "thousands of paying customers". At $5.99-$35.99/user/month, with 5,000-10,000 paying customers averaging $10/month, ARR would be on the order of $600K-$1.2M (pure speculation).
  • A 2-10 person team, independently run, able to acquire MailTracker, which suggests profitability.
  • Our data: United States 30.5%, India 17.4%; search 31.1%, direct 68.2%.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch: no earlier report in the notes. The "launch" search batch was mostly irrelevant results about meteors and rockets; evidence comes mainly from the site's About page, Wayback homepages over the years, Product Hunt, HN Algolia and the Workspace Marketplace page.
  • User-count growth figures are self-reported on the site but consistent with the marketplace review trend, credible.
  • Gaps: Reddit (u/frenchcooc's posts) and the MailTracker acquisition post were not opened; no founder interview / Indie Hackers post found; exact revenue unknown; Jean's surname comes only from a Crunchbase citation.
