Basics

  • Product: secular, nature-themed, creative homeschool curriculum as PDF downloads (no print copies sold). Early Years Vol.1/2 (ages 2–5), Levels 0–5 at 36 weeks each, Level 6 partly released, Level 7 in development; also U.S. History "A River of Voices" Vol.1/2 and Book Seeds mini units.
  • Prices (Gumroad, 2026-10): Early Years Vol.1 $22, Vol.2 $45; Level 0 $67, Level 1 $133, Level 2 $147, Levels 3–5 $162 each; U.S. History $36 per volume. Three storewide 30% discounts a year (back-to-school 7/30–9/15, 11/15–12/31, etc.).
  • Team: Kristina Garner (founder, curriculum author, co-owner, former preschool teacher/assistant director of an early learning center, BA in theatre, ran two gardening companies); Arlene (joined in 2020, registered nurse and experienced homeschool parent, in charge of community support and the Facebook group "Blossom and Root Families"). The FAQ calls it a "tiny team".
  • Domain: registered 2015-12-23; first Wayback snapshot 2015-12-28.
  • Traffic profile: United States about 99%; organic search 52%, referral 36%, direct 7%; non-brand terms are course terms such as secular ela curriculum and river of voices us history.

Where the first users came from: the evidence points to the founder's personal blog in 2015–2016 plus her Instagram/Pinterest/Facebook readers (all nature-minded homeschool moms in the Waldorf/Charlotte Mason style), with the curriculum growing out of the blog content (About page: "Soon after she began blogging, she started developing the curriculum"). In 2018 the blog wrote a lot about Brave Writer, a big brand in the homeschool world, suggesting she was borrowing that brand's reader circle. There is no public data on first sales.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Community: the Facebook group "Blossom and Root Families" has a dedicated manager (Arlene), and parents exchange tips and show off their work in the group.
  • Word-of-mouth recommendations: named by parents in Reddit r/homeschool threads asking for a "secular science/language arts curriculum"; there is a Testimonials page.
  • Grade ladder: one grade added per year (pre-K in 2017 → second grade in 2019 → fourth grade in 2021 → sixth grade in 2026), and returning users buy the next level as their kids grow.
  • Fixed discount rhythm: three storewide 30% discounts a year drive concentrated purchases (mentioned on the site, Facebook and Gumroad).
  • Long-tail search: non-brand search terms are course names and terms like "secular ela curriculum".
  • Podcast appearances: Homeschool Together, Elevating Motherhood, Modulo.

Speculation:

  • The 36% referral may come from Gumroad, homeschool curriculum review blogs and curriculum comparison directories (Every Homeschool, Cathy Duffy types), not verified one by one.
  • The affiliate links in the blog (Brave Writer, book lists) are both income and an entry point for SEO content.

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public revenue data. With one-time prices of $22–$162 and a new grade each year, it is a digital publishing business with a small team (around 2 people) and steady long-tail sales.
  • By our traffic estimate, monthly visits are not large (see the profile record).

Confidence and gaps

  • A new site written from scratch. The launch time (2015-12), the evolution from blog to curriculum and the launch time of each grade have Wayback evidence, so fairly credible.
  • Gaps: the specific release date of the first course (around early 2017; the 2017 Wayback snapshot only shows the navigation, not a launch post); the Facebook group size; sales. The search results were mostly irrelevant generic articles, and the original Reddit post would not open, so only the summary was read.
