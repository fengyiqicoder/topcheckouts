Basics

  • Product: Crash Bash Online, an unofficial browser port (the site stresses "not an emulator, not a ROM"), supports solo vs CPU, 2–4 player public/private rooms, keyboard/gamepad/touch; currently 28 arenas (the original has 29) across 7 minigame series.
  • Developer: Paolo Furlan (individual; the privacy policy states it is "run by Paolo Furlan, an individual"; Argentine media describe him as a UK individual). The same developer also has the iOS games Bitcoin Factory (App ID 1359240181, an idle mining game listed around 2018) and Wall Street Raider (stock simulator, App ID 6759681029). Small team/solo, no funding information.
  • Hosting: Cloudflare; analytics: Google Analytics; community: Discord (discord.gg/tb8GECFFWW), X, Instagram, TikTok.
  • Launch date: earliest Wayback snapshot 2026-09-20; earliest entry on the site's Updates page 2026-09-19 (key remapping, first two arenas of each series); first press coverage 2026-09-21. The.app registration date was not found in WHOIS (the 2015 returned is the registry date).
  • Monetization: Retro Dodo (9-21) said an "Arena Pass" was planned (paid unlock of remaining arenas, private rooms, ad removal; spectating/joining free), and the page had ads at the time; Italy's Everyeye (9-28) said it "runs on ads and optional donations". But the 2026-10-06 privacy policy says "no ads are shown" and the /pass page now returns 404, so the monetization model was changed in early October (possibly over fear of the rights holders; speculation).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Multiplayer rooms: in PRIVATE BATTLE the host creates a room and "share a code"; friends join via BATTLE BY CODE; empty slots are auto-filled by CPUs, so one person sharing a link can start a match immediately (official site).
  • Zero friction: browser-based, no download, playable on mobile touchscreens; press headlines repeatedly stress "free in your browser", which makes it easy to share.
  • Nostalgic IP + media chain: Retro Dodo → Everyeye (IT) → Cultura Geek (AR), re-reported across languages; server regions expanded from Europe to 6 regions within a week, then Asia was split and South America added, confirming global traffic.
  • Discord hosts the public room posts (the privacy policy mentions "Discord carries the public room posts").

Speculation:

  • The September "checkout visits ≈ 29547" may have come from the Arena Pass checkout page or a donation page; ads/Pass page were later removed.
  • Match clips on X/TikTok bring social traffic (top three social sources are X/Facebook/YouTube; no specific viral post found).
  • The April–June average of 404 checkout visits: the domain's previous use was not found (no Wayback snapshots before September).

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public revenue/user numbers. Indirect scale: 6 regional servers opened within a week + East Asia/Southeast Asia split + South America server, indicating concurrent players worldwide.
  • Pricing: Arena Pass was once planned as paid (price not disclosed), page now 404; privacy policy says no ads.

Confidence and gaps

  • Developer identity, launch date (2026-09-19/20) and media timeline are highly credible (official updates page + multiple outlets).
  • Gaps: where the launch post was, actual MAU, whether Arena Pass was ever actually sold/its price, why ads were removed; high copyright risk (unofficial use of Sony/Activision IP), survival uncertain.
