Basics

  • Product: the official site of the StatQuest YouTube channel (statistics, machine learning, neural networks and AI explainers): video index, the "StatQuest Illustrated Guide" book series (Machine Learning; Neural Networks and AI; Statistics, the last one marked "coming spring 2026"), Study Guide PDFs, merch, free live classes at live.statquest.org, and corporate training.
  • Who made it: Josh Starmer (Joshua Starmer, PhD), formerly an assistant professor in the UNC Chapel Hill genetics department (paulvanderlaken.com 2019 article); the About page says it started as explaining statistics to his UNC genetics colleagues. A personal brand; team size not found.
  • Launch date: Wayback's earliest capture of statquest.org is 2015-10-06, a WordPress blog with the tagline "An epic journey through computational methods for molecular geneticists" and a latest post dated 2015-08-27, "RNA-seq: The Pitfalls of Technical Replicates". The citation example in the FAQ dates the "R-squared explained" video to 2015-02-03.
  • Current scale: YouTube 1.7M subscribers, 301 videos, 96.1M total views (developereducators.com, 2026-10); there is an official account on Bilibili.
  • Traffic (profile file): roughly 4,131 visits in 2026-09; social 35.3%, all from YouTube; direct 48.6%; search 9.7% and all brand keywords; United States 32%, India 26%, Brazil 10%.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • YouTube is the only major entry point: 100% of the site's social traffic comes from YouTube and search is entirely brand keywords (profile file).
  • Repeatedly recommended by students/practitioners in Reddit learning-resource threads and blog lists (Reddit search summaries, paulvanderlaken.com).
  • Receives 2-3 topic requests a day and makes at most 2 videos a month (FAQ), so topics are viewer-driven.

Speculation:

  • The 2017-2020 machine learning job boom let the "clearly explained" series hold long-term rankings in YouTube search; students search concept names before exams and interviews, land on the channel, then follow video descriptions to the site to buy a Study Guide / book.
  • India at 26%: large numbers of Indian data science students and interview preppers (speculation).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Revenue sources: books (Amazon/Lulu), Study Guide PDFs (Gumroad), merch (Spreadshirt), member support ("Double BAM" and other tiers), YouTube ads, corporate training.
  • Scale: 1.7M subscribers, 96.1M total views; no public revenue figures.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Evidence comes from the site's About/FAQ, the 2015 Wayback snapshot, paulvanderlaken.com, and developereducators.com channel stats.

How did Statquest get started?

Josh Starmer of the UNC Chapel Hill genetics department originally wrote illustrated explainers on a WordPress blog to help lab colleagues who did not know statistics understand data analysis (in 2015 the tagline was still "a journey through computational methods for molecular geneticists"), paired with YouTube videos. As the data science and machine learning boom took off, his "no jargon + drawings + sung intro + BAM!" style grew the YouTube channel to 1.7M subscribers, and statquest.org became the channel's landing page and shop, monetizing through illustrated books, study guide PDFs, merch and Patreon-style support.
