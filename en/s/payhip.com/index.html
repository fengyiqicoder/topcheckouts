Basics

  • Product: a hosted ecommerce platform for selling digital downloads, courses, memberships, coaching, and physical goods; includes store building, email marketing, affiliate distribution, and EU/UK VAT handling; from 2025 a Marketplace appears in the navigation (a public product marketplace; listing requires at least $10 in sales and manual review).
  • Team / company: London, UK (167-169 Great Portland Street). The about page lists 10 people (6 in support, 3 in product: Andy Roberts, Kahin Farah, Abs Farah, 1 in marketing). Some online claims say it was founded by Abs and Kahin Farah; the official about page does not directly say 'founders', so medium credibility.
  • Funding: officially self-described as bootstrapped, profitable, with no outside funding.
  • Pricing (2026): Free $0 + 5% transaction fee; Plus $29/month + 2%; Pro $99/month 0% (plus Stripe/PayPal fees).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Sellers share product links (payhip.com/b/xxxx) on Twitter/Facebook/HN/Reddit; there are lots of payhip.com/b/ links on HN from 2015 to 2026 (such as the Crafting Interpreters author's store), and buyers who see the brand on the Payhip checkout page -> some become sellers.
  • Early 'social discount': buyers get a discount for tweeting/sharing (per the WordPress plugin description), now evolved into referral discounts + seller affiliate distribution.
  • Automatic EU VAT handling = institution-driven acquisition (since 2014-12).
  • Gumroad's price increase -> migration traffic from comparison articles/SEO; the Payhip site has lots of 'xxx alternative' landing pages (such as sendowl-alternative) and industry landing pages (sell-travel-itineraries, NFAA/MailerLite partnership pages).
  • Marketplace + public store pages = public-profile-style exposure (public_profile).

Speculation:

  • The social sources YouTube/Pinterest/Instagram correspond to 'side hustle selling digital products' tutorial videos on YouTube and sellers of printable templates/planners on Pinterest, who post images that link back to Payhip product pages; the Payhip mark on free-plan checkout and store pages works as an embed_badge. FAQ

How many checkout visits does Payhip get per month?

About 31,079 last month (Similarweb estimate), #81 of 606; the month before was 29,140. Opening checkout is not paying, so this is not revenue.

Where do Payhip's new users come from?

New-user source: Viral (Built-in sharing). The site has invite or share mechanics, and direct plus brand search is the largest source Direct 69.7%, social 13%, search 10.3%, referral 7%; brand keywords 77.9%. New users are mainly buyers who click into a seller's store or embedded link (bit.ly 3.51%, threads.com 2.44%, rekonise.com 1.85%), and social comes from YouTube, Pinterest, and Instagram.

How did Payhip get started?

A tool built by a small London team in 2012 to 'sell ebooks to your fans': free to open a store, instant PayPal payouts, and a Twitter/Facebook share link built into every book. In 2015 the EU's new digital-goods VAT rules, with Payhip automatically remitting EU VAT, made it the top choice for European indie authors and creators leaving Gumroad; when Gumroad moved to a flat 10% cut in 2023, it caught another wave of migration. The core of growth is 'sellers bring their own traffic': every seller's product page / store link is an ad slot for Payhip, with zero funding for over a decade.
