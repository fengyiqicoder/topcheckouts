Basics

  • Product: USMLE Step 1 membership (2.5 years, 500+ hours of recordings + 3 new live sessions a week + live walkthroughs of 3,000+ questions + a free Coursology question bank), $340; clinical membership (Step 2 CK / Step 3, 200+ hours); a full Step 1 lecture-notes PDF membership (continuously updated on Google Drive); 45-minute one-on-one consulting at $30 or 1,200 EGP; a US clinical rotation service in the past. Within Egypt, payment is possible by Vodafone Cash and cash, and printed books are sold at the Cairo University medical school bookstore.
  • Person: Dr.TA7 = Mohamed Abdel Fatah (written as Mohamed A. Elnagdy in the legal notice), ECFMG certified, American Board of Pathology certified, now living in the US (Facebook says Madison, MS), calling himself "a freelance medical educator in the US".
  • Channels: YouTube @MohamedElnagdy-ta7 (registered 2013-11, 61.3K subscribers, 337 videos, 3.5M views); Facebook dr.ta7.usmle (48.5K followers); Telegram @drta7_usmle (12,403 subscribers).
  • Website: built on Wix, domain registered 2016-07-19.
  • Traffic (our records): Egypt 100%; search 45.6%, direct 39.2%, referrals 9.1%; non-brand search about 27%, mainly terms looking for free lecture-notes PDF downloads.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Friends circle and student-score word of mouth: the high scores of the first students in 2011 were the takeoff point, after which results and Match outcomes spread through Egyptian doctors' circles.
  • Free content as a funnel: the full pharmacology course is free on YouTube as a sample, with 61K subscribers and 3.5M views.
  • Multi-platform community: Facebook and Telegram channels post "USMLE whiteboard lessons", "USMLE audio", livestreams and discounts, and past students appear on livestreams to tell their stories.
  • Lecture notes get searched: in our records non-brand searches are mostly "dr ta7 general principles pdf free download", the notes circulate among students (including pirated copies), which in turn leads people to the official site.
  • Interviews by other creators: interviews on channels like Medicine Way bring exposure.

Speculation:

  • The legal notice in late 2023 suggests a split from the original partner organization (possibly the "USMLE GUIDE" system on Facebook in 2020, or the original partner), after which he turned to a personal brand selling courses and notes directly; no direct evidence.
  • The first 1,000 users most likely came from Egyptian medical students (Cairo University and others) and referrals from the in-person classes in three cities.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Prices: Step 1 membership $340, clinical membership charged separately, consulting $30.
  • If 300-800 people enroll in the Step 1 membership each year, annual revenue would be about $100K-$270K (pure speculation).
  • Community size: YouTube 61.3K, Facebook 48.5K, Telegram 12.4K.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch: there was no old report in the notes. Search results had useful leads in the Facebook page, Telegram and YouTube interviews, while the "launch" and "review" sets were unrelated dictionary pages and CVE pages. The main evidence comes from the official Our Story page, the course and price pages, Wayback, and public YouTube and Telegram data.
  • The founding story comes from the official site's own account, so the timeline credibility is medium-high.
  • Gaps: total number of students, number of Match results, details of the 2023 split, the current status of the collaboration with Mohamed El Masry.
