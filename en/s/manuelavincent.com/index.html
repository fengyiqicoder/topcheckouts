Basics

  • Product: a free "2-hour workflow / Freedom Formula" PDF blueprint (email required to get it), claiming "earn $100-$1,400 a day in passive income with 2 hours a day", with a long income disclaimer on the page calling these "not typical results". What is actually sold is the "Luxe Income Lounge" in a Stan store ("over 12,000 people earning from 8 income streams", with installment and "outside the US only" tiers), a $27 storytelling template workbook, and the Luxe Unlimited community.
  • Tech stack: the page references systeme.io (with a referral code), Kajabi terms pages, and a Calendly booking for a free "dgmastermind" strategy call, showing the funnel uses the upstream program's ready-made template.
  • Person: Manuela Vincent; her YouTube bio says she is 53, has three kids, has been married 30 years, worked at a company for 20+ years, was laid off at 52 and "found a digital program in May 2024"; YouTube @ManuelaVincent has 3,020 subscribers, the channel was created 2011-11-20, country US, "live every day at 11 ET".
  • Other channels: TikTok @manuelavincentdailypay, Instagram/Facebook @officialmanuelavincent, Skool free group "The Second Act Circle" (25 members), Stan store stan.store/manuela52.
  • Domain: registered 2020-04-18 on Namecheap (RDAP).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Paid ads: our channel data shows paid ads 55%, direct 33.8%, email 6.7%, with no search terms; the disclaimer is written specifically for social ad review.
  • Personal story as material: the "50-year-old mom who got laid off" persona runs through the YouTube bio, landing page and Skool group description, targeting people over 45 who were laid off and haven't saved enough for retirement.
  • Multi-platform traffic: daily YouTube livestream + TikTok + Instagram/Facebook, all pointing to manuelavincent.com and the Stan store.
  • Conversion after lead capture: the free PDF is exchanged for an email, after which email and Calendly strategy calls push the high-priced program.

Speculation:

  • The Luxe Income Lounge, "100% profit" and "8 income streams" pitch and the systeme.io funnel template are probably handed out uniformly by the upstream MRR (resale-rights) / affiliate program, and many promoters in the same program use nearly identical pages (from memory, unverified). Her income is mainly referral commissions.
  • First users: after getting in in May 2024, the earliest ones probably came from her own Facebook circle of acquaintances and old YouTube subscribers (the channel was opened in 2011 and she was already making make-money videos in 2020), before she turned to paid ads.

Revenue and scale clues

  • YouTube 3,020 subscribers, Skool free group 25 members, the latest livestream about 825 views; her personal influence is very small.
  • Traffic mainly comes from paid ads, and revenue depends on ad spend and the upstream commission rate; there is no income self-report.
  • "Over 12,000 people" and "helped thousands of families" are the upstream program's standard claims, not her own figures.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch: there was no old report in the notes. The 5 sets of search results were almost all unrelated (rocket launches, VPN reviews, etc.), and only two of her own YouTube videos were useful. The main evidence comes from the current official site, Wayback snapshots from 2020/2023/2024/2026, RDAP, the YouTube channel bio, and the Skool and Stan pages.
  • Credible: the site's evolution (2020 affiliate blog → 2024 single-page funnel), the persona story, the paid-ad share.
  • Gaps: the exact name and commission structure of the upstream program; ad creatives (the Meta Ad Library was not checked); email list size; revenue.
