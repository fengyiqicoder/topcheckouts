A mother and daughter (their family is full of teachers and librarians) built a "serialized romance novel in physical letters" subscription with Canva in 2026: on the 1st and 15th of each month a handwritten letter written in a character's voice is mailed out, with props such as telegrams, newspaper clippings and recipes plus a QR code for an audio reading; one story runs 24 letters over a year, at $14/month or $144/year. The domain was only registered in 2026-06, and the start clearly relies on short video ads on the Facebook page ("This October, fall in love with The Violet Letter"). Basics

  • Product: a physical letter subscription of historical romance fiction. Two stories: "Sight Unseen" (1886 Montana, mail-order bride) and "Dear Stranger" (1943–45, WWII pen pals). Each letter comes with props and a QR code for an audio reading.
  • Pricing: $14/month (cancel anytime), $144/year prepaid (saves $24), $168 one-time gift version (includes a gift message), per the site.
  • Team: site About: "The Violet Letter is a family-owned business created by a mother and daughter… Our family is full of teachers, librarians, principals, book lovers". No names given.
  • Launch: domain registered 2026-06-02 (RDAP; the 2011 Wayback records are an unrelated older site of the same name, a New York Cherry Grove community newsletter); sitemap page lastmod 2026-10-08; the site was generated with Canva (HTML meta app-name export_website, template keyword "plantilla de diseño de sitio web").
  • Traffic (profile): US 100%; search 52%, direct 30.5%, referrals 10.4%; non-brand term "sight unseen" (a generic phrase that shares the story's name); referral titan.email (email clicks).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Facebook video creative (unboxing display, the "sound on" audio selling point, seasonal "This October" copy).
  • A gift subscription tier ($168 with a gift message): the recipient naturally encounters the brand.

Speculation:

  • Physical unboxing suits ad targeting of middle-aged and older women on Facebook/Instagram; gifts and the ritual of "receiving letters twice a month" may bring word of mouth and social media posts, but there is no evidence yet.
  • The subscription box model relies on paid acquisition + cash flow from prepaid annual plans.
