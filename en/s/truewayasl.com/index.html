TRUE+WAY ASL is a fully digital ASL (American Sign Language) textbook made in 2016–17 by a group of Deaf ASL teachers. The pitch is "written by Deaf people themselves, with a searchable sign dictionary, automatic grading, and integration with teaching systems such as Canvas/Brightspace/Blackboard". It does not rely on consumer-side spread: winning one ASL teacher means winning the whole class. The teacher makes it the required textbook, and students must buy the $72–103 eWorkbook through the class-join link the teacher sends. Online classes during the 2020 pandemic made a "fully digital ASL textbook" a must-have. Basics

  • Product: ASL levels 1–4 digital textbook/workbook (eWorkbook), with the "What's the Sign?" sign dictionary, lecture slides, assignments, quizzes, rubrics and placement tests, aligned with ACTFL standards; connects to school LMSs (Brightspace, Blackboard, Canvas, plus its own asl.instructure.com Canvas instance) and ClassLink single sign-on.
  • Who built it: TRUE+WAY ASL (TWA), Austin, Texas (2028 E Ben White Blvd); describes itself as a "100% Deaf-owned, Deaf woman-run LGBT business", with an author team of several Deaf ASL teachers (the 2021 site says "70+ years of combined classroom experience"); about 150 Deaf actors appear on screen. The founders' names were Not found (the YouTube vocabulary video playlist is credited to Trish Wooten, possibly one of the authors, unverified). The early WordPress admin account name was "ntidcatd", which may suggest a connection to NTID (the National Technical Institute for the Deaf) (Speculation).
  • Launch timing: domain registered 2016-11-18 (RDAP); earliest Wayback 2017-07, and by 2017-10 it was already a complete student registration/purchase site (ASL levels 1–3).
  • Pricing: student eWorkbook $72–$102.85 (Austin Community College syllabus, third edition units 1–5); in high schools the teacher usually hands out an access code, while in colleges students buy a code from the bookstore or pay online.
  • Channel profile: US 100%; direct 44.6%, referral 40.5%; search terms are login terms such as twa eworkbook, canvas twa, twa asl log in.

First 1,000 users: from the author team themselves being ASL teachers. They first used it in their own and colleagues' classrooms, then spread it to other schools through the ASL teacher community (conferences, the Deaf community) (Speculation; the "designed by Deaf teachers for teachers" positioning and the teacher conference form are indirect evidence).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Teacher adopts → the whole class is forced to buy: students need the class-join link/access code from the teacher, and register and pay by clicking the TWA module in the LMS (UTRGV and ACC documents).
  • School IT and syllabi write TWA into their processes, lowering the bar for later teachers to adopt it.
  • The site has only a "contact us" form and no public pricing, typical of sales to teachers/districts.

Speculation:

  • Where new teachers come from: ASL teacher association conferences (the site has a Conference Form), Deaf community word of mouth, and the "Deaf-owned" identity (in contrast to Signing Naturally, which is mainly from hearing publishers).
  • TikTok @truewayasl posts short videos about funny moments in ASL classes, mainly for branding rather than acquiring users.

Revenue and scale clues

  • $72–103 per student; last month checkout visits 13,000 and two months ago 31,000 (last month's 12,894 is identical across several sites in the same batch, suspected to be an estimated lower-bound value; two months ago's 31,121 is an independent figure). If tens of thousands of students pay each year, annual revenue could be in the millions of dollars (Speculation, not self-reported).
  • School distribution: universities in Texas (ACC, UTRGV) and Massachusetts (Framingham State) have been seen, and the high school access-code model also exists.
  • No funding seen; "100% Deaf-owned" suggests a small self-owned company.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, first report. Launch timing, product form and payment mechanism are solidly evidenced (Wayback + school IT documents + syllabi).
  • "Took off in 2020" is a Speculation: it rests on the 2021 site repositioning as a "virtual curriculum" and community discussions appearing from 2020, with no user-count time series.
  • Gaps: founders' names, number of adopting schools, student count and revenue all Not found; Reddit original posts could not be opened.
