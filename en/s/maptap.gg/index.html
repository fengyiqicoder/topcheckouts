An indie developer wanted a 'one puzzle a day' geography/brain game, couldn't find one, and built a set himself at nibble.games (MapTap, Swapple, Noggle and others). MapTap has the simplest rules: 5 locations a day, tap once on a globe, scored by distance out of 1,000, then read a short 'on this day in history' story about the place. After playing, one click copies a Wordle-style score text to paste into group chats — friends comparing scores is the entire acquisition strategy. About 10,000 players by mid-2025; in 2026 MapTap was split out onto its own domain, launched on iOS/Android, and gained friend groups, Elo-ranked Versus play and a MapTap+ subscription. On 2026-06-18 TechCrunch published 'MapTap is my new Wordle'; after that, big sites like nytimes.com showed up in referrals and traffic caught fire.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: daily geography game. 5 locations a day, each scored 0–100 then multiplied by 1×/2×/3×, 1,000 max; comes with an 'on this day in history' story. Also practice maps (Magellan's route, Darwin's Beagle voyage, etc.), Find the Flag, Gauntlet, Frontier, Versus play (Elo), flashcards, user-made maps; supports 12 languages.
  • Monetization: MapTap+ subscription at $2.99/month or $14.99/year (7-day trial, giftable) unlocks satellite detail, all practice maps, private groups and replaying the past 90 days; Amazon affiliate links in the stories; promises the daily game will be 'ad-free forever'.
  • Entity: MapTap Inc (App Store / Google Play developer, party to the terms of service). Founder's name not public; the site FAQ is written in the first person ('I'), and nibble.games says 'I wanted daily games like these — but they didn't exist. So I made them.' — a solo developer (name unverified).
  • Platforms: Web (maptap.gg), iOS (id6755205355, 4.9 stars / 16k ratings), Android (gg.maptap.app, 10K+ downloads, 4.8 stars / 327 reviews, Google Play updated 2026-09-28); has a Discord community, r/MapTapGame on Reddit, @maptapper on TikTok.
  • Note: the 'MapTap' on Devpost (a 2026-02 Reddit Devvit hackathon entry) is a Reddit-embedded game by someone else, unrelated to maptap.gg. maptap.space and the MapTap travel app are also namesakes/copycats.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Score sharing: 'Copy to clipboard to share' at the end of every game; the TechCrunch article itself describes 'comparing scores in friend group chats'. Channel profile: direct 74.8%, brand keywords 93.7%, meaning most people hear the name and come straight to play.
  • Groups/Versus: only MapTap+ can create private groups ('Create a group for friends!'), with an invite-code join page at /join; Versus play uses Elo. You have to bring friends into a group to compete, which is an invite mechanic.
  • Press and forums: TechCrunch coverage, daily threads on ILX and CougarBoard; referrals include nytimes.com (8.4%), arseblog.com (an Arsenal fan blog) and the endeavouros.com forum.
  • Daily puzzle plus permanent 'on this day in history' archive pages (/history/<date>) drive both return visits and long-tail search.

Speculation:

  • The nytimes.com referral may come from a mention in an NYT games/tech column or reader comments; unverified.
  • App Store 'Education/Word games' chart exposure after the 2026 iOS launch may have been the second amplifier (16k ratings); no direct evidence.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 'over 10,000 players' by mid-2025 (nibble.games' own claim).
  • iOS 16k ratings (4.9), Android 10K+ downloads. By typical rating rates, iOS installs should be in the hundreds of thousands (speculation).
  • Subscription at $2.99/month or $14.99/year plus Amazon affiliate; the daily game is ad-free. Revenue not disclosed.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written fresh from scratch (no earlier report). The 2024-09 launch, nibble.games origin, 10,000 players in 2025 and the 2026-06 TechCrunch coverage all have snapshot or primary-source evidence.
  • Gaps: founder's name, exact iOS launch date, DAU/subscriber counts not found; Reddit, CougarBoard and ILX would not load, so only summaries were used. 'Press spark' rests on the TechCrunch article and nytimes.com in referrals, but there is no traffic curve to prove a jump around the coverage.
