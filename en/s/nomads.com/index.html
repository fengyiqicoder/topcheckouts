Pieter Levels (@levelsio) first tossed out a publicly editable Google spreadsheet of "cities good for remote work" on Twitter to test demand. Once netizens had filled it in, he turned it into NomadList within a week, hit number one on both Product Hunt and Hacker News on 2014-07-29/30, and was then shared by Forbes, TNW, Tim Ferriss and others, becoming an instant hit. He then converted the traffic into a paid member community (chat groups, in-person meetups, dating and social features), and kept pulling in new users through the founder's huge personal influence on X and the "open startup" public revenue. In 2024 it renamed to Nomads.com and moved to a premium domain.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a digital nomad community and city database: cities scored on cost of living, internet speed, safety and more, with member chat, in-person meetups, trip logs, finding partners and making friends. Originally Nomad List (nomadlist.io → nomadlist.com); in 2024 the main domain switched to nomads.com, and nomadlist.com 301-redirects to it.
  • Founder: Pieter Levels, Dutch, indie developer, one-person company, no funding (bootstrapped, profitable), also makes Remote OK, PhotoAI, InteriorAI and more.
  • Domain: nomads.com was registered in 1997, a premium domain that was bought. Wayback shows nomadlist.com all 301 from 2024-10; an HN user was already citing nomads.com/community on 2024-09-14, so the switch was around September 2024.

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • The founder had an audience first: the 2013 travel post had already reached the HN front page, and on Twitter people helped fill in the spreadsheet as soon as it was posted, so on launch day there were people ready to help spread it.
  • In-person meetups: about 194 a year, about 60K cumulative attendees, and the meetups themselves pull members into the circle.
  • Crowdsourced data: the earliest spreadsheet was built by netizens, and the data users contribute is itself content and an SEO asset.
  • The founder's own media: Levels has several hundred thousand followers on X and has long publicly shared revenue and the building process (open startup), so every tweet brings traffic.
  • City pages can capture long-tail search traffic like "cost of living in X" and "best cities for digital nomads".
  • Speculation:
  • Members pull friends into membership at in-person meetups and in chat groups; dating and social features need the other person to be a member too ("invite to use"), specific mechanism not verified.
  • The last month rebound may be related to a Levels tweet driving sales or a discount campaign.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 2014: sponsorship revenue from day one, "profitable from day one".
  • 2019-03: Remote OK revenue passes Nomad List (Levels tweet).
  • 2020: the two sites together earn about $600K a year; 2022-07: Levels' whole product line earns about $2.9M a year (nocsdegree).
  • Members: 48,659 (2026-10, official FAQ), about 1,500 new per month; membership is a one-time payment (our profile: a Lifetime $19.99 tier). MRR: the /open page is under maintenance, not retrieved.
  • Traffic: about 150M cumulative visits, about 430K per month (official self-report).

Confidence and gaps

  • Second follow-up check: from the official FAQ got the founder's own account of the backstory (the 2013 HN front-page travel post, YouTube income funding full-time travel), that the launch was on the HN, PH and Reddit front pages at once, and the 2026-10 member, meetup and traffic figures. The takeoff method matches the original conclusion; only the multi-select wording was narrowed to "Launch platform".
  • The 2014 takeoff has the founder's original text and HN records, high credibility.
  • The launch dates of the membership program and chat community, and the current MRR, have no first-hand verification (WebSearch quota ran out, and the /open page is under maintenance).
