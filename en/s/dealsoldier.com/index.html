TikTok clearance-hunting creator Sean Sweeney (@superunsexy, the site says 1.7M+ followers) teaches people on TikTok to scan barcodes at Target/Walmart/Home Depot to find hidden clearance prices, and funnels his followers into a paid Whop/Discord group. It was already charging by mid-2024 at the latest (in 2024-05 someone in a reseller Facebook group said their trial was expiring at $35/month; the price later rose to $44/month). It then spread a whole "second-tier distribution network" through Whop affiliate commissions: a batch of TikTok/Instagram accounts named "Deal Soldier - Kathleen / Andrew / Group" (50k to 240k followers) keep posting deal videos for traffic, plus a pile of near-identical "independent review sites" (trydealsoldier, usedealsoldier, dealsoldierreview, godealsoldier, cookgroups and others) grabbing brand keywords like "deal soldier review / reddit / legit"; the standalone domain dealsoldier.com was only registered in 2025-06 to catch search traffic. In essence it is a paid community that monetizes a creator's followers and amplifies them with affiliate commissions.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: A paid membership community that pushes hidden clearance and penny items ("Penny Live") from big-box retailers (Walmart, Target, Home Depot, Lowe's, etc.) via Discord, with nearby store inventory lookup by zip code; also tools like Sniper Tool and Turbo Search; a beginner bootcamp livestream (hosted by "Drill Sergeant" Buddy, replays on YouTube).
  • Founder: Sean Sweeney, TikTok @superunsexy ("Super Unsexy"), USA. Team size and funding not found.
  • Channels: Whop store whop.com/deal-soldier (41,306 joined, 1,466 reviews, 4.9 rating) + standalone site dealsoldier.com (registered 2025-06).
  • Pricing: $44/month after a 7-day free trial (site); $35/month in 2024-05 (Facebook group post summary); a competitor affiliate article claims the current price is $99/month with no trial (inconsistent with the site, possibly a different tier).

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • Creator TikTok content → followers click the profile link → 7-day free trial → $44/month subscription.
  • Affiliate/distribution: review posts with affiliate links since 2024-07; in 2026 a batch of same-template "independent review sites" appeared, all buttons pointing to Whop checkout, exposed by YouTube as duplicate content; these sites specifically grab brand long-tail terms like "deal soldier reddit / legit / review". The official site also built pages such as /reddit, /is-deal-soldier-legit and /walmart-hidden-clearance to occupy those searches.
  • Brand clone accounts: multiple "Deal Soldier - [name]" accounts (Kathleen, Andrew, Group) keep posting deal videos on TikTok/IG, over 400k followers combined.
  • 86.6% of the 40.3% search share is brand keywords, with many misspellings (deal souldier, deal solider), showing people watch a video and then search.
  • Speculation:
  • The clone accounts are mostly promoters earning Whop affiliate commissions or officially signed ambassadors (commission rate not found).
  • Big monthly swings (31.8k three months ago, 12k two months ago, 21k last month) fit "viral-video driven" rather than steady search traffic.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Price: $44/month ($1.47/day), 7-day free trial (site); $35/month in 2024-05.
  • Scale: Whop 41,306 joined (including trial members); Hive Index about 43k; the site claims "Trusted by 5,000+ shoppers" and "thousands of paying members", 1,300+ five-star reviews.
  • Rough estimate: 3,000–5,000 paying members × $44 ≈ $130k–$220k monthly revenue (speculation, unconfirmed).

Confidence and gaps

  • Second-pass check: takeoff year changed from 2025 to 2024, because a Facebook group post proves it was already charging $35/month in 2024-05 and a GitHub affiliate post dates to 2024-07; 2025-06 was only the standalone domain going live. Growth engine changed from "creators or community" to "referral rewards or affiliates", based on the affiliate review-site network, brand clone accounts, and YouTube's exposure of duplicate reviews.
  • The exact date the Discord group opened, the date of Sean's first viral video, and the affiliate commission rate are still not found; the original TikTok, Facebook and Apartment Therapy pages could not be opened, only summaries.
