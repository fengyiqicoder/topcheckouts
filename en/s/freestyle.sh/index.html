Basics

  • Product: cloud VMs/sandboxes for AI Agents: full Debian + systemd, hardware virtualization, about 500ms boot, lateral forking of the entire memory state within 400ms, hibernate/snapshot restore, network rules, up to 64GB of memory; the earlier website deployment API and JS sandbox still exist.
  • Team: Benjamin Swerdlow (CEO, was technical lead of the WHO COVID-19 App Collective in high school, left an Apple consulting role to start up), Jacob Zwang (CTO, dropped out at 19 to join Apple as a senior full-stack engineer); YC S24, partner Diana Hu, 5 people, San Francisco - source https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/freestyle
  • Pricing: free $0; Hobby $50/month + usage; Pro $500/month + usage; YC founders get $50K in credits (alumni $25K) + 6 months of Pro - https://www.freestyle.sh/yc
  • Domain: freestyle.sh has no RDAP data (.sh registry); first Wayback snapshot 2024-04-17; early docs and blog were on freestyle.dev.
  • Traffic: US 30%, India 27%, Indonesia 19%; direct 64%, search 27% (brand keywords 91%), social 5.4% (X, YouTube).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • The customers' products are the distribution: every site an AI app builder deploys for an end user runs on Freestyle (a0.dev 3,000+ domains, Onlook hundreds of deploys a day).
  • Open-source templates drive traffic: the open-source adorable app builder template and the cloudstate runtime lead developers who fork them to deploy on Freestyle by default.
  • YC network: $50K credits for YC founders, $25K for alumni.
  • Hacker News Launch (322 points) + developer meetups + the founder's opinions on X.

Speculation:

  • High traffic from India and Indonesia may come from individual developers using the open-source app builder template.
  • Being benchmarked by competitors/open-source projects (Agentjail) further reinforces the "Agent sandbox" category awareness.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Pricing Hobby $50/month, Pro $500/month + usage; customers are mainly AI app builder and coding Agent companies.
  • Building its own bare-metal data center suggests usage has reached some scale (per the Launch HN); revenue and funding amount not found (standard YC investment, from memory, not verified).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The three product pivots have Wayback snapshots as stage-by-stage evidence, and the Launch HN and YC page are first-hand sources.
  • The launch/review queries in the search results were mostly unrelated pages and CVE pages; only the HN and YC offer page links were used.
  • Gaps: GitHub star count (the API was rate-limited), how the Onlook and other customer partnerships were won, and paying customer count and revenue were not found.

How did Freestyle get started?

Two young former Apple engineers, Ben Swerdlow and Jacob Zwang, joined YC S24 in 2024 with a TypeScript cloud that "turns JS classes into a database with @cloudstate", which didn't take off. In 2025 they pivoted to deployment infrastructure for "AI app builders", open-sourced a forkable AI app builder template (adorable), and won app builders such as Onlook (an open-source AI design tool with 9.4k GitHub stars), a0.dev, vly.ai and Rork as customers, catching the window when vibe coding exploded and "every AI app builder needs to deploy websites for its users". In 2026 they upgraded again to "cloud Linux VMs for coding Agents" (self-built bare-metal data center, 500ms boot, live forks that include memory). The Launch HN in 2026-04 got 322 points and 158 comments, and then the product spread in the developer community through the CEO's frequent X posts arguing "sandboxes should just be VMs", developer meetups and a $50K YC alumni credit.
