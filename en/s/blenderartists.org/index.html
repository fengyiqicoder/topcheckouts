In 2001, Dutch Blender enthusiast Timothy Kanters started a fan site, elYsiun.com (forum + gallery + tutorials). In 2002 Blender's parent company NaN collapsed, Blender went open source and the Blender Foundation was founded; the Foundation put its "Foundation discussion board" on elYsiun, with Blender's father Ton Roosendaal personally moderating it, so elYsiun became the de facto official user community. In September 2005 it was renamed BlenderArtists.org, and over the next twenty years it grew along with Blender's user base, building up through showcase and help boards, contests and long-tail search into an established forum with 190k registered users and 5.8M posts. It is now run by Bart Veldhuizen (bartv) of BlenderNation.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: English-language Blender 3D community forum (showcase, WIP, help, add-ons/resources, jobs, contests). Migrated from vBulletin to Discourse in 2018.
  • Scale (Discourse about.json, 2026-10-25): 192,468 registered users; 602,449 topics; 5,818,492 posts; last 30 days: 4,884 active users, 10,109 new posts, 1,904 new signups.
  • Team: founder Timothy Kanters (handle Kib_Tph, elYsiun ©2001); current manager bartv (Bart Veldhuizen, believed from memory to be the founder of BlenderNation, not verified on the pages checked this time) + a small moderator team (https://blenderartists.org/t/learning-blenderartists/1651003 summary: "managed by @bartv and maintained by a small team of moderators").
  • Revenue: free, no membership fee; monetization not found (speculation: ads/sponsorship).
  • Domain: blenderartists.org registered 2004-04-07 per RDAP; predecessor elysiun.com first crawled by Wayback 2000-10-12, first forum post 2001-10-14.
  • Traffic profile (profile file): US 26%, Germany 5.9%, UK 5.7%; search 68.8% (mostly blender-related brand keywords), direct 23.4%; referrals blender-addons.org, kagi.com.

Where the first users came from: the first elYsiun users in 2001 were Blender enthusiasts around the official NaN forum at the time (first repliers Jamesk, svo, etc.); exact numbers not found. What really turned it into the "official community" was the Blender Foundation moving in during 2002–2003, with Ton himself moderating the board (Evidence).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Upstream software growth: the more Blender users, the bigger the forum (21k members in 2006 → 190k registered in 2026).
  • Official endorsement: the Blender Foundation discussion board lives here with Ton as moderator; the blender.org community page lists it.
  • Community operations: showcase boards, WIP, speed-modeling contest (SMC), splash-screen contest, Suzanne Awards news, Community Journal magazine.
  • Long-tail search: 580k help/discussion topics indexed by search engines; the profile shows search at 68.8%.

Speculation:

  • Current new users more likely arrive from Google via specific question threads; Reddit r/blender and YouTube tutorials have taken over a lot of beginner discussion, so forum activity has declined relative to the 2010s (only the 30-day active count of 4,884 was seen, no historical comparison data).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Non-commercial community with no public revenue; server upkeep is paid by the manager or sponsors (not found).
  • Scale as above: 192k registered users, 5.8M posts, about 4,900 active over 30 days.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. The elYsiun origin, the 2003 Foundation section, the 2005 rename and the 2006 stats all have original Wayback pages; the oldest forum topics were fully preserved in the migration and can be checked directly. Credibility is high.
  • Gaps: why elYsiun was renamed BlenderArtists and when bartv took over were not found; exact dates of NaN's collapse/open-sourcing are from memory. Search results were mostly unrelated pages (juicers, Blender alternatives); this report relies mainly on Wayback and the Discourse API.
