80 Level launched in 2014 as a games-industry outlet under the game payments company Xsolla (the site now says "Owned by Xsolla"; founder Kirill Tokarev). At first it ran developer interviews and industry data, plus a "vendor directory" that included Xsolla Payments, which was essentially B2B content marketing. In 2015–2016 it pivoted to game artists: it found strong 3D work on ArtStation and Reddit and ran "breakdown" interviews with the authors, who contributed for free in exchange for exposure and then shared the articles themselves, giving a steady supply of content and distribution. Large accounts on Instagram, Facebook, X and LinkedIn (about 1.3 million followers combined) added more reach, reaching 1.2 million unique visitors a month. It then monetized through recruiting (80 Level Talent), workshops, event promotion and "Promote your game" advertising.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: news, tutorials, breakdowns and interviews on 3D art and game development; also a recruiting platform (pre-screened talent pool + job postings), workshops, events, the Round Table podcast and a weekly newsletter.
  • Team: owned by Xsolla, with the editorial team operating independently and labeling sponsored content; Kirill Tokarev is the founder (now Head of Special Projects), David Jagneaux heads 80 Level; there are about 10 more people across content, brand partnerships, community, and recruiting and education partnerships (https://80.lv/about).
  • Monetization: brand advertising (banners, advertorials, native partnerships), "Promote your game", media packages (for brands/schools/events), recruiting services, affiliate partnerships.
  • Launch: first Wayback snapshot 2014-05-17 (blank page); by 2015-03 it was a full site, "the best source of information on game industry trends"; the.lv domain registration date is not public in WHOIS.
  • Scale: 1.2 million unique visitors a month; Instagram 451K, Facebook 341K, X 247K, LinkedIn 296K (official About page).
  • Traffic (profile record): US 18%, China 8%, India 6%; non-brand search about 47% of new users (game news terms such as aniimo, dlss 5 release date); social 9.9% (X, LinkedIn, YouTube); referrals from github.com, atlassian.net, note.com.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Institutional start: owned by Xsolla; the early site's vendor directory and sponsor slots were all tied to Xsolla and its partners (tool vendors such as Allegorithmic and Quixel), and content and promotion resources were backed by the parent company.
  • Creator supply: it finds art authors for breakdown interviews, and authors who get exposure reshare on ArtStation and social media (confirmed by the Blender Artists thread); there is also controversy over "reposting work" (r/gamedev post).
  • Social distribution: four platforms with about 1.3 million followers combined keep pushing articles to the art community.
  • News SEO: today 47% of new users come from non-brand search, using game news keywords.

Speculation:

  • The first readers came from Xsolla's customers and trade show resources in the game developer community (the 2015 homepage carried coverage of events such as ChinaJoy, SIGGRAPH and TwitchCon).
  • "Exposure in exchange for content" keeps editorial costs very low: authors write, the platform distributes, and the platform then sells tool vendors advertising and recruiting services on that traffic.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 1.2 million unique visitors a month; about 1.3 million social followers.
  • Revenue comes from advertising, brand partnerships, recruiting and workshops; no public revenue figures.

Confidence and gaps

  • A new site written from scratch. Xsolla ownership and the team come from the official About page; the content pivot timeline comes from original Wayback snapshots, so credible.
  • Gaps: the exact founding date, whether Xsolla was the founder from the very start (from memory, 80 Level was incubated inside Xsolla, unverified), and revenue and the size of the recruiting business were not found; the original Reddit posts would not open, so only summaries were used.
