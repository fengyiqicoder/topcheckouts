Basics

  • Product: the teacher enters content, picks one of 34 templates (quiz, matching, spinning wheel, whack-a-mole, maze chase, crossword, etc.) and gets an activity that can be played on screen or printed; assignment links can be sent to students; there is a public community library (now said to hold 30 million ready-made activities) and AI tools. The free tier is limited; Standard / Pro are subscriptions priced in regional currencies.
  • Company: Visual Education Ltd (T/A Wordwall), England and Wales company number 05900439 (registered around 2006), office in Poole, Dorset; there is also a Wordwall Inc (Delaware). The privacy policy says "we are part of the Harbour Topco (UK) Limited group", presumably acquired or restructured under a private-equity or similar holding owner (date and amount not found).
  • Founders: Josh Smith and Ben (surname not public). Josh Smith worked as a researcher and script editor at Spire TV, BBC Continuing Drama and Kudos from 2003, joined Wordwall in 2006 bringing TV game-show experience; graduate of Jesus College, Oxford. The other founder came from the classroom (the UBC course blog says "teachers' helpers", the official site says "a teacher").
  • Team: about 25 people, fully remote, spread across 12 countries; the careers page says it is profitable with low churn.

Growth loop

  • Evidence:

1. Activities teachers make go into the community library by default, and the homepage shows "examples from the community" with author names.
2. Activities can be sent to students by link to play on their devices, or printed.
3. Public activity pages really do rank on Google: in the 5 search sets this round, queries like "viral story" and "tiktok marketing" had many wordwall.net/resource/… user activity pages among the top results (search ranking, first-hand evidence).
4. Similarweb: search is 47%, with non-brand activity terms such as wheel of names and ruleta si o no.
5. Referrals from school platforms (myschoolapp, Schoology, Genially) show activities are embedded in LMSs.
6. The 2016 move from installed software to the web was the inflection point of the growth curve (UBC article).

  • Speculation: teachers share activity links with each other in teaching groups and Facebook / Telegram / WhatsApp teacher groups, and students see the Wordwall brand while playing; after 2022, wartime remote schooling in Ukraine may have pushed Ukrainian usage further up, explaining why it ranks first (unverified).
