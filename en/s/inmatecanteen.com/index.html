Basics

  • Product: deposits into inmates' commissary/canteen and communication accounts, ordering snacks and daily goods for them (families complain a snack pack costs nearly $48), plus inmate e-messaging, video visitation (TKC Telecom) and prepaid phone. The site is an Angular single-page app with Google/Apple login and Plaid bank linking. Credit card deposits are capped (Eau Claire County: $500 per account/per card, summary only).
  • Operator: TurnKey Corrections (TKC); its site says it has served jails and law enforcement "for over two decades", covering 20+ states and 16,000 inmates "and growing"; software and hardware (the lobby kiosk The Hub) are built in-house; holds a user conference every last month; produces the JailTalk video podcast. Support email ICCSupport@TKC32M.com. The iOS app "Inmate Canteen" is published by TWVending Inc. https://www.turnkeycorrections.com/general-8
  • Origin: a TurnKey bid document for La Crosse County says "Our journey began in 1997" (summary only, PDF 403).
  • Domain: WHOIS registered 2007-09-24 (GoDaddy); the front-end logo filename "InmateCanteen_Logo_Vector_2016_TM" suggests a rebrand/trademark registration around 2016.
  • Note: the 2009 Alabama Department of Corrections "inmateCanteen" online deposit system was built by NIC's Alabama Interactive; it merely shares the path name and is unrelated to this site.
  • Funding: Not found; family-owned/private company.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Traffic comes from "institutional contracts + government website referrals": every contracted county's sheriff website says "go to inmatecanteen.com / team3.inmatecanteen.com to add funds", and TurnKey's own homepage directs families to Team3.
  • New counties are still being signed as of 2025 (Yamhill 2025-01, Davis 2025-02), and an iOS app was launched.
  • Bundling: after signing, multiple services are stacked on: commissary, messaging, video visitation, phone, kiosk hardware; one county site says "commissary profits are used for…" (Steele County, summary only), showing counties take a cut, which is the incentive to sign.
  • Sales/retention run on industry relationships: the annual last month user conference, the JailTalk podcast, and bidding on county RFPs.

Speculation:

  • First users: started around 1997 from commissary/vending business at local county jails in Wisconsin (the developer name TWVending is quite possibly Westby Vending, speculation), then expanded across the Midwest.
  • Revenue comes from deposit fees + product markups + communication fees, shared with counties.

Revenue and scale clues

  • TKC claims 20+ states and 16,000 inmates (this figure may be old copy and on the low side).
  • Prices: families complain a snack pack costs nearly $48 (Reddit r/HuntsvilleAlabama, summary only); deposit fees Not found.
  • Our data: about 180K checkout visits per month on average, mostly US. Similarweb shows peer inecomordering.com had higher last month visits (summary only).

Confidence and gaps

  • "Operated by TurnKey / TWVending" is backed by multiple first-hand sources (app developer, TurnKey website, county government pages), high confidence.
  • Second follow-up check: added TurnKey's owners the Westby brothers, the 1997 origin, the 20+ states / 16,000 inmates scale, county onboarding milestones from 2018–2025 and the county customer list; ruled out confusion with the 2009 Alabama system of the same name.
  • Still missing: total number of contracted counties, fee schedule, annual revenue; most government sites could not be opened via WebFetch, so only summaries were available.
