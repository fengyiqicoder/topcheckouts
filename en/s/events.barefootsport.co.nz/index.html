Barefoot Sport was originally an event organizer in Wellington, New Zealand (Barefoot Triathlon Series, XTERRA Wellington, Splash and Dash, etc., with its own website by 2019). Founders Bengy Barsanti and James Matthews were "fed up for years with results, registration and the finish line not being under their own control", so in 2024 they built their own registration + chip timing platform, events.barefootsport.co.nz, on the white-label system of Denmark's Sportstiming: first migrating participants of their own events, then signing up local New Zealand/Australian organizers with the same pain point (Kāpiti Half, Buller Gorge Marathon, IronMāori, Tough Mudder, Brisbane Trail Ultra, etc.). After that, every event sends hundreds or thousands of participants to the platform to register, pay, check results and share live-tracking pages, and participants see other events there, a typical B2B2C "organizer brings people" growth model; in 2026 it set up the separate barefootsport.net brand for outward sales and launched Orbit AI, a registration customer service agent.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: an online registration + payment + RFID chip timing + live results/tracking platform for Oceania amateur endurance events (triathlon, trail running, swimming, adventure races, HYROX simulation races, etc.); the Barefoot Lite self-service timing kit; Orbit AI ("the first AI agent for event registration and timing", handling transfers, refunds and Q&A).
  • Company: Barefoot Sport Technology (Wellington), local teams in New Zealand and Australia; founders Bengy Barsanti and James Matthews (a team with "40+ years of endurance event industry experience"). Technical base: Denmark's Sportstiming (the 2024-09 snapshot embeds the matomo.dev.sportstiming.dk analytics script); the site says it supports events of 50,000+ participants through European "affiliate partners".
  • Pricing: not public (a cut or service fee on registrations; the site stresses that "overseas platforms take a cut of your registration fees" as a contrast selling point).
  • Scale (self-reported on the site): delivers 450+ events a year across Australia and New Zealand; trusted by 150+ local events; founders have run 100+ events themselves.
  • Domain: the events subdomain first appears in Wayback on 2024-08-09; the parent site barefootsport.co.nz first appears 2019-10 (XTERRA Wellington and Splash and Dash event sites); barefootsport.net first appears 2026-06.
  • Traffic: search 44.5%, direct 40.7%, referral 8.9% (event sites such as trailtrilogy.com and xterraplanet.com, and the email service sendibm3.com); New Zealand 61%, Australia 39%; search terms are mostly specific event results/people's names ("hawks bay womens triathlon 2025 results").

Where the first users came from: participants of Barefoot's own events (Barefoot Triathlon Series, XTERRA Wellington, Splash and Dash). The platform was originally built to take registration and timing for their own events back in-house; after that, participants brought by the other organizers who signed up.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Organizer brings people: for every event signed, all of its participants have to register and pay on the platform (the site lists nearly 20 partner events/brands).
  • Results and live-tracking pages are branded and shareable with friends and family ("Live results and tracking for friends and family, with sponsor branding"); most search traffic is looking up results/people's names, which flows back to the platform.
  • Event site backlinks: event sites such as trailtrilogy.com and xterraplanet.com link to the registration page.
  • Email: registration confirmation / event notification emails (referrals include Brevo's sendibm3.com).
  • A Facebook Pixel is installed (942327286220648), suggesting it runs event registration ads.

Speculation:

  • Sales rely on the founders' connections in the Oceania endurance event scene (fellow organizers) rather than public marketing; barefootsport.net and Orbit AI are active B2B acquisition efforts that began in 2026.
  • Strongly seasonal: the 1–2 months ago rise in checkout visits corresponds to registration for the Southern Hemisphere summer season.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Self-reports 450+ events a year and support for 10,000+ participants per event; Stripe checkout visits about 4.7k in 08 and about 12.9k in 09 (tiered estimate).
  • Bounce rate 27%, 8.4 pages per visit (multi-step registration flow). Revenue and commission rate are not public.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. "Run own events → build own platform" is supported by the official About page's own words and 2019 Wayback, medium-high credibility; the Sportstiming white-label is inferred from page scripts.
  • Gaps: the platform's exact launch date, the business relationship with Sportstiming / bbtiming, fee rates and cumulative registrations all Not found; no founder interviews.
