In November 2022 James Rogers (a former painter and decorator, later a mortgage broker at 978 Finance, and a property investor himself) held a property investor meetup in Stoke-on-Trent where people talk property over curry; the first event drew only 8 people. The format was simple, the ticket was £5 and the mood relaxed, and a year later the anniversary event drew 100+. From 2024 he copied the model to other cities: in each city a local property professional (estate agent, developer, broker) acts as host, willing to organize it for free because the event brings in clients for their own business, and they promote the local events on their own Facebook and Instagram. Events grew from 2 in January 2024 to 6, then 30 by November, and 50 by the end of 2025, with nearly 1,500 members, and it has even opened in the Netherlands. Revenue comes from tickets, the £37.50/month membership, masterclasses, national conference tickets and sponsors.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • City replication: the same format (curry + guest talk + networking) is copied in every city, with events going 2 to 6 to 30 to 50.
  • Local hosts: each event says "Hosted by" a local professional, who promotes the local events on their own company's social accounts, which means every new city adds another promoter.
  • Meeting in person to membership: the fixed monthly meetup builds the habit of coming back, and then it sells the £37.50/month membership, masterclasses and the national conference.
  • Search: users search "brand + city" to find local events, plus about 11% paid search.
  • Podcasts and industry awards (Blue Bricks, summary only) bring exposure within the industry.

Speculation:

  • The hosts are mostly estate agents, mortgage brokers and developers who use the events to win clients, which is why they are willing to organize them for free; whether there is a licensing fee or revenue share was not found.
  • Guest speakers also repost their own appearances, bringing their followers.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Nearly 1,500 members (podcast, 2026-09). If all were paying members at £37.50/month, monthly revenue would be about £56K (speculation; "members" may include free signups).
  • £5 tickets, 42+ events a month; a national conference of about 180 people × £50.
  • A sponsors page exists; amounts not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The founding date and event growth come from the founder's own LinkedIn post and podcast introduction, credible but self-reported.
  • Most search results were irrelevant; no Reddit discussion.
  • Gaps: the host model (whether licensing fees are charged), the actual number of paying members, Companies House company information.
