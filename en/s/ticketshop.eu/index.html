SIA First Row Technology, a long-established small ticketing company in Riga, Latvia, has run the local ticket site ticketshop.lv since 2012 (selling Latvian shows). After 2022, many Russian-speaking singers, bands and stand-up comedians who had left Russia began touring the Baltics and Europe for Russian/Ukrainian-speaking diaspora audiences, and it went with the flow by becoming the official ticket seller for those tours. In the second half of 2024 it bought ticketshop.eu, which had been parked for years, and brought it into use in March 2025, widening the business from "Latvia local" to "pan-European tours by Russian-language artists" (Little Big, Morgenshtern, Monetochka, Verka Serduchka and others). New customers are sent mainly by the "buy tickets" buttons on artists' websites and artists' social accounts, plus "artist + city + tickets" long-tail search.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: online ticketing (concerts, theater, festivals, stand-up), e-tickets sent instantly, with optional ticket insurance from Balcia Insurance; multi-currency (EUR, PLN, CHF and others); has event-specific subdomains (such as readyfest.ticketshop.eu for READY FEST in Warsaw).
  • Who built it: SIA First Row Technology, registration no. 40103552768, address Riga, Rupniecibas str. 21/3–29, Latvia; footer "© 2012 - 2026". The About page still says "purchasing tickets to various arts and sports events in Latvia and the rest of the Baltics". Founders/shareholders not found (Lursoft and firmas.lv both would not open).
  • Charges: single tickets sold per event, e.g. EUR 20–99, PLN 129–179, CHF 50.
  • Launch date: ticketshop.lv's Wayback snapshots start in 2013, and by 2015 it was a Latvian-language ticketing site; by 2024-01 ticketshop.lv had English/Latvian/Russian interfaces. ticketshop.eu's snapshots from 2018-08 to 2024-08 are all domain-parking pages (parkingcrew "Inquire about this domain"), and from 2025-03-19 it becomes a 301 redirect to the real site, meaning it was bought after August 2024 and put in use in March 2025. Today ticketshop.lv redirects to /lv and ticketshop.eu to /en.
  • Traffic (profile data): Poland 23.7%, Russia 20.2%, Ukraine 11.1%; direct 52.4%, search 35.7% (non-brand about 29%, such as "ready fest warsaw", "morgenshtern ticket barcelona", "little big bratislava"); referrals from artist sites monetochka.world 2.0%, unnvtour.com 1.7%, tyndamusic.com 0.7%.

Where the first users came from: the old users were local Latvian audiences from the ticketshop.lv era (evidence: the Latvian-language site since 2013). After the pan-European expansion, new users come from the artists: ticket links posted on artists' websites/social accounts (evidence: Monetochka's direct link, and the top three referral sources are all artist sites), and audiences then search "artist + city + tickets" (profile search terms).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Artists' websites point their buy-ticket buttons to ticketshop.eu, bringing referral and direct traffic.
  • Each show gets its own event page, capturing "artist + city" long-tail search.
  • Tours by Russian-language artists across European cities (Poland, the Baltics, Germany, Spain, Cyprus, Hungary) are concentrated on this one platform.

Speculation:

  • It signs exclusive ticketing with tour promoters/agencies, and one deal covers a whole multi-city tour, so more artists -> more audience accounts -> promoters of the next tour are more willing to use it (a weak version of two-sided network effects).
  • The official Instagram account has only 20 followers, so the platform does almost no brand marketing itself and relies entirely on artists to bring customers.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Event IDs have reached about 107,000 (cumulative); about 12 recent concerts visible at a time. Best Tranco rank is about #296,784 (summary only, ipaddress.com). Revenue not found; under a ticketing commission model, annual gross sales are presumably in the millions of euros (speculation, unverified).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. Apart from the official site's pages, Instagram and WHOIS, search results were mostly irrelevant. Key evidence comes from the Wayback history of the two domains, the company information on the About page and external links from artist sites.
  • Latvian company registry data (founding date, shareholders, revenue) could not be opened; "the outflow of Russian-language artists in 2022" is background common knowledge, and no report was found on when ticketshop started working with specific artists.
