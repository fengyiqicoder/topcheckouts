After that, through "artists show their work + developers collect feedback online", it became Krita's official community, with traffic rising and falling with Krita's user base and releases. Basics

  • Product: the official artist community forum (Discourse) for the open-source painting software Krita, with artwork showcases, tutorials, help requests, feature feedback and development discussion.
  • Operation: run by volunteers, with permission from the Krita Foundation (a non-profit Stichting in Deventer, the Netherlands); admins/moderators include Halla (Boudewijn) Rempt, Wolthera, Dmitry Kazakov, Scott Petrovic, Takiro Ryo, tiar and others, and core developers are active on the forum directly.
  • Scale (/about page, 2026-10): 5,461 active users in the last 30 days, 2,486 new sign-ups, 11,112 posts, 703 new topics.
  • Charges: the forum itself is free; money flows to the Krita Development Fund (individual membership from €5/month, corporate from €6K/year; Intel was the first corporate sponsor at €30K/year), which is where the checkout page traffic goes (speculation).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Official endorsement + migration: krita.org news, old forum posts, BlenderNation and others brought the existing users over in one go.
  • Artists post work (Artwork section, monthly challenges, featured pieces on the krita.org homepage / splash image) → featured artists repost → new artists join.
  • Developers collect feedback on the forum in person, and users are willing to stay.
  • Continuous traffic from the official site: the krita.org homepage, social icons and the monthly development report's "featured artwork from the forum" all link back to the forum. Featured artists repost, forming an "official exposure in exchange for submissions" loop (visible in the 2026 monthly reports).
  • Reddit routing: when people on r/krita ask about a community, answers generally point to krita-artists.org.
  • Exclusive resources: the profile data shows most search traffic is resource terms like "krita brushes / krita themes". Brush pack authors post resources on the forum and users come to download them (creator supply).

Speculation:

  • The "Welcome" page / help menu inside the Krita software links to the forum, so software downloads translate into forum acquisition.
  • Googling "krita some problem" lands on forum posts, consistent with Organic Search being the top channel (long-tail search is the main engine now).
  • Donate and Dev Fund links in the forum header / posts lead to the checkout page.

Revenue and scale clues

  • The forum has no revenue; the Krita Dev Fund had about $2.7K–3.2K/month from 238–243 individuals in one snapshot (date unknown), and about €8,768/month in another period.
  • 5,461 active in 30 days, about 2,500 new sign-ups per month.
