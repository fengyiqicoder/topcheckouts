cause.link is not a public-facing petition platform but a petition landing-page tool that US progressive online organizers use to grow their lists: the domain was re-registered in 2025-01, and from 2026-03 each petition is published under a short slug (e.g. dcrjtap202743, aoc-defiance-21826). Basics

  • Product: petition signature pages (Laravel-style frontend, with a /login backend where organizers create petitions). Current homepage petition: "Tell Congress to stop the billionaire takeover of our media!" (opposing Larry Ellison's merger of Paramount and Warner), 2,778 signatures / goal 5,000; signers can choose to display their name publicly.
  • Operator clues: the signature page says "you may receive periodic updates from Dose of Democracy and its partners"; the privacy policy links to https://www.beagoodinfluence.com/privacy-policy (Be A Good Influence LLC). Good Influence describes itself as a "creator-led advocacy marketing firm" offering supporter and donor acquisition and direct-response fundraising; testimonials come from WisDems, MoveOn and Evergreen Action, and the logo wall includes DCCC, Unite Here, Earthjustice and Harris Walz (logo names inferred from file names). The exact relationship between cause.link and Good Influence (in-house tool or partner) not found.
  • Pricing: signing is free; pricing for organizations launching petitions not found.
  • Domain: cause.link currently registered 2025-01-26 (RDAP); 2018 Wayback snapshots belong to a previous owner; redirects appear from 2025-02; the first petition URL snapshots date from 2026-03-17 to 04.
  • Traffic: United States 100%; direct 49%, email 38%, paid social 7.6% (Facebook); Apr–Jun 8,506, Aug 0, Sep 3,316.

Where the first users came from: the evidence points to partner organizations' email lists (ref/cdmc tracking codes, email 38%) and Facebook ads (fbclid, paid social 7.6%); the "petition_redirect" parameter shows that signers are sent on to the next petition, cycling the same pool of people.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Email blast → petition signature → default opt-in to Dose of Democracy and partners → next blast goes to a bigger list (snowballing list growth).
  • Facebook ads drive traffic to petition pages (fbclid).
  • After signing, petition_redirect sends the user to the next petition, raising signatures per person.

Speculation:

  • Petitions are the acquisition hook; the real output is a supporter email list that can be used for fundraising (Good Influence's core business is supporter and donor acquisition).
  • Petition slugs carry dates (260505, 260514), suggesting new petitions weekly or even daily as rapid responses to news events (media mergers, AOC, etc.).

Revenue and scale clues

  • No direct revenue data; monetization likely comes from service fees for acquiring supporters/donors for clients, or list sharing (Speculation).
  • Individual petitions reach signatures in the thousands (2,778 / 5,000).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site written from scratch. Google results are almost entirely for the unrelated root-cause-analysis software Causelink (causelink.com) and getcauselink.com. Evidence comes from the homepage text, URL parameters in Wayback CDX, RDAP and the Be A Good Influence website.
  • Gaps: who runs "Dose of Democracy," its relationship to Good Influence, and whether cause.link charges anyone were not found; the petition content is political advocacy, nothing illegal.
