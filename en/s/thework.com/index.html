This is the official site of Byron Katie's "The Work": not an internet startup but the online home base of an offline spiritual practice. Katie had an "awakening" after depression in 1986 in the California desert town of Barstow, began holding workshops around the country in the early 1990s, registered thework.com in 1996, and by 1998 the site already had a message board, local contacts and an event calendar; in 2000-12 Time magazine called her a "spiritual innovator for the 21st century", in 2002 Loving What Is, co-written with Stephen Mitchell, was published, and more than a dozen books since (including one co-written with Eckhart Tolle) have kept sending readers to the official site. The core of the growth loop is "free method + facilitators who replicate it": the site offers free worksheets in about 39 languages, videos and a helpline; students can host their own events and become facilitators (there are many theworkwithXXX.com personal sites and free Zoom practice groups), and each facilitator sends people back to thework.com to download worksheets; existing users return for the three-days-a-week "At Home with BK" Zoom livestreams and the podcast.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: official site of The Work: a self-inquiry method of four questions plus turnarounds; free Judge-Your-Neighbor worksheets (including children's/teen versions), the yellow card, booklets etc. (mostly multilingual), a video library, a podcast, the helpline at helpline.thework.com; paid events (in person in Ojai, on Zoom), books, the School for The Work and Turnaround House (Ojai, California).
  • Entity: Byron Katie International, Inc.; also The Work Foundation (accepts donations). Earlier there were the Center for The Work (Barstow) and The Work Foundation, Inc. (Manhattan Beach).
  • People: Byron Katie (born 1942, real name Byron Kathleen Reid); her husband Stephen Mitchell (translator and author) co-wrote several books.
  • Domain: RDAP registration 1996-12-12; earliest Wayback 1998-12-02.
  • Users: United States 46.7%, Germany 8.0%, Brazil 6.1%; the site has about 39 languages.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Free tools: worksheets and other materials are all free to download, in about 39 languages; the homepage has stressed "everything is free" since 2012.
  • Books and media: Time magazine coverage, several bestselling books (Loving What Is etc.), co-authorship with Eckhart Tolle.
  • Facilitators and students hosting their own events: search results show many personal facilitator sites (theworkwithhanae.com, theworkwithdaniel.com) and free Zoom practice groups (theworkrunsfree.com), all sending people back to thework.com for free materials; the site also encourages "Host an event".
  • Community: Reddit r/thework (posts since 2013); the Facebook page promotes the podcast and Zoom livestreams.
  • Traffic: direct 47.1%, search 39.6% (mainly the author's name and book titles, brand keywords 63%).

Speculation:

  • The real "takeoff" was the 2000–2002 Time coverage plus the publication of Loving What Is, which turned a niche workshop method into an internationally known one; the site was the landing spot for that wave of readers (the timing fits, but there is no site traffic data to confirm).
  • The facilitator certification system (Institute for The Work) plays a role in the spread, but its specific pages were not opened this time (only a redirect page was retrieved).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Revenue comes from events, the School for The Work, books and donations; no specific figures found.
  • Site in about 39 languages, Facebook page active for years; user and revenue scale not found.
