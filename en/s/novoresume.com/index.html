Three Romanian students studying abroad (Stefan Polexe, Cristian Letai, Andrei Kurtuy) entered a school innovation contest in Copenhagen in 2014 with the idea of "a good-looking, well-organized resume template". They registered a company in 2015, first put up a waitlist page (subscribe for exclusive templates/a giveaway), and launched the resume builder in 2016-02. Two months later (2016-04) they used their own template to make a "one-page Elon Musk resume", which was picked up by Business Insider, Yahoo Finance, Forbes, Time and others, instantly pushing the brand internationally. Since then they have captured long-tail search with a large number of career blog posts such as "how to write a resume / resume examples for X role", monetizing through free-to-use templates plus a paid premium tier, and now claim 18 million job seekers have used it.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Where the first users came from: before launch, a waitlist page + Facebook page collected early-bird subscribers (2015-12); two months after launch, the one-page Elon Musk resume was republished by major outlets such as Business Insider, the first large-scale exposure (evidence). The Copenhagen Business Academy's report on the entrepreneurs shows the school circle also brought early users.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Content marketing with celebrity resume samples: the one-page Elon Musk resume was republished by mainstream business media (2016-04).
  • Career blog SEO: from the 2015 articles subsite to today's /career-blog ("internship resume", "executive resume example", "how to write a cover letter", etc.), and many search results are its own blog posts.
  • Freemium: resumes can be made for free, with premium templates/features paid.
  • Recommended in AI assistants: GEO traffic is 4% (ChatGPT 3.3%).

Speculation:

  • India accounts for 25% of traffic, related to the large long-tail search volume for English job-seeking content in India.
  • Exported PDF resumes carry no obvious branding, so word-of-mouth spread is limited, and growth relies mainly on search and brand return visits.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Claims 18 million users; pricing from $21.99/month; team of about 11. No funding information seen.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The founding story, launch date and the Elon Musk resume event all have Wayback originals and the Yahoo republished original.
  • The search results for launch queries returned unrelated pages like "launched" in resume writing, and were not used.
  • Gaps: the number of sign-ups the Musk resume brought; the yearly user growth curve (the Wayback homepage has no yearly user counts); revenue not found.
