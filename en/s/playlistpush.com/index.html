A "playlist pitching" two-sided marketplace whose domain was registered in 2017-04 and launched in June. On one side, long-tail blog posts such as "how to contact Spotify playlist curators" and "how to make money from playlists" (from 2017-07) recruit both independent musicians who want to promote songs and curators who want to monetize; musicians pay by the number of curators reached (now from $280) and curators earn money for each song they review. In 2019 it launched an affiliate program so music blogs/influencers earn commission on referrals, and during the 2020-2021 pandemic surge in demand for independent music promotion it added TikTok creator promotion. Basics

  • Product: paid music promotion platform. Musicians submit songs and pay by the number of target curators/TikTok creators; curators (1,000+ real followers) earn $1.25-$15 for each song reviewed, and TikTok creators earn money for making videos with the song. Promises "real people, no fake streams", does not guarantee results, and unreviewed submissions after 14 days are refunded as account balance.
  • Company: PlaylistPush LLC (site footer). The founder is not listed on the site; help center authors are signed Steve and Ludo; founder identity not found.
  • Pricing: Spotify campaigns from $280 (average about $450), TikTok campaigns from $350 - source https://www.chartlex.com/blog/streaming/playlist-push-review-2026 (a review written by a competitor).
  • Domain: playlistpush.com registered 2017-04-10 (RDAP); first Wayback snapshot 2017-06-05. The site says "founded in 2017".
  • Traffic (profile file): US 18%, India 10.7%, Brazil 7.9%; direct 59.9%, search 36.8% (brand keywords 96.8%), AI 3.2%; very few referrals.

Where the first users came from: no original text from the founder about early acquisition was found. What can be confirmed: a month after launch it began writing long-tail blog posts aimed at both sides (curators' "how to make money from playlists", musicians' "how to contact curators"), and the early homepage was backed by musician testimonials ("over 250K streams", "2 million streams"). The first curators were presumably recruited with the "monetize your playlist" pitch (speculation); the source of the first musicians was not found.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Two-sided network effects: more curators mean bigger reach and more willingness to pay by musicians; the money musicians pay goes to curators as review fees, attracting more curators (homepage figures roughly doubled each year: 250→500→970 curators).
  • Content/SEO: Spotify promotion tutorial blogs since 2017 and a curator interview series; the site now has landing pages like /spotify-playlists-promotion, /tiktok-music-promotion and /spotify-curator-program.
  • Affiliate commissions (since 2019-10): commissions for referring either musicians or curators, with discounts for referred musicians. Many "best Spotify promotion services 2026" review articles list it (some of these may be affiliate content, speculation).
  • Supply-side community: the official subreddit recruits curators with "Get paid to create playlists".

Speculation:

  • That 96.8% of search traffic is brand keywords shows new customers mainly come from word of mouth, review articles and Reddit "is it legit" discussions, then return by searching the brand.
  • During the pandemic (2020-2021), demand for online promotion among independent musicians rose, and TikTok becoming a main channel for music discovery drove the second stage of growth.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 50K+ musicians × an average campaign of about $450 (the average given by a competitor's review) gives a rough cumulative GMV in the tens of millions of dollars (speculation, not verified).
  • Trustpilot has 1,421 reviews; the site claims 5,982 reviews with a 4.6 rating.
  • Curators earn $1.25-$15 per song reviewed; the platform's take rate was not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The product and growth trajectory rely mainly on year-by-year Wayback homepage figures and the affiliate page, which are fairly reliable; the "launch" queries in the search results were all unrelated health web pages.
  • Reddit could not be opened, so related content uses only search snippets.
  • Gaps: founder identity, source of the first musicians, the original Rolling Stone/Fortune coverage and dates, affiliate commission rate and revenue figures were not found.
