Format Finder is an AI tool made by One Peak Creative, a short-video course company. The company had a website by late 2021 and sells TikTok/Reels courses through free live masterclasses by instructor Meg Jackson, claiming "161,453 students/creators". In spring 2026 it turned the "viral formats" from its courses into an AI tool (topics, hooks, scripts, retention analysis, one-click editing), and the homepage reuses the course students' numbers and cases directly. The first users were those hundred-thousand-plus former students and the weekly masterclass audience. The most expensive Peak tier ($97/month) even bundles 9 courses. So it is not a new site built from zero but a "course company migrating to SaaS": it converts its existing student list and ad funnel into subscription revenue, and new customers come from brand search, the course funnel and SEO resource pages.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: an AI tool for Reels/TikTok/Shorts/Facebook creators. Features: 65+ proven viral formats by niche, each with a training video; generates hooks, scripts and storyboards; second-by-second video retention analysis; One Click Editor for automatic cutting and captions; cross-platform view tracking. There is an iOS app (App Store id6772323754, developer One Peak Creative, 21 ratings, average 3.6, "members only").
  • Pricing: Starter $29/month (200 credits), Pro $59/month (unlimited), Peak $97/month (unlimited, includes 9 courses, listed as "$1,599+ value"); annual billing is half price, working out to about $15/month at the lowest.
  • Parent company: One Peak Creative Inc. / One Peak Software Inc., which sells 9 self-paced courses (from $58, including the TikTok and Reels Creator Course, Video Ad Academy $398, Content Creator Academy $497, Scale Meta Ads to $10M), a coaching program called Viral Content Accelerator, and a free weekly 90-minute live masterclass. Instructor Meg Jackson says she delivered the same training to Amazon's global social media team. The official site does not name a founder.
  • Domain: the earliest Wayback snapshot of onepeakcreative.com is 2021-12-08; the earliest for the formatfinder subdomain is 2026-03-31. The RDAP lookup failed.
  • Users: US 72%, Canada 13%, Netherlands 6% (channel profile).

The first 1,000 users: almost certainly from One Peak Creative's former students and masterclass list. Evidence: the homepage on day one already stated 160K students, the Peak tier bundles courses, and Trustpilot reviewers say they bought courses before. No specific conversion numbers were found.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Course funnel: weekly free live masterclass → courses/coaching → Format Finder subscription, with the Peak tier bundling courses into the subscription.
  • Student cases as social proof: "Mary Beth got 20M+ views on one video", plus case pages for Betsy and Kaitlyn with million-view videos.
  • SEO resource library: from 2026-10 it launched systematically with how-tos, comparison pages (format-finder-vs-chatgpt) and glossary pages; search is 35.9% in the channel profile.
  • Ads: a Trustpilot review mentions "adverts"; the parent sells a Meta ads course and has strong ad-buying capability.

Speculation:

  • Direct traffic at 52% suggests it relies mainly on the old students' email list and login return visits, not viral spread.
  • The videos users produce carry no brand, so the product itself has no sharing loop.

Revenue and scale clues

  • The parent brand claims 161,453 students/creators (cumulative course users, not Format Finder paying users).
  • Subscriptions $29-97/month; the App Store has only 21 ratings and Trustpilot 88 reviews, suggesting Format Finder has roughly a few thousand paying users (speculation).
  • The homepage shows logos of corporate training clients such as Amazon, Downy, Expedia and Marriott.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch (no earlier report). Search results were almost entirely irrelevant; the evidence comes mainly from the official site, the parent company's site, Wayback, the App Store and Trustpilot.
  • Gaps: the founder's identity (Meg Jackson is only confirmed as the instructor), the parent company's origin story (whether it began with a personal YouTube/Instagram account), and Format Finder's paying user count and ad spend were not found.
