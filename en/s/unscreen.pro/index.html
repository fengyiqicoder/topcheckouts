Basics

  • Product: AI video background removal (no green screen), supports MP4/MOV/WebM up to 2GB/4K, background replacement, API on paid tiers; the free tier is preview/watermarked only and non-commercial.
  • Who built it: no company or founder found; the site is signed only "Unscreen Pro", and the FAQ states it is an "independent service, not affiliated with Unscreen.com or Canva". GitHub user crevas created an Unscreen-Pro repo (0 stars, 13 commits, just a README pointing to the site), presumably for backlinks.
  • Pricing: at launch (2025-10-30 snapshot) Plus $29/mo for 3,000 credits, Pro $99/mo for 10,000 credits; now Plus $4.99/mo for 500 credits, Pro $99/mo for 10,000 credits. The entry price was cut sharply.
  • Domain: RDAP registration 2025-10-08 (.pro); Wayback has old snapshots from 2021-12 and 2022-08 (blank "Loading..." page, previous holder); first snapshot of this product 2025-10-30.
  • Traffic (profile data): United States 22.2%, India 8.1%, Iraq 3.8%; search 60.1%, direct 29.9%, social 6% (Reddit); non-brand search about 48.1% (video background remover, remove video background, etc.), and the original brand keyword "unscreen" also brings volume.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Brand-name interception: the domain and product name copy the original site's paid tier "Unscreen Pro", paired with "shutdown migration guide / alternative" pages.
  • Search ranking: 60% of traffic from search, about half on non-brand generic terms.
  • Backlink seeding: empty GitHub repo, AI tool directories, advertorial site articles.

Speculation:

  • The 6% Reddit social traffic may come from links dropped under "what to use now that unscreen is gone" threads.
  • The entry price cut from $29 to $4.99 is to compete on conversion with similar interception sites like unscreen.io.

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public figures; the site only says "thousands of creators". Subscription at $4.99-$99/mo.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Search results were mostly irrelevant or competitor pages; evidence from the site, Wayback snapshots, RDAP, and the VEED article.
  • Operator, real user numbers after launch, and revenue all not found. A CSDN article claims Unscreen "closed on June 29, 2026 and redirects to Canva", conflicting with the official FAQ's 2025-12-01; the 2025-12-01 date confirmed by multiple sources is used.
