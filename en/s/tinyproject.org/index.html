Basics

  • Product:
  • The Tiny Post: a physical letter subscription mailed on the 15th of every month, containing an original 4x6 art print, a handwritten-style letter, stickers, small paper goods, a matching Spotify playlist, journal prompts and a seasonal recipe; ships in the US and internationally, with monthly, 3-month, 6-month (10% off), 1-year (20% off) and gift plans (Stripe payment links). The page says "Over 5000 Members!". Source: https://www.tinyproject.org/tinypost
  • Tiny Farmers Market: a monthly community market held in her own front yard, about 25 stalls, accepting vendor applications. Source: https://www.tinyproject.org/tiny-farmers-market
  • Other: Studio Series craft classes, an events calendar, the online shop shop.tinyproject.org (selling past Tiny Posts, framed art, supplies and merch), the "Learn From Us" tutorial at $67 (was $99) and a $150 one-off "Pick My Brain" video consultation. Source: https://www.tinyproject.org/learnfromus
  • Pricing: the Tiny Post page gives no dollar amount (not found); tutorial $67, consultation $150.
  • Who runs it: Hannah and Nathan (Learn From Us page: Hannah handles brand and social content, Nathan handles operations and business); Hannah's YouTube channel is named Hannah Gustafson (@hannahleegustafson). Location: Austin, Texas, US (Linktree bio).
  • Launch timing: the old site thetinyfarmersmarket.com was registered 2025-08-08 (WHOIS), built on Webador, copyright "© 2025 - 2026", and the Wayback 2026-04 snapshot already sells Tiny Post and "now Internationally"; tinyproject.org was re-registered on 2025-10-24 (RDAP; the 2005 Wayback captures belong to an unrelated earlier holder) and has been captured by Wayback since 2026-04; the old site now shows "We've Moved!" and redirects to tinyproject.org/tinypost.
  • Traffic (profile data): brand keywords 87.2% (the tiny post, tiny post app, the tiny project snail mail), social 7.7% from YouTube, direct 48.9%, search 38.4%; United States 91%, France 9%.

Where the first users came from: Hannah's own social accounts. The Learn From Us page says outright that the business grew "organically" on the audience built through Hannah's organic social media; the Linktree points Instagram, TikTok (@tinyproject), Pinterest and YouTube all to Tiny Post signup. On the site, brand keywords make up 87% and social comes mainly from YouTube, which suggests people first got to know her and Tiny Post on social media, then searched her name to reach the site. The offline front-yard market (about 25 stalls) provides local word of mouth and filming material. No press coverage, Product Hunt or Reddit launch was found.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Founder-led content: Instagram 58.5K followers (verified), YouTube and TikTok keep posting craft / market / letter content, with the Linktree leading straight to the subscription.
  • Offline community: a monthly front-yard market + vendor applications + performer signups keep producing content and local word of mouth.
  • Gift subscriptions: the 6-month and 1-year gift plans let existing members send them to friends, bringing in new users on their own.
  • Brand partnerships: the first Linktree item is a 20% discount affiliate link for Pens.com, and there is also a partnerships email.
  • Monetizing experience: sells a snail mail business tutorial and consulting.

Speculation:

  • "Opening a letter" lends itself to short video, and members showing off letters on social media may bring secondary spread (speculation; no member UGC evidence was found).
  • The 9% of traffic from France may come from one overseas viral video after international subscriptions opened (speculation).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Members: the site says "Over 5000 Members!", the Learn From Us page says "Nearly 5,000".
  • Revenue: the tutorial URL hints at a "$50K-a-month snail mail business", but the page text does not say so, not verified. If the subscription price is estimated at $10–15 a month (price not found, speculation), 5,000 members would mean about $50–75K in monthly revenue, the same order of magnitude as the number in the URL (speculation).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Apart from the official site, Instagram and Linktree, most search results were unrelated; the evidence comes from the site's pages, the old site, WHOIS/RDAP and Wayback.
  • The exact Tiny Post price, its starting month and which video sparked takeoff were not found; TikTok and YouTube pages returned no data; the member count is officially self-reported.
