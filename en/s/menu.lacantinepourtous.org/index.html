This is not a "product site" but the parent ordering entry point of the Quebec nonprofit La Cantine pour tous. Growth does not come from the website itself: the organization first built a coalition of community organizations from the 2000s (La Table des écoliers) into about 100 member organizations (community kitchens, work-integration enterprises, social economy enterprises), then worked with schools to bring "from $1, pay what you can" elementary school lunches into schools; once a school joins, all of its parents are guided by the school and its after-school care service to register and order on this platform. It received funding from Quebec's Ministry of Education starting in 2020, the federal government announced a 3-year, CAD 65.2 million school meals fund in 2025, and Quebec allocated another CAD 9.3 million in 2026, with the number of schools rising from 3 in 2019 to 263 now. So the first users were parents at the pilot schools, and new users come from "every new school = a new batch of parents".One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: La Cantine pour tous's online ordering platform. Parents register for free, create a profile for each child (allergens can be flagged), a new menu opens every two months, and parents order lunch for their children by day; meals are delivered to the school and handed out by the after-school care service. Pay what you can, as low as $1 per meal, with the difference covered by the program; no proof of income is needed and the school cannot see how much each family pays (https://lacantinepourtous.org/ecoles/). A separate program, "La Cantine à domicile", delivers frozen meals to seniors (from $6), with its entry point at commander.lacantinepourtous.org.
  • Current state: menu.lacantinepourtous.org/fr now 303-redirects to app.lacantinepourtous.org/connexion, matching the 2025 "new online ordering platform launch" (https://10ansderepas.ca/a-propos/).
  • Operator: La Cantine pour tous, a food security charity in Quebec made up of about 100 member organizations across 14 regions. Its predecessor was the community organization collective La Table des écoliers (from the 2000s), and one of its drivers, Ghislaine Théoret, died on 2026-08-07.
  • Domain: lacantinepourtous.org registered 2016-04-29 (RDAP), with a transfer record on 2019-10-18. The menu subdomain has no Wayback archive (login page).
  • Funding: foundations including Hesse, J.A. De Sève, Saputo, Famille Léger and Desjardins, plus Quebec's Ministry of Education.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Institutional distribution: once a school joins the program, meals are delivered to the school and handed out by the after-school care service, and parents order through the platform. Schools that haven't joined can fill in a form to queue (https://lacantinepourtous.org/ecoles/).
  • Funding-driven expansion: after the Ministry of Education first funded it in 2020, the number of schools rose from 15 to 40, 100+ and 263; federal and provincial school meals funding in 2025–2026 amplifies this further.
  • Member organization network: about 100 community organizations and social economy enterprises prepare and deliver the meals, the foundation of supply-side expansion.
  • Menu cycle reminders: a new menu opens every two months, and parents who agreed to receive email get notified, which is the mechanism for returning visits (matching the 5.2% email in our data).
  • In 1–2 months ago it holds 15-minute free online info sessions for parents, concentrating new-user acquisition in the back-to-school season.

Speculation:

  • The 65.5% referrals most likely come from the main site lacantinepourtous.org and from notices/links sent by schools; there is no specific referring site data.
  • New users are highly concentrated in the back-to-school season (1–2 months ago).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Nonprofit: parents pay from $1, and the difference is covered by government and foundations.
  • Scale: 263 schools (official site), the homepage says 84,000 students; 1 million+ meals in a single school year in 2024; 4 million+ school meals cumulatively.
  • Government funding: CAD 9.3 million from Quebec in 2026; federal CAD 65.2 million over 3 years (for school meals across Quebec, not all going to it).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The timeline comes entirely from the organization's own tenth-anniversary page 10ansderepas.ca/a-propos/, with medium-to-high credibility (official account).
  • The search results were almost entirely irrelevant; the Les Affaires press release returned 403 and only the summary was used.
  • Gaps: whether the ordering platform is built in-house or outsourced; the number of registered parents; when the menu subdomain was replaced by the app subdomain (only known that a "new platform" arrived in 2025); whether the homepage's "84,000 students" and "263 schools" refer to the same school year.
