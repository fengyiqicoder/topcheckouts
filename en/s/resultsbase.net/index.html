Results Base is an amateur-event chip-timing company in Milton Keynes, UK. It was officially founded in April 2014 by a group of people who had "worked in the events industry for many years, even decades" (the footer copyright counts from 2011, so its predecessor is older), and it won local events through the founding team's existing organizer relationships - the best example being the 10,000-person Milton Keynes Marathon, a partnership of 7+ years. Its growth is not pulled in by marketing but is "sign one organizer = bring a whole event's participants": organizers collect money through its online entry form, participants create a profile, pay a service fee and come back after the race to check results and photos, the results pages get indexed by search engines, and the same profile keeps being used the next time they enter another event. During the 2020 pandemic it switched to "virtual races" (hundreds of them, including a round-the-world challenge) to survive the offline shutdown, and it now calls itself "the UK's leading chip timing company", with about 120K monthly visits.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a one-stop service for organizers - online entry (payments via Stripe; Results Base keeps only a "booking fee" per transaction and passes the rest to the organizer), RFID chip timing (in-house equipment, can mix helmet chips/ankle chips, wireless split points, live results), LiveTiming self-service timing, QR-code timing, virtual races; sister company Your Event Supplier supplies bibs, custom medals, event packs, arches and timing clocks. For participants: a personal profile (can claim results from other timing systems), entry, and results lookup.
  • Coverage: running, relays, OCR, triathlon/duathlon, swimming, dog runs, road/mountain/gravel/track cycling and more; clients range from first-time events to 15,000+ participants.
  • Company: Results Base Ltd, company number 08980122 (a registration number from 2014, consistent with "officially founded in April 2014"), address Newport Pagnell MK16 (Milton Keynes); VAT 184 0253 22. Founders' names are not public.
  • Domain: WHOIS created 2014-03-04; earliest Wayback capture 2014-05-10; by 2015 it was already an ASP.NET site (home, services, events and results, news, gallery).
  • Traffic (profile): search 46.5% (non-brand about 28%, mostly "some event results"), direct 37.9%, referral 9.3% (event sites such as thelakesman.com); UK 91%. xranks estimates about 122K monthly visits (summary only).

Where the first users came from: organizers in the founding team's existing events-industry network (running, cycling and triathlon events in Milton Keynes and central/southern England), each bringing their own participants to Results Base to enter and check results. The official site stresses that the team has "many years, even decades, of hands-on experience in the events industry".

Where new users come from: newly signed organizers (event sites link to the entry page, referral 9%); participants searching "event name + results" (non-brand search 28%); profile reuse (the same person entering other events); and automatic profile creation when organizers bulk-import entry data.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Organizers bring people: online entry is bundled with timing, so signing one event brings all of its participants.
  • Results pages carry the brand and are searchable, so friends, family and participants revisit after the race.
  • Unified profile: historical results from other timing systems can be claimed, which strengthens retention.
  • Sister company Your Event Supplier cross-sells medals and bibs, increasing each organizer's lock-in.
  • Pandemic virtual races: while offline events were shut down, participants could still enter and pay on the platform.

Speculation:

  • Word-of-mouth referrals between organizers (the UK amateur event scene is small); reviews on directory sites such as Race Directors HQ are also a customer acquisition point.
  • Clear seasonality, with autumn/winter trail and night runs and the spring marathon season.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Revenue = the booking fee on each entry + timing/materials service fees; exact rates are not public.
  • About 120K monthly visits (xranks, summary only); a single client such as the MK Marathon has about 10,000 entries; the site says it has served events of 15,000+ people.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The founding date (2014-04) and business model come from the official site's own text, so confidence is high.
  • Search results were largely irrelevant (Reddit-style scam/piracy posts, PH guides); no founder interviews or press coverage.
  • Gaps: founders' names and backgrounds, what the 2011 predecessor was, number of events and entries per year, rates; the Companies House page could not be opened.
