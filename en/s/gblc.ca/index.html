Basics

  • Business: official test centre + training. Exams: TCF Canada, TCF Québec, TCF for all audiences, TEF Canada, TFI, TOEFL iBT, TOEIC, GRE, Pearson VUE, Kryterion, Surpass; courses: graded French classes A1-B2 and TCF prep courses (about $1,000-1,920 each).
  • Address: 716 Gordon Baker Road, Unit 211, North York (near Highway 404 and Steeles/Finch).
  • Price: full TCF/TEF Canada about CAD $380-420 including tax (PrepMyFrench 2026-08); the profile file records CAD $452 / $520; the site also has single-section prices such as $220 and $231.30 CAD.
  • Website: bilingual English-French site rebuilt on Next.js, with an account system, online date selection, passport upload and payment; there is a "Scam Alert" page warning against buying certificates/test seats from third parties.
  • Our data: Canada 90.9%, US 4.8%, India 2.0%; direct 48.4%, search 38.7%, referral 7.7%; non-brand search about 39% (tcf exam booking, tcf toronto, etc.), plus a result-checking keyword (glb tcf test result).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Demand is external: candidates take the TCF/TEF for Express Entry, provincial nomination and citizenship; test seats are tight (PrepMyFrench says weekend sessions fill quickly).
  • Search capture: about 39% of non-brand search is exam registration keywords; each exam has its own landing page.
  • Third-party exam-prep sites (PrepMyFrench etc.) write test-centre guides that bring referral traffic.
  • Checking results after the exam and signing up for courses bring returning users back (direct 48%).

Speculation:

  • The takeoff point is around 2023, when French became an immigration bonus and seats were scarce; the capture ability improved after the switch to an online registration system in 2025-01.
  • Immigration consultants, French training groups and communities like Reddit r/ImmigrationCanada probably spread "which centre has seats" by word of mouth; no specific posts found.
  • Scarce seats gave rise to fake bookings/scalpers, so the site built a dedicated Scam Alert page.

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public revenue. Exams cost about CAD $380-520 each and courses $1,000+; if a few hundred candidates sit each month, annual exam revenue would be around the million-CAD range (pure speculation).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Address, authorizations, exam types and Wayback/RDAP are first-hand evidence; "since 2017" is self-reported. The policy tailwind is inference, with no traffic curve for this site.
  • Search results are mostly unrelated content such as BCLC lottery and CVEs; the site is client-side rendered, so body text came from page data extraction.
  • Gaps: when it obtained TCF/TEF authorization, who runs it, candidate volume, Reddit discussions.
