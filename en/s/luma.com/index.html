In March 2020, as COVID lockdowns began, Victor Pontis tweeted "who's building something for the COVID response, I can help", and Dan (Danqing) Liu reached out to build a tool that "moves offline small businesses onto Zoom". Victor's friend Whitney had just been laid off from a yoga studio and needed to teach on Zoom, so within a week the two built ZmURL (turning a Zoom invite into a nice registration page). A week after launch it was covered by TechCrunch and accepted by YC (which they then turned down). In 150 days hosts ran 15k events with 250k attendees; in 2020-09 it was renamed Luma and launched "refer a host, each get $100", and in 2020-12 raised a $3M seed round. It then shifted from Zoom events to in-person events: every event page carries the lu.ma domain and is forwarded by hosts to all their guests, and the next host among those guests uses it in turn. It ended up as the default event registration tool in the tech and crypto scenes, and in 2025 moved to the luma.com top-level domain.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: event pages + registration/ticketing + invitations/reminders + calendars (community home pages) + newsletters + a discovery page (luma.com/discover), with Zoom integration and multi-currency payments.
  • Pricing: free plan has unlimited events and guests, with a 5% platform fee on paid events; Luma Plus $59/month (billed annually) with 0% platform fee and more (https://luma.com/pricing).
  • Founders: Dan Liu (Co-founder & CEO, signs the blog posts), Victor Pontis (co-founder, formerly at PicnicHealth and Benchling, had founded 3 companies before and been rejected by YC 3 times) (https://web.archive.org/web/2021/https://blog.lu.ma/why-we-turned-down-yc/).
  • Funding: $3M seed in 2020-12, led by Maven Ventures and Venrock (https://web.archive.org/web/2021/https://blog.lu.ma/seed-round/). A Series A afterwards is from memory, not verified.
  • Domain: started on zmurl.com (2020-04 snapshot), lu.ma from 2020-09 (that domain was listed for sale with a Chinese registrar in 2019–2020 and became the Luma site from 2020-10); luma.com was registered in 1995 per RDAP (an old domain), was a parked page from 2024 to 2025-05, became the main Luma site from 2025-08, and lu.ma 301-redirects from 2025-10.
  • Traffic profile (profile file, checkout-page basis): US 47%, India 6.6%, UK 6.1%; direct 81.7% (guests clicking links shared by hosts), brand keywords 87.9% (luma hackathons, luma bangalore), social 6.9% (LinkedIn, X, Instagram), email 3.7%.

Where the first users came from: the evidence points to (1) the founder's friend Whitney's yoga classes (the first host); (2) Victor's tweet drawing TechCrunch coverage, which brought the first wave of hosts within a week; (3) small teachers and creators forced online by the pandemic (yoga, fitness, cooking, podcasts).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Branded output: every event page, invitation email and calendar invite carries the Luma brand and domain, and hosts proactively send them to all guests; the profile shows direct visits at 81.7% and brand keywords at 87.9%.
  • Referral rewards: the 2020-09 host referral program (each gets $100, conditional on the referred host earning $1,000).
  • External event tailwind: COVID moved in-person events onto Zoom, 15k events in 150 days.
  • Press: featured on TechCrunch a week after launch.

Speculation:

  • After 2022 the crypto/tech scene (ETHDenver side events, hackathons, AI meetups) collectively adopted Luma, creating a cohort effect of being the "default tech event tool"; the search terms "luma hackathons" and "luma bangalore" support this, but the exact start time was not verified.
  • The discovery page (2024) turns guests into registrants for more events, increasing on-site retention.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Revenue: 5% platform fee on paid events + Luma Plus subscriptions (from $59/month).
  • Scale: 2020-09 cumulative 15k events / 250k guests; 2020-12 cumulative 500k+ people. No official numbers since (from memory there is larger scale and a Series A, not verified).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. The founding story, first users, referral program and seed round all come from the original official blog posts (Wayback), so credibility is high.
  • Gaps: the original TechCrunch article, the specific milestones for moving from Zoom to in-person and taking over the tech scene after 2021, and later funding and revenue were not found. The reddit/launch/review search groups returned nothing and were mixed with the same-named Luma AI (lumalabs.ai), which was excluded.
