Basics

  • Business: dedicated coaches, shuttles and Park & Ride for concerts / festivals / big events, selling return tickets one at a time per show (Trustpilot reviews mention about £20-22 each way, £73.95 for two returns, plus a booking fee). Routes are centered on Scotland (Glasgow, Edinburgh, Stirling, Midlothian, etc.).
  • Operator: the footer says "You Rock We Roll LTD," tagline "You Rock We Roll." Claims to carry "tens of thousands of passengers a year." Founder not found.
  • Founding year: the website says 1996; the 2005 archive says "established since 1997" and "Established in 1998," which are inconsistent, so take 1996-1998.
  • Our data: United Kingdom; direct 45.9%, search 40.6%, referral 8.1%; non-brand search about 24%, with terms being specific show names such as bon jovi murrayfield, radio 2 in the park stirling, biffy clyro edinburgh 2026; social has Facebook. A third-party estimate is about 500 unique visitors a day (updowntoday, summary only).
  • Reputation: Trustpilot has 84 reviews, TrustScore 1.8, 62% one-star (queuing over an hour, being left behind when the coach was full, phone not answered), profile unclaimed.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Piggybacking on shows: every big show gets a route and a page; the roughly 24% non-brand search in our data is all "show name + place," showing new customers find it by searching for event transport.
  • Early offline outlets: the 2005 archive says tickets were sold through independent outlets with bookings by email; the 2012 site still kept a Ticket Outlets page.
  • Repeat customers: direct visits are 45.9%, fans use it again and again each year.
  • The Facebook page promotes individual shows.

Speculation:

  • The first users were local Scottish fans going to festivals like T in the Park in the late 1990s, through record-shop outlets and word of mouth, with no direct evidence.
  • It may have a partnership with show promoters (such as DF Concerts) or be listed as an official transport option, and the 8.1% referral may come from venue / promoter pages; not found.

Revenue and scale clues

  • "Tens of thousands of passengers" a year (self-claimed on the website); a single journey is about £20-22. Roughly 30K-50K trips x £20 gives annual ticket sales of about £600K-£1M (speculation, order of magnitude only).
  • A third-party estimate is about 500 visitors a day.

Confidence and gaps

  • The founding time, operating company, early operating method and route types all have primary evidence from the website and Wayback; the Trustpilot data is credible.
  • The search results provided were mostly irrelevant; a TripAdvisor thread would not open.
  • Gaps: the founder, the relationship with show promoters, real revenue.
