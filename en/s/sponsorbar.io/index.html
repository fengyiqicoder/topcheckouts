Basics

  • Product: Mac menu-bar app. Shows one ad in the menu bar, counted per "full valid one-minute impression"; users get 80%, the platform keeps 20% (FAQ). Advertisers top up a minimum of $15, CPM about $2 (floor $2, re-auctioned hourly), new advertisers get $3 for 7 days; later added targeting and an AI agent (MCP) ad-buying entry point.
  • Payouts: Whop from $3 (200+ countries), Stripe from $10 (34 countries); a payout method must be linked before the balance reaches $5, otherwise ad serving pauses.
  • Referral program: 14-day last-click attribution, referrer gets 5% of the referee's base earnings, newcomer gets +5% for 30 days, paid out of the platform's 20% cut.
  • Team: KickBot Inc. (© 2026 KickBot Inc.); founder Henry Kutrieb (X @henry_kutrieb, registered 2021-08, about 1,239 followers, bio "Building tools for streamers | @KickBotApp @VoxBotLive @TheRumbleBot").
  • Domain: RDAP query returned nothing (.io); first Wayback snapshot 2026-09-04, launch day.
  • Traffic (profile): newly appeared in 2026-09 with 4,851 visits; US 49.2%, India 42.9%, Russia 7.6%; social 41.1% (X, YouTube), direct 45.1%, search 8.2% (sponsorbar, sponsorbar not counting).

Where the first users came from: the founder's single launch tweet on X. With only a thousand-odd followers, the counterintuitive "pay to rent your menu bar" concept triggered quote-tweets and mockery across dev circles, taking one tweet past a million views; the social quotes on the homepage show the first two days ran mainly on X's quote-retweet chain.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Controversial concept → X quote-retweets (the site shows both positive and negative comments) → downloads.
  • Referral rewards (referrer 5%, newcomer +5%) → deal-hunter/passive-income circles post referral links on communities like V2EX.
  • Whop payouts covering 200+ countries → opens up users in lower-income regions like India and Russia (India 42.9%); more users mean more sellable impressions → attracts advertisers ($3 trial, MCP ad-buying entry).

Speculation:

  • Early advertisers are mostly indie developers buying menu-bar exposure for their own products, the same crowd as the user side, forming a self-loop.
  • The "sponsorbar not counting" search term shows users are highly sensitive to counting and earnings; retention depends on per-user income (a few dollars a month).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Platform revenue = 20% of advertiser spend. CPM $2 per minute-impression; the site's example account balances are $42.68 and $28.42, and an example campaign spent $146.28 (examples, not totals).
  • No public user or advertiser counts; the launch tweet's 1.146M views and 4,873 bookmarks are the strongest scale signal. About 5k monthly visits (profile).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Launch-tweet metrics come from raw fxtwitter API data, revenue-share and referral rules from the site's FAQ, so credibility is high.
  • A second check found the V2EX post says "70% to users", which conflicts with the current FAQ's 80%; the FAQ is taken as authoritative (the split may have been adjusted later).
  • Gaps: exact dates and data for follow-up tweets (Whop payouts, targeting); actual install base and number of paying advertisers; whether any YouTube video drove traffic (profile lists YouTube as a social source).
