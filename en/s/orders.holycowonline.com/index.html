This is not an internet product but the own-brand ordering page of Holy Cow, a London Indian takeaway chain. The business runs offline: takeaway shops opened one by one in residential areas, "delivered in 45 minutes, freshly made, customizable to taste," building up through word of mouth and repeat customers in each neighborhood. The website simply moves regular customers from the phone and delivery platforms to its own ordering system (Andromeda ordering SaaS + Stripe payments), saving the platform commission. Basics

  • Business: Holy Cow, a London Indian delivery/collection chain; the website lists 9 locations: Angel, Archway, Balham, Battersea, Bermondsey (opening soon, new-shop code Holy30 for 30% off £30), Hammersmith, Kilburn, Putney, Limehouse-Canary Wharf (the latter also has dine-in). Shortlisted for "Best Indian Restaurant" in 2021 (website banner). Has its own iOS/Android app.
  • Ordering system: the orders.holycowonline.com footer says "Powered by Andromeda" (a UK takeaway ordering SaaS), "powered by Stripe" for payment, and the server is Azure IIS. There was also an older ordering domain, www.holycow-orders.com (recorded on Wayback from 2014-10).
  • Prices: ordered by the dish, mango lassi £4.55, garlic naan £4.25, salmon £12.95, veggie box £9.95.
  • The main domain holycowonline.com was registered 2009-09-04 (RDAP; Wayback archives exist from as early as 2008-01, possibly a re-registration). Founder and founding year not found.
  • Our data: United Kingdom; search 50.6% (brand keywords 77.5%, such as holy cow hammersmith, holycow restaurant; non-brand about 10%, e.g. gunnersbury local food and wine curry), direct 37%, social 7.4%.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Local brand search: of the 50.6% search share in our data, brand keywords are 77.5%, and the search terms include branch area names (hammersmith), showing most new customers are "locals who know Holy Cow" searching for a shop.
  • Repeat purchase: direct visits are 37%, regular customers come back to order; there is an app and a loyalty points program (from the profile record).
  • New shops pull in customers: new shops are promoted with a discount code (Holy30).

Speculation:

  • It started with offline neighborhood word of mouth and flyers/phone orders (the standard approach for London takeaways in the 2000s), with no specific evidence.
  • Delivery platforms (Deliveroo, Just Eat) may bring in lots of first orders, and discounts on the own channel then pull regular customers back to the orders. subdomain; no direct evidence.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 9 shops (including 1 opening soon).
  • Revenue and order volume not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • The number of shops, the ordering system vendor, and the domain and archive timeline all have primary evidence.
