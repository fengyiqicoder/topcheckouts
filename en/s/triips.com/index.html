Basics

  • Product: a flight deal alert membership (the "Club") subscribed by departure airport. The system tracks all routes from your city and emails you when it finds fares 50–90% below the usual price, 2–3 per week. Clicking through redirects to Skyscanner to book; you cannot search by destination or date. It currently covers 52 departure points.
  • Team: co-founders Arnaud and Omar, surnames not public. The site says the team is a dozen or so people, 5 nationalities, fully remote. Another site, triips.net/about, says "headquartered in New York, remote team across 12 countries, founded 2025", so the two accounts are inconsistent.
  • Pricing: 7-day free trial, then $59/year (BoardingArea 2026-08). The homepage promises "save at least $500 in the first year or get a refund", and there is also a free tier ("Join for Free").
  • User distribution (profile): United States 22.6%, France 14.2%, Australia 10.9%.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • TikTok creator voiceover ads: scripted videos of "a flight attendant / someone who has been to 37 countries tells you cheap flight secrets" (@travelforless and others) that send people to the free trial page. BoardingArea judged it to be an ad. One Trustpilot negative reviewer accuses Triips of paying people to make fake TikTok videos (unconfirmed).
  • Free trial auto-converting to paid: the 7-day trial asks for payment details and auto-charges $59/year at the end. This is the main complaint on review sites and Trustpilot, which suggests conversion mainly relies on default renewal.
  • City-by-city replication: each new departure airport is a new market (Toronto → Canada → US → Europe).
  • Affiliate program: the site links to a Notion "Affiliate Resources" page, and it is hiring a growth lead for "content, social and creator partnerships".
  • Member photo sharing: the homepage shows member trip photos and numbers like "Saved $650", encouraging people to send their trip selfies to the company.

Speculation:

  • The first users (5k in the first month in Toronto) most likely came from paid Meta/TikTok ads plus the free trial rather than organic word of mouth: it took only a few weeks to reach 10K, the landing page is a typical ad landing page, and "is it a scam" discussions appeared right at launch (2025-03).
  • 86% of search traffic in the profile data is brand keywords, and paid traffic is not in the top three. The ads are most likely run in-app / on short-video platforms and users later search the brand name to return to the site, which is why third-party traffic tools do not show Paid.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Self-reported 200,000+ members (including free members), with members saving $2M+ cumulatively.
  • $59/year. Assuming paid members number 20–50K, annual revenue is about $1–3M (speculation, unverified).
  • Trustpilot has 654 reviews at 4.5, 12% one-star; the platform notes "no recent active review invitations".

Confidence and gaps

  • Second follow-up check: the growth engine was changed from "Referral rewards or affiliates" to "Paid ads", based on BoardingArea's report of TikTok creator ads and the auto-renewing trial model.
