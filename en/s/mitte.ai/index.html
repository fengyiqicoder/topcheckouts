A multi-model AI creative suite built by a small Berlin team (2-10 people on LinkedIn, incorporated in Delaware as Mitte Labs Inc, founder and CEO Azer Koçulu). The domain was bought in mid-2024, a waitlist went up at the end of the year, and in February 2025 it opened "start creating for free". It has no models of its own; it grew by putting every new model on the platform the moment it launched. In February 2026, when ByteDance's Seedance 2 was still hard to use overseas, it pitched "no VPN, no queue"; a Substack tutorial and an Instagram creator (one post with 12K likes) followed with promotion, and the official X account said users had generated "tens of thousands of videos" since Seedance 2 launched. It then packaged models and techniques into individual "Apps"/presets (celebrity-encounter videos, hotel lobby AI videos, character sheets), each App page targeting the search terms of a specific viral technique, and opened a creator program where others publish Apps and earn a commission per call. New users mainly come from two kinds of search and social content, "new model launches" and "viral video templates", and then pay a monthly subscription ($16-$499).One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a browser-based AI creative suite aggregating hundreds of image/video/audio models including Sora, Veo, Kling, Seedance and Flux, plus an asset library, team collaboration, an AI assistant and "Apps" (preset workflows). In 2026-09 it also became a Claude MCP connector (https://mitte.ai/updates).
  • Pricing: free 250 credits per day; Basic $16/month to Studio $499/month, with annual discounts; 1,000 credits = $1 (https://future-stack-reviews.com/mitte-ai-review/, recorded 2026-06).
  • Team: legal entity Mitte Labs Inc (Delaware, Dover); job postings show a Berlin office; LinkedIn says founded 2023, headquartered in Berlin, 2-10 people, 1,058 followers (https://www.linkedin.com/company/mitte-ai). The imprint names Azer Koçulu as founder and CEO (relayed by the review article above). Azer Koçulu is probably the developer behind the 2016 npm left-pad incident (from memory, unverified).
  • Domain: registered 2024-06-24 (RDAP); in 2024-07 Wayback still shows a Sedo parking page.
  • Funding: PitchBook has a profile page (https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/1462754-80); the review article says there is no funding disclosure from official channels.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • New-model launch window: Seedance 2, Kling 3, gpt-image 2.5, Kling 4.0 and others go live as soon as they are released, backed by X posts and blog tutorials (Seedance 2.5 Prompt Guide, Blender to Seedance 2) that capture "how to use model X" traffic.
  • Viral templates capture search: our data shows non-brand keywords are specific technique terms such as "hotel lobby ai", "quavo & takeoff - hotel lobby ai video" and "character sheet", with new-user SEO around 24%; the homepage and App pages are built around viral techniques (celebrity-encounter videos).
  • Creator supply: the creator program lets outsiders publish Apps and earn a commission per call (https://mitte.ai/creators), which effectively has others produce new "search landing pages + techniques" for it.
  • Social: X, the YouTube tutorial channel (@mitte_ai), Instagram creator reviews.

Speculation:

  • The 12K-like Instagram Kling 3 review and the Substack tutorial were probably paid or affiliate partnerships (no disclosure, unverified).
  • The first batch of users most likely came from the late-2024 waitlist and the founder's personal influence in the developer community (no direct evidence found).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Subscriptions $16-$499/month plus credit top-ups; team/enterprise plans exist.
  • Our data: United States 30%, followed by UK/Italy/Germany/France; direct 44.8%, search 40.2%.
  • "Users have generated tens of thousands of videos since Seedance 2 launched" (2026-02).
  • Team of 2-10 people; revenue figures Not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The launch timing comes from RDAP and raw Wayback snapshots, credible; the changelog comes from the official site's own text.
  • Founder information comes from a third-party review relaying the imprint (the official imprint is front-end rendered and could not be scraped).
  • X and Instagram could not be opened, so only summaries were used. The "Mitte" on Product Hunt is a 2017 mineral water dispenser, not this company.
  • Gaps: where the first users in early 2025 came from; paying-user count and revenue; whether the creator partnerships were paid.
