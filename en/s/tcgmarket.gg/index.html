Basics

  • Product: digital pack opening (87 pack pages), Upgrader, Zard mode, leaderboard, "provably fair" verification, marketplace listings (commission), physical card shipping worldwide ("Collect digitally, own physically. Ship globally."). Deposits via Stripe credit card and Passimpay crypto.
  • Who built it: no company entity or founder found; the site only lists support@tcgmarket.gg, X @tcgmarketgg, and Discord. Stack React + Convex. The UI includes Traditional Chinese (zh-TW) and partner streamers are mostly Taiwanese, so the team likely has Taiwan/Chinese-speaking ties (speculation).
  • Pricing: deposit-and-spend model; pack prices not obtained (a Taiwanese streamer's video mentions "one pull costs 30,000" TWD).
  • Domain:.gg WHOIS does not disclose registration date; first Wayback snapshot 2026-08-12, /upgrader captured 2026-09-22. X account registered 2026-03-11; Discord created 2026-06-25.
  • Traffic (profile data): United States 100% (small sample); direct 45.2%, search 41.1%, social 8.2% (YouTube, Facebook); brand keywords 86.3%, non-brand search 0.

Where the first users came from: streamer-specific referral codes (Evidence). The front-end code records referralCode, Meta ad attribution (metaAttribution), and UTM at signup, indicating two legs: streamer ref codes + Meta ads; the earliest visible streamer partnership is Taiwan's yan lifestyle on 2026-08-27.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Streamer sponsorships: each streamer gets a ref code (YAN / PAPERTIGER / TONG) paired with a "sign up to win $100" giveaway where viewers register and comment their account number.
  • Pack-opening highlight spread: streamer video titles lead with "300K grand prize card in one pull", "Nothing But GRAILS".
  • Attribution infrastructure: recordReferralLanding, metaAttribution, utmAttribution in the code.

Speculation:

  • Also running ads on Meta platforms (the code has Meta attribution, but no specific ads were seen).
  • Leaderboards / jackpot announcements get users posting win screenshots, creating secondary spread.

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public figures. Largest single partner video 208K views; owned social accounts are all small.
  • Revenue sources: pack margin (expected value below sale price), the upgrader, marketplace commissions.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Apart from official accounts and streamer videos, search results were almost entirely irrelevant card price guides; evidence comes mainly from YouTube video descriptions, front-end JS code, Wayback CDX, and X/Discord APIs.
  • Operating company, location, exact launch date, and revenue all not found; the mechanics are gambling-like and compliance status is unverified.
  • The profile data's "United States 100%" contradicts the mostly Taiwanese streamer lineup; the traffic sample may be incomplete.
