In 2023 it narrowed to barbershops and built booking software: each shop gets a blismo.com/s/<shop name> booking page, and customers searching a shop name land on Blismo's page (the bulk of search traffic is now shop-name terms like "some barbershop", with referrals from the shops' own websites). In 2025 it changed its pitch to "AI front desk answering calls + AI win-back of old customers + AI marketing/social media", priced at $29.99-119.99/month, and expanded to salons, nails, spas and pet grooming; at the same time it positioned itself as a payments ISO, partnering with Dejavoo, SUNMI terminals, linked2pay, Gateway First Bank, and field-sales channels such as YBS POS and ITPS. That lets it spread the software into shops through acquiring and POS channels and then earn payment revenue share. Basics

  • Product: an AI booking and shop management platform for appointment-based personal-care businesses. Features include online booking, an AI front desk (answers calls and books), AI gap filling (automatically contacts dormant customers the next day), AI marketing/SEO writer, AI social media manager, review boosting, POS, website building, customer management and a walk-in self check-in kiosk; supports English and Spanish.
  • Pricing: free trial; Starter $29.99, Standard $69.99, Pro $119.99 (per month).
  • Company: the 2020 footer reads "BARGOPAY LLC, team@blismo.com"; phone area code 425 (around Seattle, Washington). The site calls itself an ISO (independent sales organization). Founders were not found; the 2020 blog authors are credited as John Edwards, Jef Gibs and Mary Goldberg, which look like pseudonyms.
  • Domain: registered 2018-08-12 (RDAP); Wayback records from 2011-2019 are all 301 redirects; first valid page in 2020-08.
  • Users: US 100%; search 47% (shop-name terms), referral 37% (shop websites, e.g. hermansbarber.com, freshzonebarbershop.com), brand keywords only 9.5% (channel profile).

The first users: a batch of independent US barbershops in 2023-2024 (Alex VIP Barber Shop, Beatnik Barbershop, Big Six Barber Shop, etc.). There is no direct evidence of how they were won. Judging by the 425 area code and the traits of a small team, it was probably field sales or personal referrals. Staff IDs reached about 1,700 by 2024-09, suggesting a few hundred shops at the time (speculation).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Branded shop booking pages: each shop's blismo.com/s/<shop name> page gets found by customers searching the shop name, and shop websites link to it. This brings Blismo a lot of customer traffic (referral 37%, plus shop-name searches). Whether customers who see "Powered by Blismo" turn into new merchants has no evidence.
  • Payments and POS channels: partnering with acquirers, terminal makers and ISOs, "merchant distribution paired with lifecycle software".
  • SEO landing pages: industry-specific booking software pages, comparison pages and a blog.

Speculation:

  • Real merchant acquisition is payments-channel field sales plus Google searches for "barbershop booking software"; the merchant-side channel cannot be seen in the traffic data.
  • Earning payment revenue share lets the software be priced lower than Booksy/Square.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Price $29.99-119.99/month; roughly a few hundred to one or two thousand shops (inferred from staff IDs). No funding information.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch (no earlier report). Search results were entirely irrelevant; all evidence comes from RDAP, Wayback (snapshots and URL lists), the sitemap and the partners page.
  • Gaps: the founders, why and exactly when it moved from a rewards app to barbershop booking, how the first shops were won, and the shop count were not found.
