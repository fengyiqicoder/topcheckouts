Growth loop

Evidence:

  • A celebrity moving house: it opened with "thousands of readers", coming from his existing academic and media profile and his X account; of the traffic, social 29% is all from X, and referral 53% (Substack in-app recommendations and email links).
  • News events bring new users: the fastest subscriber growth, from 2024-06 to 2025-01 (32K to 65K), lines up exactly with the viral pieces during the August 2024 riots and the period he went full time; the most-engaged articles are all hot-topic controversy pieces.
  • TV and election exposure: presenting on GB News and standing for Reform kept him on screen; his Facebook page (589K followers) and Instagram posts all point to mattgoodwin.org.
  • Paid conversion: a weekly live Q&A and a paywall that keeps getting tighter. Substack's public data shows bestseller_tier=1000, meaning at least 1,000 paid subscribers.

Speculation:

  • Substack's recommendation network (other right-wing/anti-establishment writers recommending each other) is probably a big part of the "referral 53%", but no specific list of recommenders was obtained.
  • Controversies (AI fake quotes, the by-election) also brought exposure, and no evidence of a subscriber decline was seen.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Subscribers: about 13K (2023-01) → 32K (2024-06) → 65K (2025-01) → 91K (2026-01) → 87K+ (2026-10, Substack profile page), all from Wayback page metadata.
  • Paid: Substack's bestseller badge tier is 1000+ paid subscribers. The pricing page was not captured; at typical Substack prices (about £50–60 per year), annual revenue is at least tens of thousands of pounds (Speculation).
  • Instagram claims "Nearly 100,000 daily readers".

Confidence and gaps

  • Takeoff method is set to "External event tailwind": the fastest-growing half year coincides with the August 2024 riots and his move to full time after leaving the university; but there was already an existing audience migration at opening, so both hold.
  • Paid subscriber count, price and revenue have no official disclosure; 1000+ is only the lower bound of the tier Substack publishes.
  • 87K in 2026-10 is lower than 91K in 2026-01, which may be a different counting basis or a small loss of subscribers after the by-election and book controversy, unverified.
  • The Guardian profile and Wayback pages could not be opened with WebFetch; the Wayback figures were obtained by reading page metadata with curl.
  • This is a new site, written from scratch this time; most search results were unrelated generic articles, and the main evidence came from Substack's public API, Wayback snapshots and Wikipedia.
