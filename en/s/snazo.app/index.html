An AI clipping tool registered only three months ago. The product itself is no different from Opus Clip-style tools; everything is in the positioning. Instead of "creators clip their own videos", it speaks straight to the "clipper" (someone who takes clipping payout tasks on platforms like Whop and mass-reposts other people's long videos across farmed accounts to earn view payouts) with "Turn videos into clips that pay". The selling point is "every clip rewrites the visuals/captions/pacing, so it won't be flagged as duplicate or throttled", backed by an account warm-up tutorial. It feeds on traffic from the 2025-2026 Whop clipping campaign side-hustle wave, and new users mostly hear about it in short videos and communities in the clipping side-hustle scene and then search the name (speculation, with brand keywords at 88.7% of search).One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: paste a YouTube/podcast/livestream link or upload a video, and it automatically cuts about 10 vertical 9:16 clips with animated captions; credits are counted per minute.
  • Pricing: Basic $19.99/month ($9.99/month billed annually, 150 credits), Pro $29.99 ($14.99 annually, 300 credits), Ultra $59.99 ($29.99 annually, 800 credits, "Run clip farms at scale"); no free tier, no refunds, payments via Stripe. https://snazo.app/paywall
  • Who built it: the official site and terms say only "Snazo", with contact emails hello@ / support@snazo.app; no company entity, address or founder is given. Registrar Namecheap, registration details hidden.
  • Launch date:.app RDAP registered 2026-07-03; terms of service "Last updated July 2, 2026"; first Wayback snapshot 2026-10-01. Presumably launched three months ago.
  • Site: Next.js, the sitemap has only 7 pages (homepage, try-snazo landing page, account warm-up tutorial, pricing, support, terms, privacy), with no SEO content matrix.
  • Traffic (profile data): US 53%, India 22%, South Africa 4.2%; search 55% (brand keywords 88.7%), direct 33.8%, referral 6.8%.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • The positioning hook "clips that pay" + the "$600 first month" testimonial + the account warm-up tutorial, aimed at side-hustle earners (official site).
  • Tutorial videos about "becoming a clipper with snazo" appear on short-video platforms (YouTube Short).
  • 88.7% of search traffic is brand keywords, meaning new users learn the name off-site first (profile data).

Speculation:

  • The upstream traffic is the Whop clipping payout wave: the more people become clippers, the more people search for tools; Snazo enters through the pain point of avoiding duplicate flags.
  • Promotion relies mainly on videos from small KOLs in the clipping side-hustle scene and word of mouth in Discord/Whop communities; the clips users post carry no branding (no watermark), so output is not branded.
  • "Ultra = clip farms" suggests a group of multi-account bulk users contributes most of the revenue.

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public revenue data. The lowest annual price is $119.99, there is no free tier, so every user is a paying user. At under 4 months since launch, it had enough traffic to make our list, so monthly revenue is presumed to be somewhere between a few thousand and a few tens of thousands of dollars (speculation, unverified).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. Search results were mostly irrelevant (individuals named Snazo on TikTok, assorted TikTok-alternative articles); the original text of the wisdomganga review page could not be fetched, so only the summary was used.
  • Founder, team, launch channel and real user count were not found; "piggybacking on the Whop clipping wave" is inferred from the official site copy, and no Snazo page on Whop was found.
