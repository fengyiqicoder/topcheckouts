Los Angeles company dot. (founded 2020) turned the NFC business card into a $20-30 one-time consumer product: cards sold through the Shopify store dotcards.net, with free lifetime personal profiles hosted on dot.cards. Facebook/Instagram ads plus advertorial landing pages like "5 reasons why people are ditching paper business cards" made the card a hit (a Reddit user's words: "seeing ad after ad after ad"), then it went on Amazon. Every time a card is tapped, it opens a profile on the dot.cards domain on the other person's phone, so the product itself keeps generating exposure; later it moved upmarket to the dot.Teams B2B subscription. Basics

  • Product: NFC/QR digital business card hardware (dot.card / classic / thin / metal / band wristband / display stand) with a free dot.Profile page; paid tiers dot.Profile+ (Pro about $6.99/mo, per competitor V1CE's blog) and dot.Teams, a team card and lead capture SaaS.
  • Who built it: LinkedIn company page describes a privately held Los Angeles tech company founded in 2020 with 34 employees (page also lists 51-200); founders not found.
  • Pricing: single card $20-30 (standard card $20 in 2022, with logo $55), custom card $50; free shipping over $50; bulk saves up to 25%.
  • Domain: dot.cards registered 2020-06-15 (RDAP, Squarespace); Shopify store dotcards.net has 200 snapshots since 2021-03 (earlier 2002-2004 snapshots belong to a previous owner); earliest Shopify product "dot.card - custom" created 2021-09-02.
  • Traffic (profile data): United States 43.7%, Thailand 33.5%, Vietnam 16%; search 50.9%, direct 38.2%, social 11% (Instagram, X, Facebook); referral thumbtack.com 3.9%. Thai TV drama search terms are mixed in; Thai/Vietnamese traffic may be same-name noise.

Where the first users came from: no launch post or press coverage found. What has evidence is paid social ads: a Reddit user said they bought after seeing ad after ad; the site still hosts typical advertorial landing pages like "card-5-reasons" and "card-5-reasons-g" ("Did you know 88% of paper business cards end up in the trash after three days?"). The Amazon flagship store (Sold by dot. cards, Fulfilled by Amazon) is the second sales channel.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Paid ads: Facebook/Instagram ads + advertorial landing pages (multiple A/B versions with the -g suffix).
  • Branded output: every tap opens a dot.cards/username page, which is the other person's first encounter with the brand (search results show many public user pages, e.g. dot.cards/your_personal_taxi_2026).
  • User-generated content: students on TikTok posting "how to use the dot card and band" tutorials.
  • Extension into B2B: dot.Teams trades free custom devices for team subscriptions.

Speculation:

  • Amazon reviews and search ranking contributed a large share of organic orders in 2022-2023.
  • Dropping the price from $50 to $20-30 made it an impulse-buy and gift item, improving ad ROAS.

Revenue and scale clues

  • LinkedIn: 2.5M+ dot devices and 100M+ in-person connections to date (self-reported). At $20-30 per unit, rough cumulative hardware revenue in the tens of millions of dollars (speculation; the device count may include bulk team cards).
  • Negative word of mouth: PissedConsumer 31-81 reviews at 1.7 stars (shipping/support complaints).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Search results contained a fair number of relevant links, but Reddit could not be opened and the About page has no body text; main evidence from the LinkedIn company page, Shopify products.json listing dates, Wayback, RDAP, and the original ad landing page copy.
  • Founders, funding, and the specific ad/video behind the first breakout were not found.
