Basics

  • Product: an MLB player prop research platform. Venom Rankings (ranks players daily by "how bettable they are", with DUE and PITCHER VULNERABLE tags), Venom Cheat Code (recalculates stats weighted by the pitcher's pitch mix), Player Vault (a card of 9 player-specific metrics for each player), Viper Alerts, and a community. It plans to expand to 3 sports in 2026 (the X bio already lists MLB and NFL).
  • Price: currently $0, no credit card needed; Venom Premium is "coming soon to the App Store", founding members first, not yet priced. The sign-up entry is on Whop (whop.com/biz_xQkEIdFlken7i0).
  • Company: footer "© 2026 Shadowline Holdings LLC"; carries a 1-800-GAMBLER responsible gambling notice.
  • People: Jake Venom (@jakevenomapp), bio "Venom co-founder, starting a betting revolution", 206K followers on X, 2,275 tweets, account registered 2013-02; YouTube @JakeVenomSports has 4,050 subscribers, 44 videos, 145K views, opened 2026-08-02. The brand account @VenomAnalytics has 28K followers (account registered in 2010, possibly an old account taken over and renamed, speculation).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Existing followers migrate: the founder has 206K followers on X and the brand account 28K, both bios link venomanalytics.io; in our data 100% of social traffic comes from X.
  • Content-driven: weekly videos, and one person replied "I often see people sharing and quote-tweeting your videos" (search summary only); 44 YouTube videos in two months.
  • Free + urgency: "the free entry closes when the paid version launches, get in now", "founding members get the best price".
  • Retention through daily updates: rankings refresh at 6am every day, and returning users come back daily to see that day's picks (direct traffic 67.9%).

Speculation:

  • Almost all of the first 1,000 users came from Jake Venom's X followers, with people in the betting community screenshotting rankings and quote-tweeting him, creating secondary spread.
  • Registering the domain and launching just before the MLB season followed the season's rhythm; expanding to a second sport with the NFL season (from last month) catches the traffic drop after the MLB season.
  • The business model may be paid subscriptions plus sportsbook affiliate commissions (a common approach for sports betting tools), with no evidence yet.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 14,000+ users (self-reported), no revenue at present.
  • If the paid version were priced at a typical sports-betting-tool $20-$50/month with 5% conversion, monthly revenue would be about $14K-$35K (pure speculation).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch: there was no old report in the notes. Of the search results only the X profile and one YouTube video were useful; the rest were unrelated (rocket launches, Omegle alternatives, etc.); the work relied mainly on the official site, RDAP, public X account data (fxtwitter API) and the YouTube channel page.
  • The tweet bodies on X could not be opened (HTTP 402), so tweet reach and the launch post were not obtained.
  • Gaps: the exact launch date, the launch tweet, Whop sign-up count, partner's identity, paid-version pricing.
