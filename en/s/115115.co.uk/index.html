Basics

  • Product: online takeaway ordering platform: search shops by postcode, view menus, place orders, with delivery fee settings; the shops are mostly Cantonese, Chinese-Thai and fish-and-chip takeaways.
  • Who built it: the About PDF says "owned and operated by 115115 Technology Ltd trading as 115115.co.uk". A page source comment says "Powered by www.Food-Ordering.co.uk", so the system is a white-label deployment of Food-Ordering.com (a white-label ordering platform aimed at restaurants, POS vendors and resellers). Founder name not found (the Companies House page would not open).
  • Pricing (for merchants): the About page says "No commission per order, No setup fee… Simple pricing which is £10.00 per week", with money going straight into the shop's bank account; also sells add-on services such as marketing and branded apps.
  • Launch date: registered 2017-06-03 per Nominet RDAP; Wayback's earliest captures are the /order/ ordering page from 2017-07-17 and /restaurants/Menu.asp?id_r=2 and id_r=16 from 2017-09 (shops were already live in summer-autumn 2017). About PDF metadata: created 2016-02-22, modified 2017-10-19 (the template may have been in use earlier, speculation).
  • Current scale: /restaurants/index.asp lists 15 shops (Glossop, Leeds, Marple, Stockport, Manchester, Rochdale, Oldham, Salford, Lytham St Annes and others), all CLOSED when crawled (possibly just outside opening hours).
  • Traffic (profile data): UK 100%; organic search 49.8% (about half non-brand, searching specific restaurant names and menus); direct 37.7% (repeat orders); referral 7.5%.

Where the first users came from: no direct evidence found. Presumably the operator signed up Chinese takeaways around Manchester one by one (personal contacts/door-to-door), and the shops then steered their own customers to order via 115115 (in-store flyers, phone orders moved online); on the customer side, people arrive by searching the shop name (speculation).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Merchant side: zero commission + a flat £10/week, pitched as a replacement for Just Eat/Hungryhouse (About PDF).
  • Customer side: half of organic search is specific shop name/menu long-tail terms, and direct visits of 37.7% are repeat customers (profile data).

Speculation:

  • Shops move their regulars to order through 115115 to save commission, and the platform has almost no ability to attract new customers itself; growth depends on how many shops sign up, and the shop count has stayed at a dozen or so for years, so no loop has formed.
  • The memorable digit string "115115" in the URL may be meant for printing on takeaway flyers (speculation).

Revenue and scale clues

  • 15 shops x £10/week is about £7,800/year in subscription revenue (rough calculation from the current list, speculation); revenue from add-on services is unknown.
  • The number of historical pages on Wayback dropped markedly after 2017, suggesting the business has stayed at its initial scale.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. Search results were almost all irrelevant (rocket launches, anti-scam sites and so on), so only the on-site About PDF, shop list, page source, RDAP and Wayback CDX were used.
  • Companies House registration details for 115115 Technology Ltd, directors, how shops were signed up, and order volume were not found; the Companies House page would not open.
  • The "takeoff" framing does not hold: there was no takeoff event, so it is classed as long-tail search capture, a small business rather than a growth-type product.
