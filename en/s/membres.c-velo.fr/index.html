This is not a startup product but the member back office of C.vélo, the public bike service of SMTC, the public transport authority of the Clermont-Ferrand metropolitan area. SMTC launched it on 27 June 2013 (operated then by Velogik, with bus operator T2C collaborating). In August 2021 it switched to Citybike France as operator, with new PBSC bikes and an app, and in 2022-2023 annual-pass renewal and document upload were centralized on membres.c-velo.fr. Users come entirely from the municipal service itself (local residents, transit card holders, redirects from the main site c-velo.fr), and the metropolitan area pays to make the annual pass €0, so there is no growth loop in the market sense.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: C.vélo member system: sign up, log in, renew the annual pass, upload ID and proof of address, view the station map, terms of sale (CGV); the main site c-velo.fr handles introductions and news. The member system is provided by shooga.ca (profile data).
  • Who built it: provided by SMTC-AC (the joint public transport authority of the Clermont-Ferrand metropolitan area) and operated by Citybike France (since 2021); in 2013 the homepage footer listed T2C, VELOGIK and SMTC.
  • Services: self-service public bikes (annual pass €0, with Clermont Auvergne Métropole covering a €25 value; 7 days €7; 1 day €2), long-term rental bikes (from €10/day), electric cargo bikes, e-bike trials, C.BOXvélo secure parking boxes; bikes can be unlocked with the app, a transit card or a temporary code. Deposit/fine for a lost bike €200 (profile data).
  • Domain: c-velo.fr registered 2013-04-30 (RDAP); main site's first Wayback snapshot 2013-06-29; the membres subdomain's first snapshot 2023-05-29.
  • Traffic (profile data): France 100%; referral 45.3% (mainly from the main site), direct 41%, search 8.2% (only the brand keywords "cvelo clermont" and "c.velo").

Where the first users came from: direct municipal placement. At the 2013 opening SMTC promoted it, stations were set up on city streets, and the target was local residents; later it was integrated with the transit card and the annual pass was subsidized to €0 by the metropolitan area. All users of the member site are existing C.vélo users renewing or new sign-ups (45% of referrals come from the main site).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Public subsidy: the self-service annual pass costs users €0 (the metropolitan area covers €25).
  • Bundled with the transit system: transit cards can unlock bikes directly, and the folding e-bike pilot is open only to T2C transit card holders.
  • Physical stations and events (bike festival, car-free day, "back on the bike" events) provide local exposure.

Speculation:

  • Free annual passes and the post-pandemic cycling boom are the main reasons for recent membership growth (speculation, member count not found).

Revenue and scale clues

  • A public service funded mainly by subsidies; revenue comes only from short-term passes and long-term rental fees. Station count, fleet size and member count are not listed on the official site.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. Search results were almost all irrelevant speed-test sites; evidence comes from archived news on the main site, RDAP and Wayback.
  • The site is essentially the member back office of a municipal service, so the "how it took off" framing fits poorly; the "growth engine" classification is filled as Not found.
  • Gaps: member count, station/fleet count, when the annual pass became €0, and the tender details for the switch from Velogik to Citybike France were not found.
