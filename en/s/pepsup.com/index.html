A "website builder + management" SaaS for sports clubs built in 2014 by two Marseille triathlon enthusiasts who are also programmers (CEO Sylvain Combe, CTO Sylvain Colineau), later expanded into all-in-one software for every kind of French association (members, online sign-up and payments, events, bookkeeping, donation receipts, website). No explosive takeoff: it grew through a 30-day free trial, a large volume of French SEO content aimed at volunteer association managers ("practical guides" published continuously since 2016), and the fact that every club's website and payment page lives on an xxx.pepsup.com subdomain (members see the Pep's Up brand when paying, and managers of other clubs get pulled in too). Over ten years it reached 15,000 associations and €43 million in cumulative card payments. On top of that, institutional partnerships (39 volunteer sites for Malakoff Humanis, a discount for the FFVélo cycling federation's PACA regional committee) provide bulk distribution.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: all-in-one management software for French associations: member records, custom sign-up forms, Stripe/PayPal online payments, event registration, attendance, bookkeeping, donations and tax receipts, email/SMS, association website, online shop. Optional add-ons such as CAARL legal assistance and SportyPay installment payments.
  • Who built it: Pep's Up, Marseille (Marseille Innovation campus, 45 rue Joliot Curie); co-founders Sylvain Combe (CEO) and Sylvain Colineau (CTO), "two IT experts who love triathlon." The team is split into onboarding, support, communications and tech groups; headcount not disclosed. Funding not found (presumably bootstrapped). https://pepsup.com/qui-sommes-nous/
  • Launch date: domain registered 2014-06-18 (RDAP); earliest Wayback snapshot 2014-10-09, when the pitch was "build a website for your sports club in 1 minute"; the site says "Depuis 2015" (year the company was founded).
  • Pricing: Basic €10/month billed yearly (€120/year), or €11/month billed monthly; higher tier €25/month billed yearly; also a one-time "onboarding setup package"; 30-day free trial, no card required.
  • Channel profile: France 98.7%; direct 75.9%, search 13.2%, referral 6.2%; search terms are mostly club names, referrals come from club websites (camenthon.fr, gymv-leraincy.com).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Association websites/payment pages live on pepsup.com subdomains (e.g. cercle-des-nageurs-de-brest.pepsup.com, la-degaine-escalade.pepsup.com), so every member sign-up and payment passes through a Pep's Up page; referral traffic comes from club websites and search terms are club names.
  • One manager often runs several associations (testimonial: "I use it for both my clubs") and brings the tool to new ones.
  • Content SEO (practical guides, landing pages per association type) + 30-day free trial.
  • Institutional/federation partnerships bring in associations in bulk (Malakoff Humanis, FFVélo PACA).

Speculation:

  • Some members are volunteers at other associations; after encountering the product while paying, they bring it back to their own.
  • Checkout visits: 4,712 two months ago, 12,894 last month French associations concentrate renewals and sign-ups in the last month back-to-school season (but these figures are identical across several sites in the same batch, so they look like a floor estimate or placeholder).

Revenue and scale clues

  • 15,000+ associations (2026), 10,000+ (2023); €43 million+ in cumulative card payments.
  • At €120–300/year, annual subscription revenue would be roughly €2–4 million (speculation; unclear how many associations are paying).
  • No funding coverage found.

Confidence and gaps

  • First report on a new site (note: search results are drowned out by same-name noise such as Pepsi and a peptide vendor called Pepsup, so there is no usable third-party coverage).
  • Launch date, founders and scale milestones come from the official site and Wayback, and are reliable.
  • Gaps: source of the first associations, team size, funding and revenue all not found. The "Long-tail search" takeoff label rests on continuous SEO content since 2016 plus long slow growth, with no channel-split data to back it up.
