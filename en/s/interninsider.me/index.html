Basics

  • Product: internship aggregator for North American college students (claims to scan 200K+ company career pages every 15 minutes, 1,000+ new listings a day), with personalized recommendations, ATS resume builder, cover letters, recruiter emails (unlocked with credits), and a free weekly newsletter.
  • Company: Intern Insider Technologies Inc.; describes itself as built by "a group of students who've been through the internship hunt"; Facebook page lists Vancouver BC. Founder names not published on the site.
  • Pricing: free sign-up + paid subscription (exact price not published, cancel anytime) + credits. Also sells newsletter sponsorship slots (booked via Calendly).
  • Domain: interninsider.me registered 2023-10-09 (WHOIS, GoDaddy).
  • Traffic: United States 56%, Canada 35%; direct 42%, search 44% (brand keywords 77%), paid search 8.8%; social from LinkedIn and Instagram.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Free newsletter → referral reward: refer friends to unlock real resumes from big-company interns (LinkedIn post).
  • Content distribution: LinkedIn company page 29K, Instagram 49K, TikTok, Reddit; the job alerts themselves are the social content.
  • School channel: partnerships with 7 university career centers (For Higher Ed), on-campus offline events.
  • Subscribers → paid product: upgraded from newsletter to platform in 2025, free sign-up + paid membership/credits.
  • Paid search is 8.8% (traffic profile), meaning it also buys brand/job keywords.

Speculation:

  • Newsletter article pages for company name + "internship" long-tail terms (e.g. "KPMG internships") are picking up SEO (non-brand search is long-tail for specific companies' internships).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Users: 50K (2024-09) → 80K (2025-05) → 100K (2025-12) → 170K (2026-10), all self-reported.
  • Revenue sources: subscription membership + credits + newsletter sponsorship; prices and revenue not published.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site written from scratch. Timeline mainly from Wayback and the site's own claims, fairly reliable; user counts are all self-reported.
  • Gaps: founder names, funding, paid prices, and the specific source of the first subscribers (classmates vs referrals vs social media) not found; no founder interviews found.
