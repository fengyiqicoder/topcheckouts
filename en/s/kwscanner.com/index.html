KWScanner is a pay-as-you-go keyword tool registered in May 2026 (the old domain had blank captures in 2018 and 2021; this is a re-registration). It resells search volume/CPC/competition data from sources such as Google Keyword Planner: $5 buys 10,000 lookups, credits never expire, and the pitch is 'far cheaper than an Ahrefs/Semrush subscription', plus a Chrome extension that shows the data on Google results pages. 95% of its traffic comes from Pakistan, 63% is direct, 91% of search is brand keywords, the only social link on the site is a WhatsApp channel ('join for updates and free coupons'), and its Facebook page claimed 8.62 million keywords processed and 5,500+ active users by October. All of this points to a tool that spread by word of mouth through WhatsApp/Facebook groups and YouTube tutorials in Pakistan's freelancer/SEO circles, not via global SEO or Product Hunt.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: bulk keyword lookup (up to 3,000 per batch) returning search volume, average CPC, competition and 12-month trend; keyword lists, CSV export, push to Google Ads ad groups; Chrome extension (v1.3.5, about 8,000 users, 4.7 stars / 21 ratings) shows metrics beneath Google results and supports right-click lookup.
  • Pricing: new accounts get free credits, no card required; 1 credit = 1 keyword; $0.0005 per credit (10k–99k) down to $0.0002 (1M+); $5 minimum; credits auto-refunded when no data is returned; refunds within 30 days.
  • Data source: the site says it comes from 'authoritative search data sources including Google Keyword Planner', not clickstream-panel estimates.
  • Team: the About page only says 'a small, focused team that answers support emails ourselves'; the terms only say 'governed by the laws of KWScanner's place of registration', with no entity or country disclosed.
  • Domain: WHOIS created 2026-05-03 (Cloudflare registrar); Wayback has captures from 2018-08 and 2021-12 but they are empty (presumably a previous owner).
  • Social: WhatsApp channel 'KWScanner - Keyword Research Tool' with 385 followers; Facebook page (summary only); social referrals come from YouTube.
  • Traffic (profile): direct 62.6%, search 28.1% (brand keywords 90.6%: kwscanner, kw scanner extension), referral 5.6%; Pakistan 95.4%, United Kingdom 4.6%.

Where the first users came from: direct evidence is thin. Visible signals: 95% of traffic is in Pakistan, mostly direct; the only official social link is a WhatsApp channel that recruits with 'free coupons'; the social source is YouTube. The inference is that it first spread in the founding team's own Pakistani SEO/freelancer circles (WhatsApp groups, Facebook groups, Urdu-language YouTube tutorials). Free credits for new accounts let people try it at zero cost.

Where new users come from: brand searches ('kwscanner', 'kw scanner extension') after in-group word of mouth; organic installs of the Chrome extension from the store; coupons pushed via the WhatsApp channel; from autumn 2026, listings on English-language directory sites (SourceForge, TinyLaunch) as an attempt to grow beyond Pakistan.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Free credits plus a $5 entry point suit budget-sensitive freelancers.
  • WhatsApp channel pushes coupons to re-engage and acquire users.
  • The Chrome extension puts KWScanner data in front of users on every Google search, driving repeat use.

Speculation:

  • Pakistani Fiverr/Upwork sellers producing keyword reports for overseas clients need cheap bulk lookups and recommend it to each other in groups — classic friends-and-circle spread.
  • Local SEO creators have how-to videos on YouTube (the social source is YouTube, but no specific video was found).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Self-reported 8.62 million lookups (October); even if all were paid, at $0.0002–0.0005 per lookup that is only about $1.7k–4.3k — revenue is very small (estimated).
  • 5,500+ active users (self-reported); extension has about 8,000 users.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Product, pricing and extension data are highly credible; 'started via word of mouth in Pakistani circles' is inferred from the traffic country split, the WhatsApp channel and the direct-traffic share, with no founder account, so credibility is medium-low.
  • Search results are mostly unrelated (police scanner Reddit threads, viral news); no interviews.
  • Gaps: founder/company identity and country, the specific promotion groups or YouTube videos, actual paid revenue.
