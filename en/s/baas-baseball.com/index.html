BaaS (バース, Baseball As A Service) is an anonymous 'by baseball fans, for baseball fans' NPB draft tool site in Japan. It was registered and launched in late September 2025, exactly one month before the October 23 NPB draft meeting. The core feature is '仮想ドラフト/模擬ドラフト' (mock draft): one person plays against AI as the 12 teams, or competes asynchronously with other users. Every draft season large numbers of Japanese fans search for '模擬ドラフト', '仮想ドラフト', 'ドラフトシミュレーション' and 'AIドラフト', yet there were almost no good dedicated tools, so BaaS went after those terms by putting '2027〜2028年対応 プロ野球仮想ドラフト・模擬ドラフト' straight in its title: search is 81% of traffic, brand keywords 0%, and the remaining 19% comes from X. Supported by note articles, YouTube recordings of 'BaaS user mock-draft tournaments' and X/TikTok accounts, it turns seasonal draft-season search traffic into registered users, then monetizes with ¥100–1,500 AI-draft tickets and a 'new team owner pack'.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a tool for NPB draft enthusiasts — AI draft (you run one team, AI runs the other 11, scored on voting data and team needs), full-nomination mode (you run all 12 teams, lottery in round one, waiver order afterwards), multiplayer AI draft (2–11 players + AI fill-ins), '仮想ドラフトオーナー' (run a 13th expansion team as GM over multiple years), cross-era/themed fantasy drafts, nationwide asynchronous '仮想ドラフト大会' tournaments; a crowd-built database of high school/college/corporate/independent-league draft prospects, personal scouting lists, fan voting predictions, 12-team roster-needs analysis, game-watching diaries, members-only message board.
  • Pricing: base is free (registration required); AI-draft tickets ¥100 per run, ¥300 unlimited for one day, ¥1,500 lifetime; owner reset ticket ¥500; owner pack ¥1,000 (includes strategy meeting, 13-team AI draft, 60 days ad-free). Also runs ads.
  • Team: unsigned; the note bio reads 'a draft-prospect management tool by baseball fans, for baseball fans'.
  • Domain: WHOIS created 2025-09-23 (XServer, Japanese registrar); earliest Wayback capture 2025-09-27.
  • Channel accounts: note (@baas_baseball, 86 followers, 9 articles, since 2025-11-12), X @BaaS_baseball, TikTok @baas_baseball, YouTube (mock-draft tournament recordings).
  • Traffic (profile): search 81.4% (all non-brand: ドラフトシミュレーション, 模擬ドラフト, 仮想ドラフト, aiドラフト), social 18.6% (X); Japan 100%.

Where the first users came from: Japanese baseball fans searching for '模擬ドラフト/仮想ドラフト' during the 2025 draft season (October) — the site launched exactly one month before the draft meeting with a title aimed squarely at those terms, backed by prediction posts from the X account inside draft discussions. The note articles only started in November and count as the second wave. The '193 fans' nominations' figure from 2026-03 suggests active voting users in the first draft season were in the low hundreds.

Where new users come from: non-brand search every draft season (September–October, 81%); draft prediction posts on X and tournament recordings on TikTok/YouTube (social 19%); exposure from note writing contests.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Search ranking: page title '【2027〜2028年対応】プロ野球仮想ドラフト・模擬ドラフト'; the 81% search share is entirely these non-brand terms.
  • User data turned into content: fan voting/nomination data → 'fans' most-favored rookies ranking' articles → new traffic.
  • Community play: asynchronous '仮想ドラフト大会' tournaments and multiplayer AI drafts encourage inviting friends; tournament results are made into YouTube videos.

Speculation:

  • Strong seasonality: traffic concentrates around the September–October draft meeting, with another bump during summer Koshien when prospect talk heats up.
  • Draft-result screenshots (there is a 'full-screen screenshot view') get posted to X by users, creating branded secondary spread.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Micro-payments (¥100–1,500) plus ads; no revenue data.
  • '193 fans took part in nominations' for the 2025 draft; 86 note followers — overall scale is still small.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Launch timing (WHOIS + Wayback) and features/pricing (note's own text) are highly credible; 'took off on draft-season search' comes from the traffic profile plus launch timing, so it is a medium-strength inference.
  • Search results are cluttered with unrelated content (US baseball viral news, banking BaaS); no operator interview.
  • Gaps: operator identity, registered user count, paid conversion rate, X/TikTok follower counts (pages would not load).
