Basics

  • Product: HardStop blocks porn across devices (iPhone, Android, iPad, Mac, Windows, unlimited home devices), covering adult content on Reddit, X and Google Images, claiming to withstand VPNs, incognito mode, switching browsers and changing DNS; any app/website can be added to the block list; removal requires a wait (72 hours to 1 year selectable); there is a dedicated setting for "turn on the Reddit feed."
  • Pricing: 7-day free trial; annual billing works out to $15/month ("4 months free"); or $25 per 4 weeks, cancel anytime; comes with a free ebook and audiobook.
  • Guarantee: Zero Access Guarantee, a full refund if a bypass is found and they can't fix it (the press release version is a refund plus $100).
  • Endorsements / partners: recovery coach Nate O'Neill (claims to have coached 371 people, 87% reaching 90 days), sex addiction specialist Alaina D. Kuisma (MS, NCC), sex and relationship therapist Nancy Spitzer (LMFT).
  • Team: no founder named; the press release was issued from Wilmington, Delaware, US (a common place of incorporation); contact emails info@ / support@tryhardstop.com. The site is built on Shopify.
  • Traffic (our data): search 44.6%, direct 40.6%, paid 8.9%; brand keywords 76.2% (hardstop, hardstop login, hardstop for windows), plus "how do i access the blocker on chadcoachme." United States 82.5%, Canada 8.3%, Thailand 5.2%.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Coach channel: the homepage shows endorsements from three coaches/therapists; early links carried a?deal=77ascend partner code; search terms include chadcoachme (another coaching platform) + "blocker," showing coaches recommend HardStop to students as a tool in their courses.
  • Deep tie to Nate O'Neill: the scholarship campaign sends HardStop users to his high-priced course, and in turn his students use HardStop.
  • Paid traffic 8.9% + press release distribution: a small amount of ad buying and PR.
  • The "refund guarantee" itself is a conversion selling point.

Speculation:

  • The first users most likely came from partner coaches' existing students (coaching courses require installing a blocker), and only then did brand searches appear ("hardstop login" suggests many are returning users managing their accounts).
  • Members grew by about 1,200 in 3 months (2,600 three months ago -> 3,800 this month); at $15-25 a month that is a small but steady subscription business.
  • Quit-porn communities on Reddit (such as NoFap-type ones) may be another source, but no posts were found.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 3,800+ members x about $15-25/month = roughly $57K-$95K monthly revenue (assuming all are paying, pure speculation).
  • Trustpilot has 56 reviews, 25% one-star (mostly complaints about charges, hard-to-cancel, not blocking YouTube, etc.).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch: there is no earlier report in notes. Search results were mostly unrelated testosterone supplements and TRT clinics; evidence came mainly from the official site, the community campaign page, the GetNews press release, Trustpilot, RDAP and Wayback CDX.
  • Launch timing (registered 2025-11, live in early 2026) and member count (self-reported) are of medium credibility.
  • Gaps: the founder's identity, who the "ascend" partner is, the coach commission rate, which platforms the paid ads run on.
