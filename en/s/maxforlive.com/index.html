Basics

  • Product: a user-upload library of Max for Live devices (.amxd), with categories such as instruments, audio effects, and MIDI. Authors have public profile pages, and there are 'newest / top rated / most downloaded' charts. Devices are mostly free, though devices under a Commercial license are also listed, and paid devices usually link out to external stores to buy. This is quite likely why it shows up in Stripe/Gumroad referral traffic (speculation).
  • Creator: Clint (Michael) Sand, a member of the US IDM / dark ambient band synnack who also does mastering and video work (0xf8 Studios). It is a personal project with no affiliation to Ableton / Cycling '74.
  • Scale: about 'a few hundred devices, 10K+ users' in 2010-12 (Ableton artist page); 'nearly 4,000 tools' in 2019-08 (Ableton blog). The 2026-10 homepage adds about 5-10 devices a day, and the most downloaded device, STING, has about 134K downloads in total.
  • Funding: none (personal site).
  • The main country is Germany, which fits with Ableton's headquarters being in Berlin and the dense concentration of German electronic music users.

Growth loop

Evidence

  • Authors upload devices -> get a public profile page, download counts, ratings, and chart exposure -> authors post maxforlive.com links on forums, Reddit, and YouTube to distribute their work (the site owner already planned author pages and a PayPal tip button in 2009).
  • Official endorsements create ongoing traffic: Cycling '74 support docs, the Ableton blog, and the artist page have all linked to it.
  • SEO: each device has its own page, and long-tail searches like 'free max for live xxx' land here (Organic Search is the second-largest channel).

Speculation

  • Authors of paid devices use this as a distribution channel for free / DEMO versions, and users click external links to pay on Gumroad / Stripe -> bringing in more authors who want to monetize to upload (a two-sided loop). This is also why it appears in checkout referral data.
  • In 2025-2026, the lower barrier to writing Max patches with AI assistance may have pushed up new device uploads (last month checkout visits of 33K > the 4–6 months ago average of 26K). No evidence found.

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public revenue; it may be sustained by ads / donations (not verified).
  • 2010: 10K+ users; 2019: about 4,000 devices; top devices have downloads in the hundreds of thousands.

Confidence and gaps

  • The launch date, the creator, and the early positioning are all backed by original Wayback text, so credibility is high.
  • Gaps: maxforlive.com is currently blocked by Cloudflare, so the latest about page, the total device count, and any paid / membership mechanism could not be seen; no recent interviews with the site owner were found; Reddit was blocked, so community spread posts could not be checked.
