A small independent literary press founded in London in 2014 by Jacques Testard, specializing in translated fiction and long-form essays. Every book uses a custom typeface and an instantly recognizable cover scheme (white on blue for fiction, blue on white for essays). In its founding year he spent only £3,500 at the Frankfurt Book Fair to buy the English rights to Svetlana Alexievich's Second-Hand Time; the next year Alexievich won the Nobel Prize in Literature, making the press famous overnight and bringing in "six figures". Tokarczuk (2018), Ernaux (2022) and Fosse (2023) then won Nobels in turn, and "the small press that keeps picking Nobel winners" became news in itself. Readers recognize the brand and the uniform covers: they search a book title and find the site for direct purchase, or sign up for the "12 books a year" subscription, and its Instagram has 82K followers.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Business: independent press website bookshop selling fiction, essays, poetry, classic reprints, author collections, tote bags, gift cards and audiobooks; has a podcast "The Fitzcarraldo Editions Archive" and an events calendar; runs the "Novel Prize" (with New Directions and Giramondo, every two years) and the "Essay Prize" (with Mahler & LeWitt Studios, annually).
  • Subscriptions: fiction + essays 12 books for £165, plus fiction-only 6/12 books, essays and poetry subscriptions; subscribers get first editions around the publication date, with free UK shipping. Limited "First Decade Collection" tenth-anniversary hardbacks (1,000 copies of each, £30–50).
  • Scale: Wikipedia says 22 titles a year, the site's subscription page says "over thirty a year"; headquartered in Deptford, London; distributor Grantham Book Services; carries Arts Council England and Creative Europe funding logos.
  • Design: by Ray O'Meara, with a custom serif typeface "Fitzcarraldo".
  • Founder: Jacques Testard. Previously a co-founder of the literary magazine The White Review (from memory, not verified).
  • Our data: UK 39.3%, US 20.9%, Germany 5.5%; search 54.7% (brand keywords 66.8%, about 22% non-brand are book titles such as Drive Your Plow Over the Bones of the Dead and Helen of Nowhere), direct 34%, referral 6.8% (author personal sites hanaloftus.co.uk 5.81%, catherinelacey.com 1.73%, and book review sites).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • External events: four authors won Nobels, and each announcement brought press coverage and a search spike, and in 2023 produced a second wave of "small press picks Nobel winners" sharing.
  • Branded output: the uniform blue-and-white covers and signature typeface are recognizable at a glance in bookshops and on social media, creating a "collect all the blue books" mindset; the books themselves are the advertising.
  • Subscriptions: £165 prepaid for 12 books locks in core readers and brings steady direct visits (34%).
  • Searching book titles: non-brand search is mostly book titles; author personal sites send traffic to the website.
  • Literary prizes and podcast: its own Novel Prize and Essay Prize attract submissions and attention from the literary world.

Speculation:

  • The first readers came from the founder's existing London literary magazine circle (The White Review) and independent bookshop channels, with no direct evidence.
  • The 82K Instagram followers mostly come from the cover aesthetic and users voluntarily sharing "bookshelf photos".

Revenue and scale clues

  • Six figures in income after Alexievich's Nobel (Wikipedia). 22-30+ new titles a year, priced £10.99-14.99 each; subscription £165 for 12 books. Overall revenue is not public (speculated to be at the scale of a small press, within a few million pounds a year).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The founding, Nobels, rights price and subscription prices have Wikipedia and on-site evidence; the first book titles and the founder's background are from memory.
  • The Reddit thread would not open so only the summary was used; Wayback pages could not be read due to rate limiting.
  • Gaps: founder interviews, sales and revenue, website traffic before vs. after the Nobels.
