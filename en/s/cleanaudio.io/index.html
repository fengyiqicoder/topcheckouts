Basics

  • Product: browser-based AI noise removal (wind, echo, hum, traffic, AC, keyboard, crowd chatter); video keeps the original picture and only swaps in the clean audio track; also a vocal-remover for voice separation. Files auto-delete after 24 hours, 60-minute limit per file.
  • Who built it: no team or company named on the site; terms are governed by California law, contact email support@cleanaudio.io. Founder not found.
  • Pricing (profile data): free preview; PRO $16.99/mo; one-time $4.99/1 hour, $9.99/5 hours, $16.99/10 hours.
  • Domain: cleanaudio.io registered 2026-03-03 (WHOIS, Porkbun); first Wayback snapshot 2026-05-19.
  • Traffic (profile data): United States 39.6%, India 22.3%, United Kingdom 4.8%; search 82.8%, direct 12.9%; keywords "remove background noise from audio", "background noise removal", "remove background noise from video", "noise remover".

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Keyword as landing page: /remove-background-noise-from-audio, /from-video, /remove-wind-noise-from-video, /remove-echo-from-audio, /vocal-remover, one high-intent term per page.
  • Long-tail content: about 60 blog posts covering "how to remove noise in [software]" (iMovie, Final Cut, GarageBand, OBS, Canva, Clipchamp, Filmora, Descript), "[scenario] noise" (fan, AC, cafe, keyboard, traffic), and competitor comparisons (Adobe Podcast, Krisp).
  • No-signup preview lowers the barrier: hear 30 seconds of the result before logging in to pay; landing pages show before/after samples.

Speculation:

  • India at 22% of traffic suggests the cheap hourly purchase appeals to price-sensitive regions.
  • No obvious viral element (output audio carries no watermark or branding).

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public figures. Hourly billing + monthly subscription; a typical single-site SEO tool of this kind does a few thousand dollars a month (speculation).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Search results were mostly competitors and generic "AI audio tools" lists; the Probedex page returned 403. Main evidence from the site's changelog (very complete dates), sitemap, WHOIS, Wayback.
  • Gaps: developer identity, backlink sources, number of paying users.
