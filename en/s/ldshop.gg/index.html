Basics

  • Product: game top-up and digital goods store. Direct top-ups for mobile/online games, game coins, Steam and other activation keys, gift cards, subscriptions, live-streaming app top-ups; open to third-party sellers; backed by a large volume of game guides, redeem codes and character tier-list articles; has Discord, X, TikTok and Facebook accounts.
  • Who built it: both the About footer and the BrawlValue article name the operator as LDVERSE TECHNOLOGY LIMITED (Hong Kong, registration no. 75522164, Lee Garden One, Causeway Bay). LDShop's own promo-code blog says it is "backed by the well-known Android emulator LDPlayer"; Traditional Chinese site name "雷電商城"; ldplayer.net is a 2.35% referral source (profile). LDPlayer is the overseas version of China's 雷电模拟器 (from memory, unverified).
  • Launch date: no RDAP data for the.gg domain; first Wayback capture 2024-01-03 (www returned 200), captured monthly since. The footer's "1997-2026" does not match reality; origin unknown.
  • Self-reported scale (About, as of 2026-06): 1.5M+ purchases, 70% of users repurchase.
  • Third-party: Trustpilot 759 reviews, mostly positive; X @LDShop_Official about 10k followers, 908 tweets (twstalker summary); Facebook 1,718 followers (2025-02 summary).
  • Traffic (profile): US 9%, Taiwan 7.3%, Russia 7.2%; organic search 75.4%, brand keywords only 37.6%, non-brand about 60% (game redeem codes, tier lists, banner terms); referrals from yandex.ru 3.3%, game8.co 3.19%, ldplayer.net 2.35%; direct 22.5%.

Where the first users came from: no launch record found. The strongest clue is the parent relationship: LDShop says it is backed by LDPlayer, and ldplayer.net remains one of the main referral sources today, so the first buyers presumably came from LDPlayer emulator users (people playing mobile games on an emulator naturally need top-ups) (speculation, based on self-description + referral data). Throughout 2024 Wayback only saw the homepage; content rollout began after 2024-10, so the ramp-up should be in 2025.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Game guide/redeem-code SEO: for every hot new game (Wuthering Waves, Genshin, Endfield, Aniimo, Bleach: Soul Resonance) it writes redeem-code, tier-list and banner articles; non-brand search about 60%; game8.co referrals at 3.19% show it is also placed on guide sites.
  • Bulk sponsorships of small and mid-size YouTube gaming channels (465 videos, estimated $98–220 each).
  • Revenue-share banners on gaming tool sites (BrawlTrack, shareuid tracking).
  • Upstream emulator funnel (ldplayer.net referrals).
  • Repurchase: 70% of users have bought more than once (self-reported).

Speculation:

  • Redeem-code content must be updated every game version, creating a continuous "new version → new article → new traffic" loop; the Russian share and yandex referrals suggest Russian-language search is also being worked.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 1.5M+ purchases (as of 2026-06, self-reported). At $10–20 per order, cumulative GMV would be roughly $15–30M (pure speculation).
  • YouTube sponsorship spend estimated at 465 videos × about $100–220, roughly $50–100k cumulative (speculation).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The "launch" and "review" search sets returned nothing; main evidence from the About page, the site's own blog summaries, SponsorRadar, BrawlValue, Wayback CDX and the profile.
  • The ownership relationship between LDShop and LDPlayer rests only on the self-description "backed by LDPlayer" + the Traditional Chinese "雷電" naming; unverified. Official launch date not found.
  • Monthly blog counts are Wayback first-capture counts, useful only for trend, not equal to actual publishing volume.
