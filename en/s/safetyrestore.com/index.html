A mail-in repair business that started in 2012 with a small PrestaShop store plus an eBay store. After a crash, a seat belt pretensioner locks up and the SRS airbag module stores crash data, and a dealership wants to replace the whole part for over a thousand dollars. Safety Restore lets owners mail the part in, fixes it for $49-$65 and ships it back within 24 hours. It grew through car owners searching long-tail question terms like "seat belt locked after accident", "airbag module reset" and fault code "B1049", through salvage-car rebuilders (Copart auction cars), and through word of mouth on car forums (IH8MUD, Acura enthusiast groups). Over a dozen-plus years it became a company of twenty-some people in Westfield, Massachusetts, and now charges a flat $99.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: mail-in repair service: repair of locked/deployed seat belt pretensioners, SRS airbag module reset (clearing crash data and hard fault codes), seat belt webbing replacement (including colored webbing), instrument cluster repair; lifetime warranty, 24-hour turnaround, all vehicle models.
  • Who built it: Safety Restore Inc., 40 Main St., Westfield, MA; the About page lists President/CEO Artem Martynyuk, and the team page lists 22 people. In 2012 the site's contact address was a mail-drop box (PMB) in Greenville, South Carolina, and since 2013 it has been in Westfield. The About page says "Trusted Nationwide Since 2013".
  • Pricing: in 2012 $49 (1996-2005 models) / $59 (2006-2013); in 2015 airbag reset $50, seat belt $65, dual-stage pretensioner $100, webbing $75; now a flat $99 per item (title $99.99).
  • Domain: registered 2012-06-22 per Verisign RDAP; first Wayback snapshot 2012-09-13.
  • Traffic (profile data): US 78.1%, Australia 3.9%, Canada 3.6%; organic search 48.8% (brand keywords 67.7%, non-brand about 20%, such as "can you rebuild an airbag", "nissan nv200 fehlercode b1049"), direct 38.4%, email 7.7%.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Long-tail search: non-brand terms are fault codes and "can you fix an airbag" questions, with the blog and FAQ kept up since 2015.
  • Car forum word of mouth: members of model-specific forums such as IH8MUD and Acura RSX recommend it.
  • Returning customers/repair shops: direct 38.4% + email 7.7%.
  • Social ad copy targets the salvage-rebuild crowd (Copart).

Speculation:

  • Small repair shops and salvage rebuilders are frequent repeat customers and form a stable B2B base.
  • The price anchor "dealer $1,400 vs us $99" is the main conversion hook.

Revenue and scale clues

  • A 22-person team; a single service page with 4,292 reviews; "serving thousands of customers in the US and Canada". At $99 per order and thousands of orders a month, annual revenue is roughly in the millions of dollars (speculation, unverified).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. Search results included the official site, forums and review sites, the rest irrelevant; evidence comes from yearly Wayback snapshots, RDAP, the About page and the IH8MUD thread. Reddit and Facebook could not be opened, so only summaries were used.
  • The founder and why they started, the early scale of the eBay store, and real revenue were not found. The About page says "Since 2013", which differs slightly from the domain being registered in 2012 and a web store existing in 2012; the 2012 store opening is taken as correct.
