Two freshmen who started in 2023 (Sarthak Dhawan at Duke and Rudy Arora at Northwestern, childhood friends from a Dallas suburb) built "record the lecture, get notes/flashcards/quizzes automatically" as TurboLearn. The first 100 users came from handing out donuts in the Duke freshman dining hall and posting flyers in bathroom stalls. The real takeoff came from Arora himself filming "how I use this app" TikToks in classrooms, several a day, most of which nobody watched until one blew up (self-reported about 100 million views). They then copied that format across 101 owned accounts and 2,300+ videos, and used the TurboShip program to pay outside creators to post from fresh accounts. Before running any ads they already had 300K users and $500K in revenue. In 2025 it renamed to Turbo AI and moved to turbo.ai, and in 2026-07 it passed 10 million users.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: AI study/note-taking tool that turns lecture recordings, PDFs, YouTube videos and reading material into notes, flashcards, quizzes, podcasts and chat tutoring; in 2026 it added Learn Mode (generate a structured course from a few words). Originally TurboLearn (turbolearn.ai), renamed Turbo AI in 2025.
  • Team: co-founders Sarthak Dhawan (CEO, Duke class of 2023, previously built the app UMax) and Rudy Arora (Northwestern, runs social media growth); the two are childhood friends from a Dallas suburb and both dropped out. The company is in Los Angeles; about 15 full-time in 2025-10, while 2026 reports say a team of about 10.
  • Funding: raised about $750K from investors who came to them, already profitable at the time; won the VentureCat 2024 student startup competition.
  • Launch: around early 2024. The official 2025-10-16 blog says "started building Turbo about a year and a half ago". The turbo.ai domain was registered in 2017 and bought later; a redirect appeared around 2025-10, matching the rename.
  • Note: do not confuse it with the 2023 GPT-4-generated $TURBO meme coin, or with iturboai.com in Shanghai.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • First 100 users: on-campus footwork at Duke (donuts, bathroom flyers), which the founders told in their own words twice on the official blog.
  • Takeoff: Arora acting as the "founder streamer", filming several a day in classrooms and betting that one would hit. The first 300K users and $500K in revenue all came before running ads.
  • The growth engine is a short-video matrix: copying the hit format across 101 owned accounts and 2,300+ videos; the TurboShip program pays (reportedly $20–2,000 per video) outside creators to post from fresh accounts.
  • Controversial marketing: from 2025-11 it used AI-generated fake videos such as "professor meltdown" and "someone enraged over cheating with Turbo AI", which drew fact-checks but also a great deal of exposure.
  • Channel data (ours): direct 68.5%, search 31.5%, and search is 100% brand keywords (turbo ai, turbolearn). That means users see it in short videos and then search the name; search only catches the demand.
  • The free tier (2 hours of recording per month) lets people try before paying.

Speculation:

  • Clear seasonality around finals week and back-to-school (our data: low in July, recovering in Aug–Sep).
  • Dhawan's previous app UMax was also TikTok-driven, so the team already had a short-video playbook.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Pricing: $20/month, $120/year; there is a free tier.
  • Before ads: 300K users, $500K+ in revenue.
  • Starter Story (2024 figures): monthly revenue about $167K.
  • 2025-10: eight-figure ARR, profitable; 5 million users, about 20K new per day.
  • 2026-07: 10 million users, about $13M cumulative revenue, a team of about 10, only $750K raised.

Confidence and gaps

  • Second follow-up check: got the first-hand account of the first 100 users from the official blog; added "300K users and $500K before ads", the matrix playbook of 101 owned accounts and 2,300 videos, the TurboShip paid creator program, the AI fake-video controversial marketing from 2025-11, and passing 10 million users in 2026-07. The takeoff method changed from "Friends and local circle" to "Creator or press spark": the campus only brought the first 100, and the real takeoff was the founder's own viral TikTok.
