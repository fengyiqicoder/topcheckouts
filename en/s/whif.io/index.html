Basics

  • Product: AI character chat specialized for BL (web + app), with a popularity ranking, creator-uploaded characters, a "BL scene mode", an adult mode toggle, and many BL world-setting tags (Omegaverse, Guideverse, etc., with a tag system modeled on Ridi and Lezhin); conversations can be shared.
  • Who built it: Bunker Kids of South Korea (the site's meta author is "bunkerkids"). Founder names not found (not the focus).
  • Launch: no RDAP data for the domain (.io); Wayback first captured it on 2025-07-15; Korean Naver experience posts of 2025-09-05 and 2025-09-17 describe trying it with credits "before the official launch"; Dealroom: launched in Japan, Taiwan, Thailand and the US in 2025-10, now covering 13 countries.
  • Funding: Series A on 2026-08-11, $10.6M in the headline and $12.6M in the body (the numbers contradict each other), investors including Kolon Investment, Kakao Ventures and Mirae Asset Capital.
  • Traffic (profile file): South Korea 56.9%, Thailand 17.6%, Taiwan 11.4%; direct 63.5%, social 27.4% (YouTube, X, Plurk), organic search only 5.5%; brand keywords 66.8%, with the competitor emochi among the search terms.
  • Pricing: not found (credit-based; a blogger mentioned "spending several hundred thousand won").

Where the first users came from: the BL fan-fiction circle among Korea's existing heavy AI character chat users. The evidence is the sponsored Naver experience posts before the 2025-09 launch: the bloggers were themselves heavy Zeta users who had written BL fan fiction for years, WHIF gave them credits to try it and write reviews, and the posts' selling point was "other apps treat male users as women, WHIF is built for BL". The first overseas users came from the X / Plurk circles after the simultaneous multi-country launch in 2025-10 (Taiwan's Plurk is a gathering place for fujoshi); exact numbers were not found.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Influencer/KOL seeding: sponsored Naver bloggers before launch; in 2026, influencer-by-influencer placements on X (Airbridge channel=influencer, each influencer with their own short link).
  • Official X accounts run per country (X_KR / X_JP official bio links, comment contests, submission contests, holiday events).
  • Creator supply: open creator uploads of characters + creator support funds, with the popularity ranking getting good characters seen.
  • Very strong retention: paying users average 217 minutes a day, and direct traffic is 63.5%.

Speculation:

  • Conversations are shareable (a blogger mentioned "you can share your own conversations"), and sharing excerpts on X brings new users, a small branded-output loop, but there is no data.
  • The 27.4% social share mainly comes from paid influencer content rather than organic spread, so pure product spread is judged "Mixed".

Revenue and scale clues

  • Paying users average 217 minutes a day; monthly paid revenue grew an average of 110% month over month over the first 9 months; overseas revenue is 40%, targeting 60% by year-end (Dealroom).
  • Series A of $10.6M or $12.6M (contradictory within the article); the Dealroom graph also lists $14M cumulative.
  • Instagram has 3,025 followers (search summary), so the scale is not big; total users and absolute revenue not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The "launch", "review" and "tiktok" search groups were almost all unrelated generic articles; the main sources are the Dealroom funding story, the originals of two Naver blog posts, the site's meta, and the Airbridge tracking links in the Wayback CDX (which directly show the placement channels and campaign names).
  • The founders, pricing and the Korean official launch date were not found; X posts could not be opened because of the paywall.
  • The takeoff method is classified as "Existing audience": users came from the BL circle within general character chat apps such as Zeta; there is also an element of paying influencers.
