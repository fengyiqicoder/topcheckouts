An 'anime/gaming/cosplay convention with a Christmas atmosphere' launched in Orlando in 2011. It grabbed the year-end slot: Florida's anime cons mostly run in spring and summer, December had few, and from the start it played the 'charity' card (Toys for Tots; a charity ball with toys as admission) to pull in Florida's existing anime and cosplay circles. Over the next fifteen years it scaled step by step from 'hotel con → bigger hotel → convention center'. Every year it books voice-actor guests and runs holiday-themed events (Christmas runway, holiday rave, winter ball), while cosplayers and the community promote it organically on TikTok, Instagram and Reddit, so returning attendees come back year after year. Tiered price increases create pressure to buy early; the $1,900 lifetime VIP ticket locks in superfans. The website is just the annual entry point for buying tickets, booking hotels and applying to exhibit, so traffic rises and falls with the event calendar.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: Holiday Matsuri (HolMat), a year-end multi-genre fan convention (anime, gaming, sci-fi, cosplay) in Orlando, Florida, self-described as 'Florida's Largest festive Anime, Gaming, & Cosplay Convention'. The site sells tickets (app.holidaymatsuri.com), offers hotel room blocks, guest lists and schedules (cms.holidaymatsuri.com), exhibitor applications, volunteer and ambassador applications.
  • 15th edition in 2026: December 18–20, Orange County Convention Center West Hall, with 18+ and late-night events at the connected Hyatt Regency Orlando.
  • Tickets: three-day passes in 4 time-based tiers at $80/$90/$100/$110; single-day $55–70; VIP $300; Forever Festive lifetime VIP $1,900 ($1,800 in 2025); the charity ball is ticketed separately with 100% of proceeds to Gamers Outreach.
  • Organizer: HMCONVENTIONS, LLC (FAQ footer). Neither Wikipedia nor the site names the founders; not found in this research.
  • Domain: registered 2011-06-02 (RDAP); earliest Wayback 2011-07-09, a WordPress site whose first blog post on 2011-06-03 read 'Welcome to Holiday Matsuri…the new holiday themed convention coming this winter'.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Guest-driven: voice actors and actors booked every year (Matthew Mercer, Jennifer Hale, Dante Basco, Max Mittelman and others); each guest announcement is a promotion moment; idol groups (such as Nocturna) announce appearances on TikTok.
  • Community operations: ambassador program, community-team offline meetups, Community Stage fan performances, Discord, volunteer (Staff) system.
  • Pricing mechanics: tiered increases (four months ago $80 → last month $110) push early-bird buying, lifetime VIP locks in superfans, hotel blocks bundle the stay.
  • Search: the channel profile shows search at 48.2%, brand keywords 67.8% ('holiday matsuri', 'holmat 2026'); returning attendees search annually.

Speculation:

  • Cosplayer photos and videos on TikTok and Instagram carry the convention's hashtag and bring new attendees (social sources TikTok, YouTube) — a 'branded output' side effect.
  • After moving into the convention center, larger capacity and exhibitor/Artist Alley revenue support a bigger guest budget.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Claims 50,000+ fans (2025 wording; may be cumulative or total visits). At $80–110 for a three-day pass and tens of thousands of attendances, ticket revenue should be in the millions of dollars (speculation, unverified), plus VIP, exhibitors, vendor booths and hotel-block commissions.
  • A third-party estimate puts annual site visitors at about 300,000 (updowntoday, low credibility).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written fresh from scratch (no earlier report). Evidence for venue changes, first-edition timing and the organizing company is fairly hard (Wikipedia + site + 2011 Wayback snapshot).
  • Gaps: no first-hand sources on the founders, year-by-year attendance or where the first attendees came from (Wikipedia itself is flagged for 'relying too much on primary sources'); Reddit and X posts were only available as summaries. This is an offline convention, so the online taxonomy is only approximate.
