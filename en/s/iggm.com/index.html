The playbook is the standard 2000s 'gold farming' industry formula: an old domain (registered 2002) + one 'buy XX gold/items/boosting' landing page per popular game to capture Google search (including paid search); stock up immediately whenever a new hit online game appears to intercept demand; then lock in trust and repeat purchases with 187k positive Trustpilot reviews, points/VIP discounts, a 10% affiliate program, social media giveaways and sponsored articles on gaming news sites. Basics

  • Product: a marketplace for in-game virtual goods: gold, items, powerleveling/boosting, accounts, gift cards/CD keys; the menu lists 40 games (MMODude records 50+, covering its 108 game pages).
  • Company: Hong Kong Game Bee Technology Co., Limited (12/F A7, Astoria Building, 34 Ashley Road, Tsim Sha Tsui, Hong Kong; registration number not published). The About page says core members come from "leading virtual-goods trading companies in Europe and the US" and have been in the business "since early 2008". Founder names not disclosed; the person interviewed by a gaming news site in 2024 was credited only as "IGGM webmaster". The team being in mainland China is an industry-convention guess, unverified.
  • Domain WHOIS registered 2002-01-12 (some review sites write "operating since 2002" based on this, unreliable). Site footer reads "Copyright © 2017-2026". The Wayback CDX WebFetch was blocked this time, so the first snapshot could not be verified.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Search ranking: one landing page per game and per private server (e.g. Warmane), so players land there when searching "buy X gold/coins"; in our data Organic Search is first and Paid Search third; non-brand keywords in the profile include "fc 27 web app" and "facas mm2".
  • New-game interception: opens a "marketplace" as soon as a new game launches, and posts listing announcements and guides on Facebook and the site's news section (the news section carries large numbers of game guides/event articles that double as SEO).
  • Affiliate commissions: 10% of order value, aimed at YouTube/Twitch/Facebook/Instagram creators; running Google/Bing ads and coupon aggregator sites is prohibited. https://www.iggm.com/affiliate
  • Social media giveaways + coupon codes + holiday promotions (self-described in the webmaster interview).
  • Sponsored articles/review sites: the 2024 eyesongame interview came with an ad placement; several RMT review sites (MMODude, bettergamer, boostingreviews) list it.
  • Trust and repeat purchases: 186k Trustpilot reviews with every negative review answered; points (up to 10% off) + VIP (up to 5% off) + daily check-in + buyback (Sell To Us).

Speculation:

  • The first users came from customers, supply channels and search know-how the team accumulated at Western RMT companies (hinted at on the About page, unverified).
  • The jump in German traffic over the past 2–3 months may correspond to a popular new European game (e.g. the EA FC series) or a newly opened Stripe payment channel.

Revenue and scale clues

  • About 186k Trustpilot reviews (36,578 in the last 12 months); assuming a 5–10% review rate, roughly hundreds of thousands of orders a year (speculation).
  • Third-party estimates: about 724k monthly visits (bettergamer), organic search about 77k/month and trending down (boostingreviews).
  • MMODude ranks it 38th of 413 sellers and 17th of 174 direct sellers.
  • No revenue data found.

Confidence and gaps

  • Company entity, affiliate terms and Trustpilot data come from first-hand pages, credible; the 2008 start and the "Western RMT company background" are self-reported only.
  • Founders, first-user source and brand launch year all not found. This industry is a grey area; the only public interviews are ad-backed sponsored articles.
  • Wayback and the original Reddit posts could not be opened this time; Reddit content comes only from search summaries.
  • Second-pass check: added the 2024 webmaster interview (confirming 2008 and the marketing tactics: social giveaways, coupon codes, holiday promotions, news section), the 2024 impersonation-scam notice, third-party traffic estimates (about 720k/month, organic search declining), and the 2017 copyright start year; the old conclusion was not overturned.
