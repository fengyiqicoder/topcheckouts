Says it is not a law firm and can serve as an expert witness.
  • Company: Sun Factory LLC dba My Solar Claim, registered address 30 N Gould St Ste R, Sheridan, WY 82801 (a Wyoming registered-agent address, common for shell/remote companies); the team claims "200+ years of combined solar experience". Founder Not found (not the focus).
  • Homepage pitch: "the average solar homeowner has $5,000 in unclaimed, unknown incentives"; it also lists failed/bankrupt solar companies by state, linking to news and legal resources.
  • Current traffic (profile): US 100%; direct 63%, paid 27.7%, social 5.5% (Facebook); search terms are all brand keywords (my solar claim, sun factory llc dba my solar claim). From 2026-08 to 09 it went from 0 to 6,633.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • External event: the homepage lists failed solar companies by state, aimed directly at homeowners orphaned by the bankruptcy wave.
  • Paid acquisition: 27.7% paid traffic plus a Facebook social source; search is brand keywords only, meaning people are brought in by ads/outbound calls and then search the name.
  • Stacked trust: actively collects Trustpilot reviews (246, 87% five-star), shows "Verified Client" reviews on the homepage, and responds to bad reviews.
  • Funnel: free inspection → report → $997+ upgrade package / partner installation (referral fees may be one source of revenue).

Speculation:

  • The first customers came from targeted Facebook ads aimed at solar homeowners (or outbound calls from purchased lists of bankrupt companies' customers); the "copy cat ads" mentioned in the reviews show this acquisition model has been copied by peers.
  • The surge in 2026-08/09 corresponds to a boost in ad spend.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Upgrade package from $997; 246 Trustpilot reviews, which at a typical 2–5% review rate implies thousands of homeowners served (speculation).
  • Revenue: Not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • The people behind the company and the specific way the first customers were acquired (ad creatives, outbound calls, mailers) have no direct evidence.
  • Bankruptcy dates are from memory; the original news was not verified in this round.
  • Review dates on the Trustpilot page are not sorted consistently, so the earliest review date may be off.
