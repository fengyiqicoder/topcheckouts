It started in 2005 as an in-house IT training center that the IT outsourcing firm Roue (ルウガルソン) opened for its own staff in Kamiyacho, Tokyo. The center first ran in-person "CCNA hands-on seminars" for the public, then put a free CCNA online question bank and a Cisco command simulator on its site to funnel people into the seminars (finishing the simulator earned a 5,000 yen enrollment discount code). In 2007-08 it was spun out as Ping-t Co., Ltd., and in November launched as the standalone site "Ping-t(β)", bringing over the old site's users, exam-pass reports and study-diary community. For the next decade-plus it spread by word of mouth in Japan's IT certification circles on "free question bank + pass reports and study diaries written by candidates themselves", gradually shifting from seminars to selling premium question-bank memberships and an enterprise edition. Registered users: 5,000 in 2008, 50,000 in 2011, 150,000 in 2017, 250,000-300,000 in 2021, 500,000 in 2026.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: Japanese online question bank for IT certification exams (Cisco CCNA/CCNP, LPIC/LinuC, AWS, OSS-DB, Oracle Master, HTML5, IT Passport, etc.); wrong answers are automatically sorted into "ミス/ヒット" (miss/hit) by how many times answered correctly, with detailed explanations and AI Q&A. Also has a community of pass reports and study diaries; an enterprise "受講管理システム" (training management system); resale of LPI exam vouchers; and from 2026-08 an open "study materials marketplace" where outside authors sell materials.
  • Company: Ping-t Co., Ltd. (株式会社Ping-t), founded August 2007, president 中川 徹, capital 10 million yen, Hanamigawa-ku, Chiba City; business described as "IT education"; LPI-Japan platinum sponsor; development advisor is SonicGarden (ソニックガーデン) (https://mondai.ping-t.com/g/contents/company).
  • Predecessor: Roue Co., Ltd. (ルウガルソン(株)) Kamiyacho training center (opened 2005-02, originally to teach its own staff IT fundamentals, "opened to the public after word of mouth spread"); the site roue.co.jp/coactive had CCNA hands-on seminars, a free "最強WEB問題集 CCNA" (strongest CCNA web question bank) and a Cisco command reference.
  • Launch: ping-t.com registered 2007-09-06 (RDAP); "Ping-t(β版) オープン" announcement 2007-11-02; the mondai subdomain first appears in Wayback in 2021-05 (new question-bank system moved to mondai.ping-t.com).
  • Pricing: some questions free; premium membership is a one-time purchase with no auto-renewal, 1 month 2,640 yen... 1 year 6,930 yen (profile); enterprise edition quoted separately.

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • Cold start: users came from the CCNA learners and seminar students already on the parent company's training center site (the old site already had a free question bank and pass reports), all moved to Ping-t in 2007. The free question bank/simulator was at the time a lead-gen tool for the in-person seminars.
  • Community content: candidates submit their own "pass reports" and "study diaries", which scroll live on the homepage and continuously generate long-tail pages (e.g. /g/passings/6293), while signaling to newcomers that "drill until everything is a ヒット and you'll pass".
  • Actively asking for word of mouth: in 2010 the homepage explicitly said "this site runs on everyone's word of mouth" and asked users to recommend it to classmates and write blog posts.
  • Third-party content: lots of "passed LPIC/CCNA with Ping-t" study logs on Qiita, note, Hatena Blog and YouTube (many search results); brand keywords make up 90.7% of search (profile).
  • Institutional channels: LPI-certified training material, LPI-Japan sponsorship, exam voucher resale, and an enterprise edition for bulk corporate training rollouts.
  • Speculation:
  • Japanese corporate new-hire training commonly requires CCNA/LPIC, and "drill Ping-t until all ヒット" has become the default method in the field; the enterprise edition and word of mouth reinforce each other.
