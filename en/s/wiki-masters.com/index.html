Basics

  • Product: French web trading card game (TCG) where each card corresponds to a French Wikipedia article (about 2.7 million); article pageviews determine rarity, length determines attack, quality determines defense. Gameplay: open a 5-card pack every 10 minutes, albums, quiz battles with friends, player-to-player trading and auctions (virtual currency "wikibidou"), guilds, private messages, leaderboards, achievements, tournaments. 18+ only.
  • Author: Naïm Ouellet, an indie developer from Quebec, Canada (per Next). Currently 2 developers, hiring up to 4. No funding found; looks self-funded.
  • Domain: WHOIS shows the current registration date as 2026-03-19 (Namecheap). The 2018-2019 Wayback snapshots belong to a previous owner and are unrelated to this product; a 2019 HN post "Wikipedia Manipulation and Shilling as a Service" pointing at this domain is the old site. The new site's earliest snapshot is 2026-05-09.
  • Language and market: mainly France, also covering French-speaking regions such as Quebec, Switzerland and Belgium.

Growth loop

Evidence

  • Friend battles: quiz battles can only be played against friends, so to battle you have to bring friends in (presse-citron, official site).
  • Trading and auction market: needs other players for liquidity; more players means more value (official site).
  • Guilds plus leaderboards: guild members push rankings together and actively recruit (official site, outilstice).
  • Streamers: opening packs and "pulling an article you or your viewers know" are naturally watchable; once ZEvent streamers played it, viewers signed up on the spot (presse-citron, next).
  • Press and controversy: Wikimedia licensing, addiction risks, "don't let students play" and other controversies drew follow-up coverage.

Speculation

  • The one-pack-every-10-minutes countdown with a 10-pack cap drives return visits and DAU.
  • Players screenshot rare or celebrity cards and post them to X, Reddit, Discord (social channels include YouTube, X, Reddit, consistent with this).
  • Cards are Wikipedia articles, so "look who I pulled" is inherently talkable, well suited to fan content and meme spread.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Free sign-up. Pro subscription CAD 9.99/month (about 6.5 euros): more frequent packs, higher rarity, more battles and auctions. Virtual currency: 2,500 wikibidou for CAD 4.99 (next, outilstice).
  • DAU: about 10K before the spike, about 200K–215K in late 2026-09 (author's own statement, next and jeuxvideo.com headline).
  • Our data: about 198K checkout visits last month, nearly 0 over the past 4–6 months, consistent with the last month spike.
  • MRR: Not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • The timing of the spike (ZEvent, early last month) is cross-confirmed by multiple French outlets, high confidence. Which streamer played it first and which stream brought the most players varies by source (Domingo, Anyme, Etoiles, ZeratoR); no stream recording evidence obtained.
  • The path from 0 to 10K DAU between March and two months ago is a blank; Reddit was blocked and no launch post by the author was found on X.
  • The original jeuxvideo.com article returned 403; only the headline and search snippet were obtained.
  • The author's full name "Naïm Ouellet" comes from Next only.
