This is not a standalone product but the customer login portal of Dynamo Cover Ltd, an insurance broker in Barry, Wales, UK (company registered 2013, FCA 781314). The company started in 2013–2015 as "cycling insurance designed by cyclists", distributed "online + through bike retailers nationwide"; it then pivoted to vehicle add-on cover (breakdown, GAP, alloy-wheel repair, pothole cover), using low-price hooks like "breakdown cover £19.99/year, £4 a month" to get onto comparison sites (Compare the Market, GoCompare, MoneySuperMarket, Confused.com) and search ads, and by 2022 was selling about 175,000 policies a year ("one every 3 minutes"), claiming 1M+ policies in total. Traffic to the myaccount subdomain is just existing customers logging in; the 2026-09 jump (August 73 → last month 4,986) is a customer-portal migration/domain change, not new growth.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

BIBA member, FinTech Wales member. In 2017–2018 it was an appointed representative of Your Company Matters (FCA 624908), later obtaining its own FCA licence 781314.

Where the first users came from: around 2015 it started through bike-retailer channels and online cycling insurance (site wording "Available Online and Through Retailers Nationwide"). Real scale came after the pivot to vehicle add-ons, via comparison sites and search: the homepage carries Compare the Market, GoCompare, MoneySuperMarket, Confused, Uswitch and money.co.uk badges, and product links carry tracking parameters like affiliate=SEOGAD. Whether the comparison sites are direct distribution partners is not stated clearly on the page (inference).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Low-price hook (breakdown £4/month, £19.99/year) + comparison-site/press badges → purchase → monthly billing and auto-renewal (Trustpilot complaints confirm renewal price increases are a revenue lever).
  • Multi-product cross-sell: the same owner can stack breakdown, GAP, alloy-wheel, pothole, excess and other low-price policies.
  • Brand-keyword search (dynamo breakdown) brings existing customers back to the login page.

Speculation:

  • Most acquisition spend goes to comparison-site commissions and Google search ads (paid acquisition); the myaccount subdomain itself has no acquisition function.
  • The in-house call centre does phone upselling.

Revenue and scale clues

  • About 175,000 policies in the 12 months before 2022 (company's own statement); 1M+ policies and 2M+ customers in total (self-reported); Trustpilot 24,488 reviews, 671 in the past 12 months.
  • At a rough £50–100 average per policy per year, annual premium volume would be about £9–18 million (pure estimate, unverified).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Search results were almost entirely irrelevant (Discover logins, speed-test sites, etc.), so this report relies mainly on the site's About page, Trustpilot, RDAP and Wayback snapshots; the company evolution timeline has medium-high credibility.
  • The 2021 takeoff year is a rough back-calculation from the 175,000 policies/year already reached by 2022-04, not precisely verified.
  • Gaps: Companies House page would not open (registration date, directors, financials unverified); founders' names; whether comparison sites really are the main channel; the exact cause of the 2026-09 subdomain traffic spike.
