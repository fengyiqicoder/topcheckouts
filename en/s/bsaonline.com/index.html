This is not a "startup website" but the public portal that BS&A Software, a Michigan municipal software vendor (making local-government finance, assessing and building-permit software since 1987, headquartered in Bath, MI, claiming 2,100+ local government customers), provides to its clients. A municipality buys BS&A's back-office software, then puts public lookups for property taxes, water bills, permits and property records on a BS&A-hosted site, and links to it from the city's own website so residents click through. The portal was formerly called AccessMyGov.com (already in use before 2011) and was renamed and moved wholesale to bsaonline.com in mid-2018. The traffic comes from B2G contracts: every municipality signed brings a group of residents who must come here to pay and look up records; on top of that, the property record pages are indexed by search engines, so real estate agents, title companies and people looking up a homeowner also search their way in.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: BS&A Online. After picking a municipality you can look up property records and property taxes, pay utility bills and taxes, apply for building permits, schedule inspections and check inspection results; there are also government employee self-service (payroll, attendance) and financial reporting modules. Only open to "participating municipalities".
  • Company: BS&A Software, 14965 Abbey Lane, Bath, MI 48808, "since 1987", claims 2,100+ local government customers; business concentrated in Michigan, with other states too (speculation). Ownership changes not found.
  • Fees: not stated on the homepage; online payments by residents usually carry a convenience fee (from memory, not verified).
  • Traffic (our data): direct 56.8%, search 32.4%, referral 6.5%; brand keywords 82.7% (bsaonline, bs&a), non-brand about 6.5% (e.g. watervliet township property taxes online). Referrals come from municipal sites saginaw-mi.com 5%, bloomfieldtwpmi.gov 4.75% and marquettemi.gov 4.52%. United States 99.9%.
  • Domain: RDAP shows registered 2003-11-17 (the date the previous holder registered it; it later changed hands).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Institutional distribution: municipal websites (Troy, Detroit, Saginaw, Bloomfield Township, Marquette, etc.) link "go to BSAOnline to look up taxes / pay"; the top three referrers in our data are all municipal sites.
  • Every municipality signed adds a fixed group of users: residents must pay property tax and water bills every year, and contractors must apply for permits and book inspections; direct visits are 56.8%.
  • Search: brand keywords are 82.7%, plus long-tail queries like "[town] property taxes online".

Speculation:

  • The 2018 rename was to unify the "BS&A" brand on the portal; the 301 from the old domain carried over years of accumulated links and bookmarks.
  • Property record pages are useful to title companies, real estate agents and people looking up homeowners, a passive long-tail search source.
  • Scale is set by BS&A's software sales team, not by the website acquiring customers itself.

Revenue and scale clues

  • BS&A claims 2,100+ local government customers; revenue comes from software licenses and maintenance fees paid by governments (amount not public), and possibly part of the online payment convenience fees (speculation).
  • The site itself is free for residents.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch: there is no earlier report in notes. Search results were mostly municipal pages and various scam-checker sites, with no startup story; evidence came mainly from Wayback (CDX and snapshots of both bsaonline.com and accessmygov.com), the BS&A website and Slashdot.
  • The timing of the AccessMyGov -> bsaonline.com migration (mid-2018) is reliably evidenced; the earliest AccessMyGov launch can only be placed before 2011.
  • Gaps: BS&A ownership changes, revenue, the growth curve of client municipalities, online payment fee schedule. The explanation pages for Detroit and Troy failed to open twice.
