Basics

  • Product: creators list photos or videos, each item sold only to the first 5 buyers; after a buyer orders, the creator handwrites a message to that buyer on the photo (or records a personal message video), delivered as digital data (nothing is shipped). Items can be sold "immediately" or on a "scheduled" basis, and the official advice is to announce the sale time on Twitter (official note on how to sell, 2023-01). Early rules: each member could make at most 3 posts a day, i.e. at most 15 orders a day (QOOLONG blog, 2020-04). The site is labeled "あなただけの写真集" (a photo book just for you) and "patent pending", with Japanese/English/Malay/Korean interfaces.
  • Price: each item is priced separately. A fan blog estimates photos at about ¥1,000–2,000 and videos at about ¥3,000 (in-the-water.com, the author's impression, not official). Payment by credit card (Stripe); early on, users without a card were directed to Bandl Card.
  • Operation: 株式会社DHN (ONLY FIVE division), 13-4 Araki-cho, Shinjuku-ku, Tokyo. Founder and funding Not found (not the focus this time).
  • Creator admission: corporate/agency accounts + individual (freelance, including people who only do SNS activities) accounts, with a screening process (official note, 2023-01-08).
  • Similar competitor: Marche (marche-yell.com) from the company yell, which a fan blogger calls "almost the same" (allthefeel, 2022-04).

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • First supply = whole idol agency groups moving in (QOOLONG alone brought two groups), motivated by live shows halting during COVID and cheki income dropping to zero; first buyers = these groups' existing fans, drawn in by idols announcing the sale time on Twitter (both the QOOLONG blog and the official note advise "announce on Twitter etc.").
  • Secondary spread on the fan side: fan blogs wrote tutorials/reviews (from 2020-04), and an overseas fan subreddit discussed it (2020-05).
  • The platform has no in-site discovery (the search page is empty), so traffic = creators' external referrals; channel profile: direct 47%, social 29% (mainly X), search 19% (searching creator names + "onlyfive 支払い方法").
  • Platform operation tactic: a sales ranking campaign (the 2023 winter campaign) motivates creators to promote hard.
  • Speculation: the scarcity of the first 5 → sold out as soon as it opens → fans show off the handwritten messages on X → this stimulates fans of the same idol and other idols to follow and move in; word of mouth spreads between agencies (one agency moving in whole = one batch of supply).

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public revenue data. The early cap of 15 orders per member per day implies a single idol's daily GMV of about ¥15,000–30,000 (at ¥1,000–2,000 per order, speculation).
  • last month checkout visits were 17K; monthly GMV may be on the order of several million yen (speculation).
  • Revenue split: the QOOLONG blog says payments first all go to the operator, and agencies set members' share themselves; the platform's commission rate is not public.

Confidence and gaps

  • Medium. The founding timing and the first supply (idol agencies moving in because of COVID) have first-hand blog evidence; the exact launch date, platform commission, number of creators and DHN's founder are Not found. The Reddit post is summary only.
  • Second follow-up check: changed the takeoff method from "Not found (speculated Existing audience)" to "External event tailwind", added first-hand evidence of whole idol agency groups moving in during COVID in 2020-04, the 2023 opening to individual creators and the ranking campaign, and the main creator types (underground idols + gravure idols, not adult-oriented).
