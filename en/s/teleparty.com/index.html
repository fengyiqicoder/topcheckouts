Netflix Party, a Chrome extension built at the end of 2015 by a developer (the site is signed "Made with ♡ by Stephan"), took #1 of the day on its Product Hunt launch, then grew slowly for years on "long-distance couples/friends watching Netflix in sync" to about 500,000 users by 2019. When people stayed home in 2020-03 because of the pandemic, demand for "watching together" exploded overnight. The extension grew to 10M+ and later a claimed 20M+ installs through a forced invite loop: "host opens a room, sends the party link to friends, and friends must install the extension to join". Around 2020-10 it was renamed Teleparty and expanded to Disney+/Hulu/HBO and other platforms; in 2023 it launched a Premium subscription (video and voice chat, more platforms) to monetize, and after the pandemic windfall faded it turned to TikTok creator UGC marketing.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: browser extension (Chrome/Edge/Safari) that plays Netflix, YouTube, Disney+, HBO Max, Hulu, Prime Video and others in sync with group chat; Premium adds video/voice chat, hosting for Crunchyroll/Paramount+/ESPN/Peacock, and custom emojis and badges. https://www.teleparty.com/, https://www.teleparty.com/premium
  • Who built it: in 2016 the site read "Made with ♡ by Stephan" (Wayback 2016-03-08); the original author is presumed to be Stephan Boyer (from memory, unverified). A recruiting company Sprout's case study names the current CEO/founder as Shaurya Jain (UPenn computer science, ex-Google engineer, "built the original extension on his own"), which does not match the 2016 signature; he may have taken over or co-developed later, unverified. In 2020–21 the site carried a Patreon appeal for donations, suggesting it was small and unfunded at the time (Speculation).
  • Launch timing: Product Hunt launch 2015-12-30 (Netflix Party, #1 of the day and #2 of the week); earliest Wayback of netflixparty.com 2016-02; the teleparty.com domain was registered/re-registered on 2019-12-04 (the 2002 snapshots belong to a previous owner), and from 2020-12 teleparty.com became the main site.
  • Channel profile: US 31.5%, India 7.3%, Canada 6.1%; direct 83.8%, brand keywords 96.1%; referrals include hotstar.com, steamcommunity.com, fancode.com.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Invite to use: the host opens a party → "Share the party URL with friends" → every participant must install the extension and have an account for the matching streaming service. Every party brings the extension new installs. (Site's How it works)
  • Community word of mouth: recommended as the standard answer in r/LongDistance and r/netflix (Reddit search summaries).
  • Deliberately building creator UGC from 2026 (Sprout case study).

Speculation:

  • The 2020 explosion mainly came from media "how to watch shows with friends during the pandemic" tutorial articles recommending it all at once, stacked on the invite loop, with no paid acquisition.
  • Referrals from hotstar and fancode (Indian sports/streaming) suggest it is used in India to watch cricket and live sports together.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Users: 500K+ in 2019-07 → 10M+ in 2021-01 → a claimed 20M+ installs (Sprout).
  • Monetization: Patreon donations in 2020–21; Premium subscription from 2023 (the pricing page does not publish numbers, a trial is offered); the free version has promo popups (a Reddit summary mentions "promo ads").
  • Checkout visits: average 20,083 over the past 4–6 months, about 13,000 over the past 1–3 months, a steady mature site.
  • Funding and team size Not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, first report. The PH launch and the user milestones (self-reported on the Wayback site) are solid evidence; "took off with the pandemic" is supported by the site's numbers going from 500K to 10M, but the exact dates of the surge and the media events were not obtained in the original text.
  • Gaps: the founder's identity conflicts (2016 signature Stephan vs. Sprout saying Shaurya Jain); Premium price and revenue unknown; the exact rename date is inferred as 2020-10 from a YouTube tutorial.
