Basics

  • Product: SAT Math only. 7,300+ practice questions, a video explanation for every question (elsewhere the site says 2,000+ video explanations), 24 full-length mock exams, a free 12-question diagnostic with an adaptive study plan; optional 1-on-1 tutoring at $80/hour.
  • Pricing: $49/month, 14-day no-questions-asked refund; the free tier includes a few questions per topic plus one mock exam (official site).
  • Who built it: an unnamed SAT Math teacher, "10+ years of SAT teaching", "perfect scorer", who writes the questions, records the videos, and tutors himself (about page).
  • Launch date: domain registered 2026-02-12 (WHOIS, Namecheap); no Wayback snapshots; earliest sitemap lastmod is 2026-09-07 (about page).
  • Other assets: YouTube @SigmaPrepSAT; free math worksheets (Pre-Algebra through Calculus and Statistics, 541 topics); an AP Calculus AB page; Sigma Prep Math Worksheets listed as an MCP on OpenAI Suite and offered as an API on RapidAPI.
  • Users: United States about 72%, India about 11%, Kazakhstan about 3% (our profile).
  • Note: thesigmaprep.com (CFA/FRM mock exams) and Instagram @sigmaprep.io (electrical engineering) are unrelated products with the same name.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • YouTube teacher IP: the channel speedruns every official practice test to a perfect score, about 700k views per month (self-reported on the site); video descriptions use /go/ short links pointing to the matching question sets on the site.
  • Paid ads: about 53% in our data, the largest source.
  • SEO: 450+ free worksheet PDF pages, formula pages, an "SAT Question Bank" comparison page, and a "best free worksheet generators" comparison article (with itself on the list).
  • Score-report social proof: the site displays students' official score reports and YouTube comment screenshots.

Speculation:

  • The first users probably came from YouTube viewers and the teacher's existing tutoring students ("built by a tutor", and 1-on-1 tutoring has been for sale throughout). No direct evidence on how much earlier the channel started than the site.
  • The worksheet PDFs mainly target teachers downloading them, a low-cost entry point that feeds the question-bank subscription.

Revenue and scale clues

  • $49/month; paid ads account for more than half of traffic, suggesting LTV is enough to cover ad spend (speculation; SAT prep subscriptions typically last only 1–3 months).
  • YouTube about 700k views per month (self-reported).
  • Paying user count and revenue not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • Domain registration date, product and pricing, and channel structure are credible; "700k monthly views" and "+119 point average improvement" are self-reported and unverified.
  • Neither the YouTube channel page nor video pages yielded subscriber counts or publish dates; channel start date unknown.
  • No Reddit, Product Hunt, or press coverage in search results; the teacher's identity is not public.
  • Gaps: when the channel started and its breakout videos, paying user count, who the teacher is.
