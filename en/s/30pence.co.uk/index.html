A UK solo site owner who lived off bank switch bonuses at university noticed that UK bank switching rewards generally require "at least 2 active Direct Debits" to be moved over, and that Stripe's minimum subscription amount is 30 pence. In 2024-06 he registered 30pence.co.uk and used Ghost to build a "30p member newsletter", which is really just the cheapest possible direct debit for sale, with a name that is itself the search term ("30p direct debit"). The first users came by word of mouth in bargain-hunting communities such as the MoneySavingExpert forum (by 2024-11 someone on MSE was already recommending "30pence.co.uk, 30p direct debit, quick to set up"). After that he posted daily on bank switching offers and UK personal finance news to catch long-tail search, and placed referral links for Monzo, Trading 212, Chip, Zopa and others on the pages to earn commission; he claims to have helped with 2,000+ switches over two years.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a "member newsletter" at 30p per month (BACS direct debit via Stripe), used to meet the direct debit condition of bank switching rewards; you can open several (30p each), and there is also a 60p tier to "help us cover costs"; small debit card transactions are also possible. Comes with daily updated switching offer comparisons, beginner guides and UK personal finance news.
  • Operator: a UK independent site owner (the About page is written in the first person "I", unsigned), who says he lived off switching rewards at university.
  • Pricing: 30p/month per direct debit (the owner says the 30p tier loses money), a 60p tier; cancel any time, protected by the Direct Debit Guarantee.
  • Launch: domain registered 2024-06-13 (Nominet RDAP); first Wayback snapshot 2024-06-15 (redirect); the About page says "the past two years" (2026).
  • Traffic (profile record): United Kingdom 100%; organic search 70%, non-brand keywords about 56% of new users (30p direct debit, low price direct debits, best direct debits to set up for bank switching), brand keywords only 37%; AI referrals 4.5%; direct 22.5%.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Product name = search term: non-brand search makes up 56% of new users, who search for terms like "30p direct debit"; the price is the lowest anywhere, so it naturally ranks first in comparison articles (StoozeMax).
  • Community word of mouth: users recommend it on their own in the MSE forum; the switching crowd is small but active, and one switch needs 2 DDs, so users keep coming back.
  • Content + referral commission: daily switching offers and finance news bring long-tail traffic, with Monzo, Trading 212, Chip and Zopa referral codes and Free Pet Lotto links embedded in the text to monetize.

Speculation:

  • The earliest users may also have come from bargain-hunting communities such as r/beermoneyuk and HotUKDeals (the first post was Not found).
  • AI referrals at 4.5% suggest ChatGPT-style tools recommend it when answering "cheapest direct debit".

Revenue and scale clues

  • Claims 2,000+ switches, and "hundreds of people" per month. Direct debit income is tiny (30p–60p/month each); the main income is probably commission from switching and personal finance app referrals (Speculation).

Confidence and gaps

  • A new site written from scratch. The launch date comes from RDAP and the community recommendation from the original MSE forum post, so credible.
  • Gaps: the owner's identity, the first launch post and revenue were not found; Trustpilot has only a few bad reviews (DD takes 8 days to show); the "launch/review" search queries were all irrelevant.
