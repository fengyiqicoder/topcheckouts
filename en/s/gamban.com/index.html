Two Britons with personal gambling-problem histories, Jack Symons and Matt Zarb-Cousin (the latter a well-known anti-gambling-machine campaigner), built software in 2015 that blocks only gambling sites and touches nothing else, playing the "founders have been there themselves" card. Early on it was completely free ("charging is wrong, many can't afford it"), which let recovery organizations, student unions (NUS), forums, banks and regulators confidently write it into their own guidance, with press coverage (The Guardian and others) and industry awards following. Once the user base was established, it switched to a 7-day trial plus low-priced subscription (£4.99/month, £29.99/year), while also doing B2B: gambling operators, banks, recovery organizations and national regulators buy licenses in bulk or funnel their own users through a "Signposting" program. A Portuguese version launched in late 2024, catching Brazil's gambling legalization and the 2025 launch of its national self-exclusion system; Brazil now accounts for nearly half of traffic.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: cross-device (Windows, Mac, Android, iOS) blocking of gambling websites and apps, 190K+ sites and 5,000+ apps, list updated daily (roughly 700 new sites added per day on average), plus recovery tools and impulse-management content.
  • Pricing: 7-day free trial; £4.99/month, £29.99/year; partners can distribute licenses for free.
  • Founders: Jack Symons (founder/CEO), Matt Zarb-Cousin, Mavia Siddiqui (co-founders). UK company, B Corp certified (claims to be the first gambling-blocking tool with B Corp status), ISO 9001/27001.
  • Scale: 55K+ active users, 250K+ license activations, covering 195 countries (About page).
  • Domain: gamban.com registered 2015-01-15 (the 2007 Wayback snapshot belongs to a previous owner), first product page 2015-10.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Institutional referrals: recovery organizations, student unions, banks, regulators and operators recommend it through partnerships or the Signposting program; our data shows 7% of referrals come from gambling-recovery services such as primarycaregamblingservice.co.uk.
  • Being free early on (2016 to roughly 2020) lowered the bar for institutions to recommend it.
  • The company itself says new users mainly come from "online search and word of mouth"; within the 35.1% search share, brand keywords make up 88.3% (gamban, gamban para pc, gamban app).
  • Community: long-running discussion on Reddit r/problemgambling and GamCare forums (both positive reviews and "don't use it" complaint threads, summary only).
  • Multilingual entry into new markets: Portuguese version plus Brazil's policy window.

Speculation:

  • The Brazilian traffic surge comes from media attention after the 2024–2025 legalization, plus recovery organizations and government self-exclusion guidance mentioning "also install a blocking app". No direct evidence.
  • B2B licensing (operators and banks paying for their customers) may be the bulk of revenue, with consumer subscriptions as a supplement.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 55K+ active users, 250K+ license activations (self-reported). If all were on the £29.99 annual plan, consumer revenue would be on the order of a million pounds a year (rough estimate).
  • Our data: Brazil 47.9%, United States 7.7%, Singapore 5.2%.
  • Funding and revenue not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • Founders, founding year, free period, partner list and Brazil timing have first-hand evidence from the official site and Wayback.
  • Search results are full of "casinos not on Gamban" SEO spam; the TikTok group is entirely irrelevant; Reddit could not be opened.
  • Gaps: how the free period was funded (from memory, unverified: early funding from UK gambling operators), the exact date of the switch to paid, the specific source of Brazilian traffic, and revenue.
