Basics

  • Product: a furry / anthropomorphic art community for uploading illustrations, comics, music and fiction; SFW and adult ratings, journals, forums; self-serve ads at ads.furaffinity.net, FA+ subscriptions, a merch store (shop.furaffinity.net, described as "opened to fund the site").
  • Who is behind it: founded in 2005 by someone under the alias Alkora; run 2007–2015 by Sean "Dragoneer" Piché's Ferrox Art; acquired by IMVU in 2015; bought back by Dragoneer through Frost Dragon Art LLC in 2021; Dragoneer died in 2024-08 and his ex-wife Susan "Sciggles" Hess took over (Wikipedia).
  • Launch: domain registered 2005-01-15 (RDAP); forums opened 2005-01-16, and the main site moved from FurPawz to this domain a few days later (Chinese WikiFur); earliest Wayback capture 2005-01-20.
  • Scale: about 20 million visits/month (Wikipedia citing Similarweb); reached 10 million submissions in 2013-02.
  • Traffic structure (profile data): United States 51%, Russia 10%, Germany 5%; direct 87.9%, brand keywords 95%; referrals from theporndude, inkbunny and carrd.co (artists' personal link pages).

Where the first users came from: furry artists (and their fans) who left SheezyArt after its adult-art ban, followed by the migration after y!Gallery banned furry art.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Rival platforms "push people out" → FA takes them in: the SheezyArt (2004) and y!Gallery (2006) migrations are both documented (WikiFur).
  • Artists use FA as their portfolio home; the personal link-page service carrd.co is one of the main referral sources (profile data); Reddit r/furry has many threads on "how to get noticed on FA", showing artists treat it as their main base.
  • The in-person FA:United conventions (2007, 2008) strengthened community identity.
  • The 2022 ban on AI art sided with artists.
  • Revenue: ads (self-serve ad system), FA+ subscriptions, merch, and fundraisers in crises (2005, 2008).

Speculation:

  • Network effects: more artists → more commission buyers → more artists, forming the fandom's de facto "artwork archive"; direct at 88% shows traffic is mostly returning users.
  • Recent growth relies mainly on the furry fandom itself expanding (new fans in the Twitter/Telegram era arrive via artists' links); FA's own product iteration is minimal.

Revenue and scale clues

  • About 20 million visits/month (Wikipedia citing Similarweb); 10 million+ submissions (2013).
  • Revenue sources: ads, FA+, merch; acquired by IMVU in 2015 and returned to a private company in 2021, prices undisclosed.
  • It survived on user fundraisers several times in the early years, suggesting cash flow has long been at "community site" scale.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. Main evidence from English Wikipedia and Chinese WikiFur (English WikiFur returned 403), RDAP and Wayback CDX.
  • Gaps: the IMVU acquisition and 2021 buyback prices, FA+ subscriber count, recent registered user counts; details of the Alkora-to-Dragoneer handover.
  • Reddit, Sitejabber and similar search results were viewed as snippets only.
