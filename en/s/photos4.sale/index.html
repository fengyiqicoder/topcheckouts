New Zealand photographer Allan Ure started in 2013 with a personal photography site, photos4sale.co.nz ("About Me / Gallery Sport"), shooting participants from the sidelines of trail runs, XTERRA, Nduro mountain bike races and Run Walk series around Auckland, then letting them find their photos by bib number after the race and buy them one by one ($9.95 per photo in 2014, $14.95 in 2015). In 2015-16 he changed the pricing to "free if the event/sponsor pays, otherwise voluntary payment (PWYW)" - in his words, "it's a risky move, but we trust the trail running and cycling communities to pay a fair price". This pricing made organizers willing to bring him into more events and left participants with no purchase barrier, and coverage grew from a few dozen events in 2015 to about 300 NZ and Australian events today. Traffic is "each event sends a batch of participants back to look for their photos": organizers send the link, participants search the site name, with almost no marketing.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: an event photography shop - search for your own photos by event + bib number; digital files are free (subsidized by the event/sponsor) or voluntary payment, with no minimum price; for prints it suggests uploading them to Snapfish yourself.
  • Team: the only name on the pages is Allan Ure (orders@photos4.sale); the 2019 event list groups entries by photographer, such as "Oscars 100 Hut 2 Hut - Elise / - Allan", showing a small team with several photographers on the course. The About page says it sends "photographers who understand both the emotion of sport and the technique".
  • Coverage: mainly New Zealand (Tarawera Ultramarathon, the XTERRA series, Taupō Ultramarathon, etc.), plus some Australia (Buffalo Stampede, Margaret River Ultra, Warburton Trail Festival). The event list on the About page has about 300 entries.
  • Domain: earliest Wayback capture of photos4sale.co.nz is 2013-02-17 (personal photography site); GA account UA-39868322 is also from 2013; photos4.sale first seen 2019-07-23, and both domains now coexist.
  • Social: Facebook "Photos4saleNZ" has about 4,500 likes (summary only); Instagram @photos4.sale posts a "photos are online, voluntary payment" post after every event (the 2026-10-17 Taupō Ultramarathon post has 47 likes).
  • Traffic (profile): search 49.8% (brand keywords 83%), direct 33.6%, referral 10%; New Zealand 100%.

Where the first users came from: the participants Allan Ure himself photographed at trail run, mountain bike and XTERRA events around Auckland - whoever he shot would come to the site to find their photos by bib number. The earliest "customers" were really the organizers who let him onto the course; the participants followed the events.

Where new users come from: every additional event adds a batch of participants; after the race, organizers put the photo link in emails/social media (referral 10%), and participants search "photos4sale + event name" (brand searches are 83% of search traffic); it also posts on Facebook/Instagram after every event. After the switch to free/PWYW, organizers were more willing to bring it in (a sponsor can pay so the photos become a perk for participants), and event coverage widened.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Event → participants: one event has hundreds to thousands of people, and all of them come looking for photos afterwards (direct + brand search add up to about 80%).
  • Pricing redesign: free/PWYW since 2016, and the site directly invites organizers to "learn about using the PWYW model at your event".
  • Event coverage: dozens of events in 2015 → expanded to Australia from 2019 → about 300 events now.
  • Social posts after every event drive traffic (Facebook 4.5k likes, Instagram).

Speculation:

  • The photos themselves carry a watermark/site name, so when participants repost them on social platforms they indirectly advertise the site.
  • The trail running community is small with a high repeat rate, and the same people run several events a year, so return visits are strong.

Revenue and scale clues

  • In 2014-15 photos were sold at $9.95-$22.95; since 2016 it is free + PWYW, with revenue from sponsor package fees and voluntary payments; amounts are not public.
  • About 300 events; Facebook about 4,500 likes. No revenue data.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The whole timeline comes from original Wayback pages, so confidence is high; "the founder is Allan Ure" rests only on the About page contact and the photographer grouping, with no interview seen.
  • Search results were almost all irrelevant (generic startup articles, viral photo news); there are no interviews, press coverage or Reddit discussion.
  • Gaps: Allan Ure's personal background, average order value / payment rate after PWYW, the price of the sponsor subsidy model, headcount.
