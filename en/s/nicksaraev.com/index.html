nicksaraev.com is the personal site of Canadian AI automation creator Nick Saraev. It sells nothing itself and works as the business card + email signup + traffic router for his content business. After running AI copywriting (1SecondCopy, nearly $100K/month) and the automation consultancy LeftClick, he took a friend's advice and started making Make.com tutorials on YouTube, and found that titles with "Make.com" got clearly more views (borrowing a tool name for long-tail search): about 3,000 subscribers in month 1 and about 10K in 3 months. He then opened a paid community on Skool (first called Make Money With Make, $28/month, capped at 400 people; he put the link under the hottest videos and on Twitter, made nearly $1,000/month overnight, and within 10 days raised the price to $128/month and filled up), later renamed Maker School ($184/month, 2,600 paying members), with the free community Maker Zero (34K people) as the funnel. He claims 1.5 million subscribers across YouTube + Instagram and about $330K/month from Maker School.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a personal site (long posts, bio, a collection of free resources, email signup, sponsorship contact); monetization happens off-site: the paid Skool community Maker School ($184/month, claims to "guarantee your first AI service client"), the free community Maker Zero, the consultancy LeftClick, and YouTube/Instagram/newsletter sponsorships.
  • Who runs it: Nick Saraev, Canada; started businesses at 19 and has done event promotion, a Udemy course (about $80K), door-to-door local marketing, wedding/corporate videography (zeroed out by COVID in 2020), Upwork front-end freelancing, the AI painting site 1SecondPainting (topped Hacker News in 2020, about $2,200/month before being sold), the AI copywriting service 1SecondCopy (taking orders on Fiverr/PeoplePerHour, "a little under $100,000" a month), and LeftClick (co-founded with partner Noah, later solo, peak month about $72K).
  • Launch timing: domain registered 2020-03-21; the Wayback 2020-09 homepage introduced him as "technology enthusiast & body language coach", "teach around 25,000 people online", selling AI books and an AI art generator; the site says "He has written here since 2018" (old blog migrated over, not verified). YouTube channel registered 2020-03-20; Skool account joined 2023-07-17.
  • Current numbers: YouTube @nicksaraev 570K subscribers, 352 videos, 25.97M total views; Instagram @nick_saraev 712K followers; Skool personal profile 45.2K followers; Maker School 2.6K members, 114 reviews at 4.9; Maker Zero 34.1K (free); podcast "Nick Saraev: Claude, Codex & AI Agents".
  • Traffic (profile data): search 41.5% (brand keywords 77%: nick saraev, maker school), direct 43.9%, social 8.8% from YouTube.

Where the first users came from: early YouTube viewers were people searching for Make.com tutorials (his own summary: "videos with Make.com in the title get more views"); the first 400 paid community members came from friends he messaged, Twitter contacts, and a link placed under a few hot videos (his bio's own words).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Starting the channel through long-tail search: tutorial titles borrow tool names like Make.com (later n8n, Claude Code) to capture search.
  • Video → community: hot video descriptions carry the community link; a cap plus step-by-step price rises ($28 → $128 → $184) create scarcity.
  • The free community Maker Zero (34K) is the upper funnel for the paid Maker School; the promise of "guaranteeing your first client".
  • Members helping each other lowers operating cost (LinkedIn post: "people who joined last month answer the people who joined today").
  • Multi-platform distribution: YouTube, Instagram reels (712K followers, the /resources page collects the free tools mentioned in reels), podcast, newsletter; takes video/reel/email sponsorships.

Speculation:

  • He updates his content direction with every AI tool wave (Make → n8n → Claude Code), using each new tool's search window to keep bringing in new users.
  • The personal site mostly catches "search his name" brand traffic and helps build trust; it is not the main acquisition channel.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Official site FAQ (per a WebFetch summary): Maker School 2,200+ paying members × $184/month ≈ $330K/month; combined "$4M+ per year". The Skool page now shows 2.6K members.
  • Third-party LinkedIn post: Skool community makes $315K+ a month, run by one person in about 90 minutes a day.
  • Early on: 1SecondCopy nearly $100K/month, LeftClick $72K in one month.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The useful search results were the official homepage, the bio page, the Skool page, the LinkedIn post, and Instagram/podcast listings; YouTube about, the Wayback 2020 snapshot and RDAP were also used to fill in data.
  • The bio page gives no exact year or month for the YouTube start or the community launch; 2023 is inferred from the Skool account created in 2023-07, which conflicts with "May–July 2024" in the bio and could not be confirmed with 2024 Wayback snapshots.
  • All revenue figures are self-reported by him or third parties and not verified.
