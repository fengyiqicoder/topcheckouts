Started in 2005 from a 'catch plagiarism' project at the Université de Savoie (France), made its name through an AFP wire story, and sold B2B plagiarism checking to universities (Magister). Once universities used it, students had a hard need to self-check before handing in a thesis, so in 2010 it launched Studium, a pay-per-use consumer product, then used multilingual SEO blogs (how to write thesis acknowledgments, how to end an internship report) to capture student search traffic. After ChatGPT in 2023 it added AI detection for a second wave of growth; Italy became the largest consumer market, matching the plagiarism-check demand for Italian university graduation theses (tesi).One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • B2B -> B2C spillover: institutions use Magister to check student work, and students use Studium to self-check to avoid getting caught (the Studium page copy: 'avoid penalties, hand in with peace of mind: tesi, tesine, internship reports...').
  • Multilingual (French/English/Italian/Spanish/German/Portuguese) blog content on writing methods for long-tail SEO (such as 'how to write thesis acknowledgments' and '8 examples of how to end an internship report').
  • A reseller/distributor network expands to overseas institutions (since 2020); attending education conferences and BD field trips (AQPC, Liksik, APAIE, ENAI), positioning as 'the European alternative to Turnitin' to win institutional deals.
  • Integrates with Moodle, Blackboard, and Canvas, so teachers use it directly inside the LMS and there is steady usage once a school buys; Similarweb referrals include university domains in Ecuador, Mexico, and Lille, France.
  • Studium's first analysis is free and shows the 3 most similar sources, a hook for converting students to paying users.
  • Speculation: there is one piece of indirect evidence for Italy becoming the largest country: the official site specifically held an Italian-language AI conference with the University of Parma and MIUR attending, which shows Italy is a priority institutional market. This may stem from several Italian universities buying Compilatio (or an Italian distributor pushing it), combined with the hard self-check need in Italy's thesis season; Referrals ranking third also points to referrals from school LMSs/school websites.
