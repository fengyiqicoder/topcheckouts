German composer Hendrik Schwarzer, because "existing tools couldn't express what he wanted", first built a gap-filling Kontakt string runs library, Orchestral String Runs, in 2010 (released 2010-12-01, a small Joomla site taking PayPal), selling it to film/game composers, a small crowd already gathered on forums and in the Kontakt ecosystem. In 2013–2014 it expanded into the Berlin series (strings/woodwinds/brass/percussion) recorded at Berlin's Teldex studio and became one of the professional orchestral sample library brands. In the second half of 2020 it launched its own free SINE player and a per-instrument store; from 2021, "free forever" SINEfactory instruments, Berlin Free Orchestra, and other freebies capture "free vst" searches and get reshared by plugin news sites and YouTube to pull in new users, who are then converted from account holders into paying buyers.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: orchestral and experimental sample libraries (Berlin series, Metropolis Ark, TIME, etc.) running in its own free SINE Player (VST/AU/AAX); buy full collections or individual instruments; free instruments available (15 SINEfactory instruments, Berlin Free Orchestra, FFOSSO, Layers, etc.); 40% student/teacher discount.
  • Team: founder and CEO Hendrik Schwarzer (composer); offices in Berlin and Freiburg; partnered with Berklee College of Music on Berlin Orchestra (2021) and the free YouTube teaching channel "Virtual Orchestration". Source: https://www.orchestraltools.com/about
  • The About page says "founded in 2005", but the orchestraltools.com domain was registered 2010-08-23 (RDAP), first Wayback snapshot 2010-11-01.
  • Traffic: United States 24.7%, Brazil 9.8%, Taiwan 5.3%; direct 55.3%, organic search 36.9%, non-brand search concentrated on free-instrument queries like "sine player", "berlin free orchestra", "free church organ vst"; referrals from free plugin sites like audiopluginsforfree.com; Instagram 33k followers.

Where the first users came from: no direct evidence found. Inferred from the product form (speculation): the first product in 2010 was a niche string runs library in Kontakt Player format, aimed at scoring composers already using Native Instruments Kontakt, and the homepage featured only the founder's own scoring work; the main discovery channels for such libraries at the time were composer forums like VI-Control and demo videos (from memory, unverified).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Free instruments for accounts: SINEfactory and other free instruments require logging in or registering an OT account to claim, then install into the SINE player, where paid instruments can be bought directly.
  • Free instruments capture search and plugin news sites: non-brand search queries are mainly free-instrument terms; referrals come from sites like audiopluginsforfree.com; KVR lists SINEfactory.
  • Co-branded giveaways: a joint giveaway of a paid instrument with Mastering.com in 2026 (covered by BPB); SINEsingles single-instrument flash sales at 50% off.
  • Education channel: Berklee partnership + 40% student/teacher discount + free YouTube teaching channel.

Speculation:

  • Early on (2010–2015) growth relied mainly on composer word of mouth and demo videos; once the Berlin series, recorded at Teldex with high quality, became one of the "standard film scoring libraries", brand search and direct visits took the bulk (direct 55%).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Individual instruments €20–€50, full collections cost more; no public revenue or user numbers.
  • Instagram 33k followers; the high share of traffic from Brazil and Taiwan is presumably related to the free instruments and education discount.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site written from scratch. The 2010 start and product evolution are backed by Wayback snapshots; the SINE/SINEfactory dates come from first Wayback CDX capture times, which are only upper bounds, not exact release dates.
  • Search results were mostly coupon sites and generic recommendation articles; Reddit queries returned irrelevant results.
  • Gaps: the 2005 vs 2010 founding year contradiction; first-user channel (forums like VI-Control) is from memory only; no revenue figures.
