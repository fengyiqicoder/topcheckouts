Ace opened a UK-based grading service with "nearby postage and nicely made cases and labels", and drew its first submissions through Randolph's own channel and the unboxing videos of the card YouTube/TikTok community. After that, every Ace-graded card circulating on eBay and other secondary markets carries an Ace label and certificate number (buyers search "ace cert check" to verify authenticity), so the graded cards themselves became a continuous customer-acquisition advertisement.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: trading card (mainly Pokémon) authentication and grading + ultrasonic encapsulation + custom labels (three types: Standard / Colour Match / Ace Label), with QR codes and Cypheme anti-counterfeit tags; has a population report and certificate lookup. Charged per card, from about £12-18 per card (profile file).
  • Users: UK TCG collectors and sellers (UK traffic 77%). International submissions opened in 2026 (TikTok homepage: "International Submissions now open").
  • Team: media reports say "Randolph is the person behind Ace Grading" (GoNintendo); Tabletop Gaming reports that the company director is Andrew Shane. The office is in Banbury, UK (Nintendo Life search summary, summary only). Randolph is a UK Pokémon pack-opening YouTuber; his channel size is, from memory, several hundred thousand subscribers, unverified.
  • Funding: not found; looks like a YouTuber's own business.

Where the first users came from: Randolph's own YouTube audience + social warm-up (the 2021-08 Coming Soon page first carried five social links), and after opening, the "Launch Edition" commemorative case created launch scarcity; at the time PSA's backlog ran to months, so UK collectors had a ready-made pain point. The evidence is site snapshots; the specific number of first submissions was not found.
Where new users come from: card YouTube/TikTok "Ace grading unboxing / score reveal" videos (with discount codes), Ace-graded cards circulating on the secondary market (brand search + "ace cert check"), and the mainstream media exposure from the 2024-12 theft.

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • Graded cards carry the brand: each card's label + certificate number gets checked by buyers on resale; the profile shows about 14% of non-brand search comes from "ace cert check"-type terms.
  • Creator unboxings: multiple YouTubers post Ace score reveal videos with a 5% discount code (creators have an affiliate/discount arrangement).
  • Founder's own audience: Randolph is a YouTuber, and the media all report it as "a YouTuber's card company".
  • Speculation: at launch, the PSA/CGC backlog was the key external tailwind; the "nice-looking cases and labels" are themselves designed for filming and photo sharing, suited to spreading through unboxing content.

Revenue and scale clues

  • About 930 Trustpilot reviews (2026 summary); the stolen cards were worth about £250K / $315K, indicating a large volume of submissions on hand.
  • At £12-18 per card, no direct revenue figure was found.

Confidence and gaps

  • The launch timing comes from Wayback snapshots, so credibility is high; Randolph's relationship to the company comes from media reports, credibility medium-high; Randolph's channel size, the number of first submissions and revenue were not found. The BBC, IGN and Reddit originals could not be opened, so only summaries were used.
