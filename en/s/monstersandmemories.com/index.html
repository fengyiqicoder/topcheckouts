Shawn Lord, lead designer of the original EverQuest, ran a series of livestreams in 2020 interviewing old EQ colleagues and fans, and the conversations gradually turned into "let's build our own low-budget classic MMO." Development was then streamed publicly throughout; the team is 40-plus part-timers and volunteers (including people from the Project 1999 and EQ emulator scenes), self-funded, and from 2023 on it ran repeated free public stress tests and playtest weekends. Every test got picked up by MMO outlets like MassivelyOP and mein-mmo and by veteran player communities, and each round drew more testers than the last (about 3,000 in 2024-04, over 10.5K in 2026-06). Finally, on 2026-10-01 it opened early access through its own launcher on the official site at $15/month, skipping Steam, and passed 30K subscribers within two weeks. This is a case of cashing in, all at once, the demand built up over 15 years by the EQ nostalgia community.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: classic-style (EverQuest-type) fantasy MMORPG, low-poly hand-painted look, emphasizing grouping, open world, no instanced dungeons. Early access launched with level cap 60, 28 zones and 18 classes.
  • Pricing: $15/month subscription, no one-time purchase price and no in-app purchases; distributed only through the official site and its own launcher, not on Steam.
  • Developer: Niche Worlds Cult (US), formally incorporated 2021-05. Creative director Shawn Lord (aLovingRobot), formerly lead designer on EverQuest, design lead on DC Universe Online and executive producer on Star Stable. Team of 40-plus, most of them part-time or hobbyist contributors.
  • Funding: no outside investment, no crowdfunding, no publisher; reportedly about $160K spent in total from 2020 to 2026-05 (costs published on the site's /costs page; MassivelyOP report 2026-06).
  • Website: earliest Wayback snapshot 2021-05-30 (Squarespace; the homepage said it "grew out of MMO fan discussions during a series of livestreams").

Where the first 1,000 users came from: Shawn Lord's livestream audience plus the EverQuest nostalgia scene (Project 1999 / EQ emulator communities, which is where team members themselves came from), gathering on Discord. The first public test in 2024-04 already had about 3,000 sign-ups. Discord membership and the earliest livestream links were not found.

Growth loop

Evidence

  • Public dev livestreams: the developers frequently stream their work (mentioned on both the official site and the cannonade blog); viewers become testers, testers become community members.
  • Recurring free public playtests: every round of testing is a media event (MassivelyOP / mein-mmo / mmorpg.gg / mmos.com cover each one), with participation growing from 3,000 to over 10,000.
  • MMO social-friction design: forced grouping, open world, no instanced dungeons. Veterans need to bring their regular group or guild along, which is group-based multiplayer user acquisition.
  • Radical transparency: the costs page and the live metrics page (subscriber count, players online per server) have themselves become media material; mein-mmo's report was written straight from the metrics page.
  • Not on Steam: all traffic and payments run through the official site (Direct is the top channel).

Speculation

  • Guilds and regular groups signing up as a unit: one guild leader brings in 10–40 people.
  • Streamer and YouTuber playtest videos drive secondary spread (YouTube leads the social channel), but no specific breakout video was found.

Revenue and scale clues

  • $15/month; 30,277 subscriptions on 2026-10-14, 32,909 when scraped on 10-19 → roughly $450–490K in monthly revenue (rough estimate).
  • About $160K in cumulative dev costs 2020–2026; the first month's revenue covered the entire cost (mein-mmo's conclusion).
  • Concurrent players (when we scraped) 492 across 6 servers; 1 EU server, players mostly from the US.

Confidence and gaps

  • The timeline, tester counts and subscriber numbers are all backed by media coverage and the official metrics page, so credibility is high.
  • Gaps: Reddit was blocked, so we did not get the early posts from r/MMORPG and r/MonstersAndMemories; Shawn Lord's earliest livestream channel and dates, the Discord size, and whether any big streamer drove growth were not found. Some MassivelyOP originals returned 403, so only search summaries were read.
