Andup, a small Japanese company, first built an "AI prompt marketplace" in 2024 that almost nobody bought from (prompts on the homepage sold for ¥50–1000, mostly free, with single-digit downloads). Around August 2024 it turned into a platform for "AI illustration and AI manga posting + membership subscriptions + tips", catching Japanese AI creators who were restricted or buried on Pixiv, Fantia, DLsite and similar places because of AI works. The platform pulled creators in with prize contests (the first one had a ¥20,000 prize); to get paid, creators bring their own fans over from X, DLsite and Fantia, fans buy platform credits to tip creators, and the platform takes 15–20%. New users grew from 4,000 a month in mid-2025 to 7,000 a month by year-end, membership passed 100,000 in March 2026, and in October it was renamed Aniborn to go overseas.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: an AI illustration, manga and prompt posting platform with leaderboards, creator profiles, membership subscriptions (monthly-fee exclusive content), single-item purchases, gift tipping, user-run contests, community, video, DMs and a mobile app (2026-06). Fans buy platform credits (on-site currency) and tip with them, and creators can cash out in yen. Cut 20% (15% for some, see profile).
  • Company: Andup Inc. (Shibuya, Tokyo), founded 2024-03-15, representative director Takahiro Fujita. It also runs XenoChat (AI character chat, officially launched 2026-03), XenoStrike beta, PromptCraft and the "ミーハー" trend app. Source https://andup.co.jp/
  • Domain: registered 2024-04-07 (RDAP).
  • Renamed Aniborn (aniborn.com) on 2026-10-01, with the old domain redirecting automatically. https://andup.co.jp/news/promptcom-to-aniborn/
  • Funding: not found. Announced a business partnership with US-based Kazama Inc. on 2026-04-15.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Pivot: it only started to get volume after turning from "selling prompts" into "AI work posting + fan payments" (the 2024-05 snapshot shows almost no downloads; from 2025, 4,000 to 7,000 new users per month).
  • Contests to pull in supply: it started with official prize contests, then opened them up so anyone can run a contest or set a theme, giving creators a continuing reason to post.
  • Creators bring fans: since creators want subscriptions and tips, they bring their own fans over from X, DLsite and Fantia; in our referral data dlsite.com and fantia.jp are 5.79% each and donmai.us 3.86%, and social comes from X, Pixiv and YouTube.
  • Public leaderboards, creator profiles and a supporter ranking, and work pages can be shared.
  • Japan is 89.8%, brand keywords are 73.5% of search, and direct visits are 70%.

Speculation:

  • The external push behind takeoff was the restrictions or segregation of AI works on platforms like Pixiv, Fantia and DLsite, leaving AI creators needing a dedicated place to earn money. From memory, the exact dates of each platform's policies unverified.
  • A lot of the content is suggestive "AI beautiful girl" material, using this kind of theme to attract paying fans (the first contest's theme was literally "AI Beautiful Girl").

Revenue and scale clues

  • Total monthly creator support: about ¥2 million+ in 2025-06 and about ¥4 million+ in 2025-11 (official homepage). At a 15–20% cut, the platform's monthly take is about ¥600–800K (estimate).
  • 100,000 members (2026-03-17), 7,000+ new users per month (2025-11).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The company, timeline and scale figures all come from the official site and original Wayback text and are credible; the scale figures are self-reported by the platform.
  • Most search results were unrelated companies with the same name (myprompt.com, prompt.com domain for sale and so on); no media coverage or PR TIMES press release was found.
  • Gaps: the founder's background, where the first creators were recruited from (whether by DM invitations on X), funding.
