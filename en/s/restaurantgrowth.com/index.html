Partners Luke Januschka and Mike Coborn ran restaurant ad management for about 10 years (claiming $480M in cumulative sales for clients, from mom-and-pop shops to a Nasdaq-listed chain). In early 2024 they bought restaurantgrowth.com, which had long been listed for sale on Afternic, and unified the business under the "Restaurant Growth" brand: managed ads + 1-on-1 coaching + a weekly live webinar community + the $199/month Restaurant Growth University. Acquisition is a classic agency funnel: they run ads and post content on Facebook/Instagram themselves (23.6% of traffic is social from FB/IG; Instagram has 28K followers and 1,000+ posts), lead people to a "14-day free trial / two-week free webinar" and a "book a call", then close with client case studies and Trustpilot-style reviews. Since the second half of 2024, 60+ blog posts and service pages also target long-tail terms like "facebook ads for restaurants". The claimed client count grew from 2,000+ (2024-04) to 4,800+ (2026), and Canada sends the most traffic (54.7%). During the pandemic they ran a viral "free toilet paper with takeout" campaign, the only takeoff event in their own account, but its date and spread data were not found.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: restaurant marketing agency (Facebook/Instagram/Google ads, SMS, email, SEO, geofencing, etc., with a trackable dashboard of customer visits and spend), 1-on-1 coaching, weekly live Zoom webinars and a community, and Restaurant Growth University ($199/month, aimed at restaurants under $30K monthly revenue or those who want to DIY, with a free trial); it also advertises "interest-free funding".
  • Team: partners Luke Januschka and Michael (Mike) Coborn; head of operations Jenna Doerring; coaches Adam Barringer, Marcus Osterhout, Angela M. Hepler, Alí A. Quero, Donald Burns and others. Source: https://www.restaurantgrowth.com/about/

  • Domain: RDAP shows registration on 2005-03-10; 2005-2008 it was an Orlando travel spam-content site, 2016-2019 a JS/cookie blocking page, 2021-12 it redirected to an Afternic sale page; since 2024-04-03 it has been the current Restaurant Growth site.
  • YouTube @restaurant-growth: registered 2019-09-26, 6.45K subscribers, 515 videos, 5.38M total views (2026-10).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Paid social + content: 23.6% of traffic is social from Facebook and Instagram; IG @restaurantgrowth_ has 28K followers and 1,026 posts; YouTube has 515 videos.
  • Free hooks: 14-day free trial, two-week free webinar, free demo, then "book a call" to close.
  • Long-tail search: 60+ blog posts and service pages, with non-brand keywords such as facebook ads for restaurants and how to calculate cogs (organic search is only 4.7%).
  • Client return visits: clients check in-store visit data from their ads in the dashboard; direct traffic is 68.5%.

Speculation:

  • Canada is half the traffic, possibly because ads are mainly placed in Canada or existing clients are concentrated there (unconfirmed).
  • Before 2024 the two likely ran the same agency under another brand/domain, and renamed and upgraded to Restaurant Growth in 2024; the old brand name was not found.
  • A typical agency model: growth comes from ad budget and sales calls, with no product self-spread.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Client count claims: 2,000+ (2024-04) → 4,000+/4,800+ (2026; numbers on the same page disagree).
  • Claims $480M cumulative sales for clients and $44M+ in ad spend managed (the 2024 site counter reads 44, unit presumed to be $M).
  • Revenue not found; the course is $199/month, agency pricing is not public.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Google results were almost all irrelevant review-site articles; the main sources are the site's About/Webinar pages, the sitemap, Wayback snapshots and the YouTube channel page.
  • Client counts and sales figures are the site's marketing claims, and the page's own disclaimer says results are not typical.
  • Gaps: the company's founding year, the brand used before 2024, the date and press coverage of the toilet paper campaign, the founders' backgrounds and revenue were not found, so the takeoff year is "Not found".
