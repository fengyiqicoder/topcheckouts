Basics

  • Product: AI content studio aggregating third-party models (Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1, Sora 2, Seedance 2.5, Wan, Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image, Midjourney, Grok Imagine, etc.) to generate video/images/audio; features include Motion Control (motion transfer), AI influencers, templates (one photo generates a multi-shot viral video, marked new), voice and stem separation. Sources: https://ausar.ai/, https://www.viddir.com/products/ausar-ai
  • Who built it: the site only says "© 2026 Ausar", with no company entity, address or founder; the Terms page is a 404. A third-party review (aiera.blog 2026-05-20) also says "new brand, short track record, young domain, low Scamadviser trust score, but appears to be a real working service". Team and country Not found.
  • Launch date: domain registered 2026-05-15 (RDAP); earliest Wayback snapshot 2026-07-18, by which point it was already a complete product (image/video/Motion Control/audio/AI influencers, affiliate, "46% off" promotion) but did not yet have the template feature.
  • Pricing: free sign-up without a card; $9/month ($108 billed annually), Starter $15/month, etc., plus $31 and $119 tiers; subscriptions include credits, and members get 10–25% off top-up credits; all paid tiers have no watermark.
  • Channel profile: US 55%, India 16%, UK 8%; direct 43%, search 43% (brand keywords 86%), paid 8.6%; social comes from Instagram, Facebook and X.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Own-account viral post → comment keyword → DM with template link → user uploads one photo to generate the same video: IG/FB post captions are uniformly "Comment 'Ausar' for the link to the template".
  • Creator supply: the site's "Creators on Ausar" section showcases partner creators (Kuba with 1.6M TikTok followers, patryczek with 390K), and their videos carry an ausar.ai watermark (alici.ai noticed the ausar.ai watermark in the top-left corner when dissecting kuba.creates.ai's videos).
  • Affiliate program: 30% commission, covering subscriptions and credit top-ups, uncapped, no follower threshold, paid out via PayPal/Wise. https://ausar.ai/affiliate
  • A "Made with Ausar" showcase wall on the site invites users to submit their work to the homepage.

Speculation:

  • Free-tier output probably carries a watermark (paid tiers are stated as "no watermark"), so videos posted by free users act as ads.
  • 8.6% paid traffic plus a big-discount countdown (46% off) suggests it is also buying ads.
  • Templates productize the "viral video" itself: viewers see a hit, replicate it in one click, then post it again, creating secondary spread, but the evidence so far only reaches the official-account level.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Checkout visits: average 120 over the past 4–6 months, 367 three months ago, 90 two months ago, 13,433 last month
  • IG 109K followers; best single reel 262K likes.
  • Pricing $9–$119/month; at the scale of last month checkout visits, monthly revenue could be in the tens of thousands of dollars (Speculation, not self-reported).
  • No funding or MRR self-reports seen.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, first report. The launch timing (RDAP + Wayback) is reliable; the takeoff via "IG template short videos + comment DMs" is supported by multiple social media summaries, but the original Instagram/Facebook posts could not be opened, and the like counts come from search summaries.
  • Gaps: the operating team and country are entirely unknown; TikTok account data, and which specific video drove the last month jump, cannot be proven directly (the timing just matches); two months ago's checkout visits of only 90 are low and may reflect a payment channel switch affecting how it was counted.
