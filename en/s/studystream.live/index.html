During the 2020 pandemic school closures, a few students (company name Knowledge Collective, Oxbridge student background, operating on UK time) moved "the feeling of everyone studying together in a library" onto Zoom: several big Zoom rooms open 24/7 with mics and chat off and cameras on, where anyone could click a link and study together. The room itself is the product; the more people, the stronger the "library feel". Add a Discord community, mindfulness classes, "Creators" events hosted by top students, and study-with-me content on Instagram/TikTok, and by 2021 it quickly claimed "100K+ people entering Focus Rooms daily" and a Discord of 320K+. By its Product Hunt launch in 2021-08 it was already a mature community; it then migrated from Zoom to its own web app plus mobile apps, monetizing through paid small rooms and membership. Today the homepage shows 17K people online in real time, and two thirds of traffic is returning users coming back directly.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: online "study with strangers" rooms (Focus Room). Camera on, no audio, no private chat, with Pomodoro timer / tasks / music; web + iOS/Android apps + Discord server.
  • Company: Knowledge Collective (site footer ©2021 to present).
  • Founding team: the makers on Product Hunt are Sarujan Ranjan, who says he built it with co-founders Tianyou Xu and Erfan Soliman (maker comment on the PH product page); Michael Seibel's name appears on the Launch Team without a stated role. The official site does not name the founders.
  • Early form: in 2021 it was still Zoom meeting rooms; the FAQ said "log in to Zoom before entering a Focus Room", "we turn off audio and private chat", "16+, separate pre-university and university servers". A Her Campus review described the first university server at about 800 people on screen (51 pages).
  • UK traces: event times in BST; in 2021-06 the site had a batch of "personal statements from Oxford/Cambridge/Imperial students" pages (abbie-earth-sciences-oxford etc.), doing admissions-content SEO plus tutor matching ("Live On-Demand Tutoring, matched with students from top UK/US universities").
  • Domain: registered 2020-08-23 (RDAP); first Wayback snapshot 2020-11-04, then called "StudyTown", with the page saying only "StudyStream is an online learning and motivation platform, sign up to enter StudyTown".
  • Current state: homepage shows roughly 17K online in real time (including people online in Discord and the app); the StudyStream Discord server currently has 197,580 members (checked via the Discord invite API). Traffic by country: India 30%, United States 26%, Vietnam 7%.

Where the first 1,000 users came from: no direct evidence. What can be confirmed is that within six months, 2020-11 to 2021-04, it went from a sign-up page to Zoom servers with hundreds of people on screen; channel traces include Instagram (@studystream_), the official Reddit account, Discord, student media (Her Campus) and a YouTube channel. Speculation: the earliest users came from the founding team's UK university/secondary-school circles and the study-with-me scene on Instagram/TikTok, with pandemic school closures as the biggest tailwind.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • The room is the product and crowd size is the value: public Focus Rooms are free with no barrier (click the Zoom link and you're in), and the more people on screen the stronger the "library effect" (the site's "Psychology of Mimicry/Accountability").
  • Community operations: Discord (320K+ at PH time, 198K now), weekly events, mindfulness classes, "Creators at StudyStream" (top-student hosts), Reddit/Facebook groups, email reminders for events.
  • Free to paid: public rooms free, Premium small rooms/events paid (2021 FAQ), later in-app membership.
  • Organic user sharing: in 2021 many people in the Zoom rooms put their Instagram handle in their display name (Her Campus); "study with strangers" is a self-spreading topic.

Speculation:

  • Content like "I study on Zoom with thousands of strangers" on TikTok/Instagram was the main driver of the 2021 surge (the company later started its own TikTok); no specific viral video found.
  • New-user search terms are now mostly brand keywords (91.7%), meaning growth relies mainly on word of mouth and social media, with SEO a small share.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 2021: claimed 100K+ people entering rooms daily and 1M+ study sessions (self-reported, possibly inflated); Discord 320K+ (self-reported on PH).
  • 2026-10: about 17K online in real time; Discord 197,580 members.
  • Revenue: free public rooms + Premium/membership, prices not found; team size and funding not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site written from scratch. The early form (Zoom rooms, Knowledge Collective, PH 2021, Discord size) has Wayback and PH evidence and is fairly reliable.
  • Founders known only from the PH maker list, no interviews found; "founded by Oxbridge students" is inferred from the site's personal-statement pages and BST time zone, unverified.
  • Gaps: the specific source of the first users, whether there was a viral TikTok in 2021, what the "recent changes" actually were, and revenue scale are all not found. Search results are mostly irrelevant generic pages.
