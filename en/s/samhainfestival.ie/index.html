Not an internet product but the website of a community Samhain (Halloween) festival and Púca parade in the small town of Clonakilty, County Cork, Ireland. It is organized by the local arts group Geata Arts; the parade is powered by a few hundred local artists, musicians and residents who form their own groups and register. The site is just the schedule, registration and donation hub for the few weeks before the festival, so traffic pops up seasonally every October–November (August 0 → September 6,332) and is near zero the rest of the year.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: festival website — event calendar, individual/group registration for the Púca parade, sponsors, photo/video/audio archive, donations (GoFundMe). The 2026 festival runs November 5–8, with the parade theme "The Music Of What Happens".
  • Who is behind it: contact email geataartsgroup@gmail.com; the GoFundMe organizer is listed as Geata Arts, which calls it "Ireland's longest-running annual Samhain festival and parade". The website was built as a sponsorship by local company Screenzest Digital (sponsors page).
  • Launch: the current domain was registered 2023-09-13 (.ie WHOIS); earliest Wayback capture 2024-01. There is also a 2005 capture of www.samhainfestival.ie/robots.txt, so the domain was in use years ago (whether by the same organization is not found). The photo archive covers 2017, 2018, 2019 and 2022–2025, so the festival has run since at least 2017 (2020–21 missing, presumably cancelled for the pandemic).
  • Sponsors: about 20 local businesses including Clonakilty Park Hotel, The Studio Spillers Lane, West Cork Discovered and Screenzest Digital; no public funding mentioned on the page.
  • Traffic (profile data): Ireland 73%, United Kingdom 27%; search 47.3% (brand keywords 86.8%, e.g. samhain festival clonakilty), direct 39.5%.

Where the first users came from: the local community — the parade is a collective art event involving "hundreds of visual artists, performers, composers and musicians" (/about); participants register individually or as groups and make their own costumes and masks; the audience is mainly locals and tourists visiting West Cork. The website itself does no acquisition.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Parade participants register (/parade-registration); they are both performers and promoters. The site keeps archives of past years' photos/videos/audio.
  • Visits are almost entirely brand-keyword search and direct, concentrated seasonally before the festival.

Speculation:

  • Groups that take part each year (schools, bands, art collectives) bring family and friends to the event and post photos/videos on Facebook (facebook.com/ClonakiltySamhain) and Instagram, feeding next year's participants; local tourism accounts (West Cork Discovered is a sponsor) help drive traffic.
  • Seasonal press about "Ireland as the birthplace of Halloween" brings a small number of overseas/UK visitors (UK is 27%).

Revenue and scale clues

  • A non-profit community festival funded by local business sponsorship and donations; the 2026 GoFundMe targeted €7,000 and raised only €495 (11 donors), with the organizers describing severe underfunding.
  • Traffic scale: about 6K monthly visits before the festival (profile data).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. Search results were almost all generic Samhain culture articles and same-named festivals in other towns (Limerick, Malahide, Kells, Galway), unrelated to this site; used the official homepage / about / sponsors / photo archive, GoFundMe,.ie WHOIS and Wayback CDX.
  • Gaps: the festival's first year, attendance figures over the years, Geata Arts members, the website used before 2023 (possibly a Facebook page). "Longest-running" is the organizer's own claim, unverified.
  • Takeoff year cannot be determined, so it is marked "Not found"; this is a local community event with no takeoff moment in the internet sense.
