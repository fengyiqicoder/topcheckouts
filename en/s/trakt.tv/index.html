Trakt launched in 2010 as a beta, entering by writing "automatic logging plugins (scrobbling)" for home media centers like XBMC (later Kodi): after you finish an episode, the record syncs to Trakt automatically. The first users were the XBMC/HTPC player community, and the "now watching" feed scrolling on the beta homepage ("pang2000 is watching CSI: Miami") came entirely from XBMC. It then expanded to Plex, MediaPortal and Boxee and opened an API so community developers could build apps. Today Stremio, Kodi plugins, Plex and other third-party apps all treat "sign in or sync with Trakt" as standard, and Trakt has become infrastructure for TV tracking data. The product is embedded in other players, and every integration brings in new users for it. It monetizes through VIP subscriptions: in 2025 it raised the price to $60 a year and tightened the free quota; after TV Time shut down in 2026 it launched Trakt Time to take in migrating users.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a movie and TV tracking website and app. Features include automatic watch history logging from media centers or players, mobile check-ins, show progress tracking, a calendar, lists, ratings and reviews, recommendations and "where to watch"; an open API (developer.trakt.tv), a community forum and r/trakt. In 2026 it added Trakt Time (tvtime.trakt.tv, with an "import from TV Time" entry).
  • Pricing: free version plus VIP. VIP was originally $30/year; in 2025-02 it announced a rise to $60/year while limiting the number of lists and watchlist items for free users; in 2026-02 it published new quotas, and from 2026-07 free users can connect only 1 community app.
  • Team: the 2014 About page lists three co-founders: Justin Nemeth (development), Sean Rudford (development), Jimmy Douglas (business). The current ownership situation was not found.
  • Domain: earliest Wayback snapshot 2010-10-14 (beta page, © 2010).
  • Users: US 28%, Spain 8%, UK 5%; direct traffic 76.6%, brand keywords 94.5%; referrals from imdb.com, stremio.com and simkl.com (channel profile).

The first 1,000 users: XBMC media center players. The evidence is that the viewing activity on the 2010 beta homepage was all "watching in XBMC", and the earliest plugin on the download page was also for XBMC.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Embedded in upstream players: XBMC/Kodi, Plex, Stremio and others all have Trakt plugins or sync, referral traffic includes stremio.com, and users in Reddit's StremioAddons community share their Trakt lists.
  • Open API and a community app ecosystem: "many community apps" as early as 2014, and the developer portal is still being updated.
  • Social sharing: early on it supported syncing watches and ratings to Facebook, Twitter and Tumblr.
  • User lists (official lists like the IMDb Top 250, and user-made lists) are referenced by various plugins, forming a supply of content.

Speculation:

  • People come back to check in every day (direct traffic 76.6%); the more watch history accumulates, the higher the switching cost, which is why it can raise prices without losing its core users.
  • The 2026 shutdown of TV Time is an external tailwind, and Trakt Time may absorb a batch of mobile TV-tracking users.

Revenue and scale clues

  • The company says it relies "entirely on VIP membership revenue" (its response when raising prices in 2025-02). VIP is $60/year.
  • Total users and paying users were not found (from memory there is talk of "millions of users", unverified).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch (no earlier report). The origin path (XBMC plugin → multi-media-center → API ecosystem) rests on 2010-2014 Wayback snapshots, so the evidence is fairly solid.
  • Gaps: later ownership changes and funding, the exact origin of Trakt Time after the TV Time shutdown, and user scale were not found (the official /about page and the Google Play page could not be opened, and the About snapshots after 2019 are compressed data that could not be parsed).
