Basics

  • Product: shared-subscription account platform. After signing up, users pick up shared logins for streaming, ChatGPT/AI tools, Canva, design, cloud storage, VPN and other "100+ subscriptions" from a panel (numbered account pages like /cgcode2.php, /canvacode5.php). Also sells paid "private/team seats" (Trustpilot reviews mention buying a 3-month AI private plan and getting kicked out of the team, and paid private accounts turning out to be shared).
  • Who built it: the 2025-01 page lists the address "Bagmane Laurel, C V Raman Nagar, Bengaluru" and email admin@oxaam.com; the Trustpilot profile says San Francisco with contact email oxaam5@gmail.com (a personal Gmail). Operator's name not found.
  • Launch date: RDAP registration 2024-09-23; first Wayback capture 2024-09-23; first homepage content captured 2024-11-29; about, freenetflix, freechatgpt and testseries pages captured 2025-01-02.
  • Evolution: 2025-01 title "Free Netflix, Free ChatGPT & Best Online Test Series" (exam question bank + free Netflix/ChatGPT) → 2025-03 added /articles/ AI blog (WordPress) → 2025-05 numbered account-distribution pages appeared → 2025-07 homepage became "World-Leading Free Digital Marketplace, 100+ paid apps, subscriptions for ₹0", claiming "4.5 stars (50.4k reviews)".
  • Traffic (profile): India 52%, Pakistan 11%, Bangladesh 5.9%; direct 81%, brand keywords 99.4%, almost no non-brand search; referrals t.me 2.6%, cpa.rip 1.77%, 5amsayabas.blogspot.com 1.62%; social from YouTube, Telegram, Facebook.
  • Third-party: Trustpilot 4.2 stars, 1,425 reviews, but flagged by Trustpilot for violations and fake reviews removed; Reddit r/AI_Agents has a post "Oxaam is a scam guys don't buy this crap" (summary only); a Glarity summary says it is known for "offering ChatGPT and other subscriptions free or very cheap".

Where the first users came from: no launch post. The most direct evidence is the traffic structure (direct 81%, brand keywords 99.4%, referrals from t.me and cpa.rip, social from YouTube and Telegram), which says users saw the site name in Telegram channels or YouTube "free ChatGPT Plus / Netflix" videos and then searched for it themselves. Which specific channel or video kicked it off: not found.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • "Get paid subscriptions for free" is itself a strong hook, spread via Telegram/YouTube tutorials (profile referral and social sources).
  • cpa.rip referrals: a typical CPA content locker/task wall; users presumably have to complete tasks to unlock accounts, which is both monetization and a revenue share for promoters (cpa.rip's nature inferred from the domain, unverified).
  • Fake reviews as trust signals (site claims tens of thousands of reviews; Trustpilot removed fake ones).

Speculation:

  • Promoters (YouTubers, Telegram channel owners, the blogspot site 5amsayabas) keep publishing tutorials for CPA commissions, forming a paid distribution network. When accounts get banned, new ones are issued, so users keep coming back to "pick up accounts", which explains the very high direct share.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Revenue sources (speculation): CPA tasks/content lockers, ads, paid "private/team" plans (Trustpilot reviews confirm paid plans exist). Amounts not found.
  • Scale: 1,400+ Trustpilot reviews; the site claims 50k reviews (not credible).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Most search results were irrelevant (SQL load testing, CVEs); official site blocked by Cloudflare. Main evidence comes from Wayback snapshots of 2025-01 / 2025-07, Wayback CDX, RDAP, Trustpilot and the profile.
  • Gray-area business; the promotion channel (which Telegram channel/YouTuber) was not found. Takeoff classified as "YouTube/Telegram tutorial spark" based on traffic-source structure, with no specific event.
