In May 2025, a group of neighbors in Washington DC ran a "Month Offline" challenge (trade your smartphone for a flip phone for 30 days, with weekly in-person meetups). Participants needed a working flip phone, so they flashed a $20 TCL Flip 2 with dumbOS, which syncs to the main phone, and sold it as "dumbphone 2" ($20 device + plans from $15.99/month). The first users were Month Offline participants in each city's cohort (10–30 people per cohort, 400–500+ in total). Its rebellious "against all social media" persona then rode the Gen Z phone-detox wave to free press from Axios, the Guardian, TechCrunch, CBS, TIME and others, and in August 2026 it self-reported 5,000+ active users.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: dumbphone 2, a "companion phone" built from a modified TCL Flip 2 + in-house dumbOS, syncing the main phone's texts/call forwarding/WhatsApp/Signal, with maps, Uber/Lyft, Spotify, 2FA, no social media and no email; runs on the T-Mobile network. Hardware $20, three plans Dumbest $15.99 / Dumber $20.99 / Dumb $25.99 (per month), 4-month minimum. Notebookcheck calls it the company's "second device", showing an earlier generation existed (dumbphone 1, details not found).
  • Team: a small DC team in their 20s–30s; the about page lists only first names (Lydia, Theo, Jack, Milk, Marco, Danny, Aaron, Afreka, Josh). Founding CMO / "chief dumb organizer" Lydia Peabody (27, a former licensed psychotherapist and herself a Month Offline participant); communications director Afreka Ebanks. A Guardian snippet mentions a male founder who "tried seven 'dumb ideas' with money made from his first company", name not found (the Guardian original failed to load, summary only). Funding came from friends and family (TechCrunch), no institutional round seen.
  • Related community: Month Offline (offline.community), contact email organize@dumb.co, and dumb.co provides loaner flip phones, so it is in fact a user-acquisition community run by dumb.co itself.

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • First users = Month Offline participants: the challenge itself requires swapping to a flip phone, and dumb.co lends/sells the phones; each cohort of 10–30 meets weekly in person, then becomes alumni, then starts new cities (DIY guide, "1-844-FLIPOFF" voice signup). The user CBS interviewed took exactly this path (offline.community; CBS).
  • "Project Xtraordinary": funds college students to run "dumb down" events on campus (community page, Luma signup) → campus grassroots outreach.
  • Anti-social-media persona + topical marketing (handwritten cover letters, phone-free bars, New York Fashion Week) → keeps earning free press (40+ pieces on the press page); new users mostly search the brand name after reading coverage (our channel profile: direct 53.8%, search 34.7% of which 88% brand keywords).
  • The phone is a conversation piece in public: "strangers come up and ask" (TechCrunch quoting Ebanks).
  • Early reviews by KOLs in the dumbphone circle (Jose Briones / Dumbphone Finder, r/dumbphones).
  • Speculation:
  • The company doesn't run its own social media, and media exposure is the main traffic source, so checkout visits are extremely spiky (surged after the September BI coverage).
  • A 4-month minimum plus monthly fees gives each user an LTV of at least $84, enough to cover the cost of running the offline community.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 2026-06 "hundreds sold" (Notebookcheck) → 2026-08 "5,000+ active users" (CBS, company self-reported).
  • Rough math of 5,000 users × $16–26/month gives about $80k–$130k monthly revenue (speculation).
  • Our data: April to June averaged 2,726, July 1,755, August 661, September 18,392.

Confidence and gaps

  • The origin (Month Offline in DC in 2025-05) and user count (CBS 5,000+) are well evidenced; the founder's name is still not found (not the focus); the date of dumbphone 1 is not found.
  • The Guardian, BI and Washingtonian originals could not be fetched, so only summaries were used.
  • Second-pass check: takeoff year changed from 2025 to 2026 (2025 was the community start; product sales and the media surge both came after 2026-05); added Month Offline's scale/cohort mechanism, the two scale points of "hundreds" in June → "5,000+ active users" in August, and the early Dumbphone Finder review.
