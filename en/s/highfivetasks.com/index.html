This is the crowdsourced recruiting site of the AI data collection company Vera AI Co, Inc.: it pays ordinary people in the US to strap a phone to their head and record first-person video of themselves washing dishes, doing laundry, cleaning and mowing the lawn, paid weekly, with the video used to train "the next generation of wearable devices" (in practice, the first-person training data for robots/embodied AI that is hot in 2026). The domain was registered in May 2026 and it pulls people in two ways: first, recruitment ads pitching "1099 side gig, earn money by doing chores" (the application link carries utm_campaign=1099v3), and second, Dub generates a personal referral link (go.highfivetasks.com/name) for every onboarded person, and new people applying through the link are credited to the referrer. In September-October 2026 it added a "Collect & Win" sweepstakes: uploading task videos randomly earns bronze, silver and gold collectibles to exchange for prizes, using gamification to raise recording volume.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a participant app "High Five Contributor"; applications go through Typeform (2 minutes, no resume), a head mount holds the phone to film first-person video, with no audio recording; recordable tasks: dishes and kitchen, laundry, cleaning and tidying, lawn care. Payment: the homepage says both "paid every Monday" and "paid every two weeks", amount not public. Participants sign as independent contractors (1099).
  • Company: the sweepstakes rules name the sponsor as "VERA AI CO, INC"; the homepage says the video is "used to improve next-generation wearable technology".
  • Coverage: "most US cities"; our records show United States 89.1%, India 4.2%, Philippines 3.8%.
  • Subdomains: interview.highfivetasks.com (application page, Typeform embed), go.highfivetasks.com (Dub referral short links).
  • Domain: registered 2026-05-05 on Namecheap (RDAP).
  • Traffic (our records): direct 66.2%, search 25.3% (brand keywords 86.5%, plus "chores jobs"), paid 5.1%; social sources Facebook, Instagram, Reddit.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Ads: the homepage application button carries utm_source=highfivetaskscom&utm_campaign=1099v3; a source comment says about 60% of applications before July carried utm_campaign, showing ads are the main source; in our data paid traffic is 5.1% and social comes from Facebook and Instagram.
  • Referral commissions: every participant has a Dub referral link (go.highfivetasks.com/name), and the backend has a dedicated referral lead-processing script.
  • Gamified retention: the Collect & Win sweepstakes, "at most one item per task per day", encourages recording every day.
  • Low barrier: "2-minute application, no resume, no interview, do the chores you already do".

Speculation:

  • The first participants came from recruitment ads on Facebook/Instagram and Reddit side-gig boards (r/WorkOnline, r/beermoney and the like); no specific posts were found.
  • The India and Philippines traffic is from overseas side-gig seekers who saw the ads but cannot apply (US only), which is spillover traffic.
  • The key to scaling is data buyers' budgets: Vera AI sells the data to companies building household robots or smart glasses, so collection volume is driven by orders.

Revenue and scale clues

  • This is a cost center (it pays participants); revenue comes from selling first-person data to robotics/wearable companies, amount unknown.
  • Participant count unknown; referral links have appeared under several different names, showing a fair number of active participants.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch: there was no old report in the notes. The search results were all unrelated (a Reddit post about a same-named Japanese entertainer, health sites, freelance site roundups); the work relied mainly on the official homepage, sweepstakes rules, contractor agreement, application page source comments, Wayback CDX and RDAP.
  • Source comments are first-hand evidence, so the ad and referral mechanisms are credible; that the utm name "1099v3" points to recruitment ads is speculation.
  • Gaps: Vera AI's background and funding; pay rates; number of participants; data buyers.
