listary.net is not a new product but the Chinese official site of the long-established Windows file search / launcher Listary (listary.com, since 2010), registered in 2023 and launched in April 2024. It connects existing users already built up in the Chinese productivity community (brand keyword searches make up about 70%) to a Chinese-language site, converts them with a price cut of "free core features + Pro bought out at ¥98 in China", then writes blog posts like "Everything vs Listary" and "What to do when Windows search is bad" to pick up non-brand long-tail traffic, while routing traffic to Gety (AI full-text search), a new product from the same team.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: Windows file search + app launcher, featuring 0.01-second search, fuzzy matching and pinyin initials, "quick switch" of folders inside open/save dialogs, and searching network drives and shared drives; V7 Beta was released in 2026-07, rewritten in Rust, claiming up to 4x faster launching and search and about 30% less memory, and offering a local HTTP search API for AI/Agents. Sources: https://www.listary.net/, https://www.listary.net/v7
  • Pricing: core features free (personal use only); Pro is a one-time buyout, list price ¥140, China discount ¥98, including commercial licensing, lifetime upgrades, up to 3 computers, and a 30-day refund; Stripe checkout. Source: https://www.listary.net/pro
  • Who runs it: the footer copyright in 2024 read "© 2010-2024 Bopsoft" and in 2026 became "© 2010-2026 Little Carved" (the entity name changed, reason not found). The site does not name a founder; the sister product Gety (gety.ai) is from the same team. The founding team are Chinese developers (from memory, unverified).
  • Launch timing: listary.net registered 2023-06-08 (whois); first Wayback capture 2024-04-17, promoting "Listary V6.3 Beta released"; first Chinese blog post 2024-04-22. The English main site listary.com was already captured by Wayback in 2010-01, then still called "Free Search Utility for Lists".
  • Traffic (profile data): brand keywords 69.6% (listary, listary pro), non-brand search 16.6% ("类似于 everything 的软件" [software like Everything], "快速 搜索 文件 工具" [quick file search tool]); direct 44.8%, referral 8.3%; China 78.9%, Hong Kong 5.4%, Taiwan 5.2%; about 3,000 per month, fairly stable.

Where the first users came from: Listary's early users came from English software download sites and tech blogs around 2010 (Scott Hanselman's recommendation is one piece of evidence; coverage by Lifehacker and others is from memory, unverified). The first visitors to the listary.net Chinese site were ready-made Chinese users: brand searches make up about 70% and direct visits 45%, and people on Chinese forums such as 52pojie have long discussed Listary Pro (https://www.52pojie.cn/thread-2043176-1-1.html, summary only); Chinese productivity media (Sspai, Appinn) recommended Listary in earlier years (from memory, unverified).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Free core + bought-out Pro: the free version covers personal use, and Pro is cut to ¥98 in China with commercial licensing and lifetime upgrades to drive conversion.
  • Search ranking: Chinese blog posts target long-tail terms like "software like Everything" and "Windows search is bad", with non-brand search at 16.6%.
  • Cross-promotion: the homepage and a built-in Listary command (type gety + space) send users to Gety, and in turn Gety has a "gety-for-listary" landing page.
  • Version updates bring exposure: after the V7 Rust rewrite, media such as XDA and WindowsForum published comparison articles; comparison pieces + the AlternativeTo list keep bringing "Everything alternative" traffic.
  • Community: the Chinese and English forum discussion.listary.com and a Bilibili channel.

Speculation:

  • Word of mouth is the main source: productivity-tool users recommend the "Everything + Listary" combination to each other on forums, Zhihu and Bilibili, which shows up as a high share of brand keywords.
  • The Malwarebytes false positive may have briefly cost some overseas downloads, but it has little effect on the Chinese site.

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public revenue or user numbers. The Chinese site gets about 3,000 visits per month and Pro is ¥98 one-time; even with a decent conversion rate the Chinese site alone would make only a few thousand to ten-odd thousand RMB a month (speculation); the main revenue should come from the English site and enterprise licensing, not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Most search results were relevant (official site, forum, whois, Malwarebytes, XDA, AlternativeTo); the timeline comes from whois, Wayback and the site's blog, so credibility is high.
  • The founder's name, the reason for the entity changing from Bopsoft to Little Carved, and revenue and user numbers were not found; early media coverage is entirely from memory, unverified.
