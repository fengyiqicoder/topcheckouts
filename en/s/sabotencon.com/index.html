At the end of 2007, AniZona, Phoenix's only anime convention, announced it would not run in 2008. Matt Solberg (Square Egg Entertainment), an anime vendor who made a living setting up at AniZona and also the show director of Phoenix Comicon, immediately registered sabotencon.com and posted an open letter, used the forum to rally fans and volunteers, and in May 2008 put on the "stopgap" Saboten-Con. In 2010 it was handed to the husband-and-wife team Monkey Paw Entertainment, which fixed it on Labor Day weekend and took a "fans for fans" community route (volunteers + 800 hours of fan-run programming + guests such as voice actors every year). After moving into downtown Phoenix in 2015, attendance grew from 2,730 in 2011 to about 23,000 in 2021 (50,000 turnstile attendance), the gaming show SaboSlam was added in 2023, and 50,000+ attendance is expected in 2026.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: Arizona's largest and oldest anime convention (4 days, Labor Day weekend, at the Sheraton and Renaissance hotels in Phoenix); the website publishes guests, schedule, contests, hotels and tickets.
  • Founding: launched in 2007-12 by Matt Solberg (describes himself as Saboten Con chairman, Phoenix Comicon show director and anime vendor); Hoodline says it was founded by Square Egg Entertainment, the company behind Phoenix Fan Fusion, and acquired in 2010 by Monkey Paw Entertainment, owned by the couple Greg and Stacy Fennell.
  • Tickets (2026): Friday $30, Saturday/Sunday $50, Monday $30, full weekend $70; SaboSlam day pass $20 / full weekend $40; free for under 12.
  • Domain: sabotencon.com registered 2007-12-13 (RDAP), first Wayback snapshot 2007-12-21.
  • Traffic (profile file): US 97%; direct 45.8%, search 40.7% (brand keywords 78%, non-brand such as "phoenix anime convention"), referral 8.1% (Facebook, Meetup); traffic concentrates around the convention.

Where the first users came from: it directly took over the Phoenix anime fan base left behind when AniZona stopped - the founder was a longtime AniZona vendor and Phoenix Comicon show director, rallied fans, volunteers and staff through an open letter on the site and the forum at the end of 2007, and landed well-known voice actors like Laura Bailey for the very first edition.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Community co-creation: the slogan "For the fans, by the fans"; in 2026, 800+ hours of "community-driven" programming and lots of volunteers; forum (early) → Facebook/Meetup (now).
  • Guest-driven: each year it announces voice actors, cosplayers and bands as guests, with social media announcing them one by one (Facebook "Please Welcome Sora Tokui").
  • Fixed dates + product line expansion: a fixed Labor Day weekend builds an annual habit; adds SaboSlam (gaming) and SaboEve (New Year's Eve).
  • Regional monopoly: after AniZona it became Phoenix's only dedicated anime convention, with dates staggered from Phoenix Fan Fusion.

Speculation:

  • Attendees post photos/cosplay to social media every year, bringing local new fans (natural word of mouth for events).
  • The 2015 move to a large downtown hotel was key to the acceleration (attendance grew 30%+ a year in 2015-2017).

Revenue and scale clues

  • 23,000 people in 2021 × tickets of $30-$70 gives rough ticket revenue in the million-dollar range (speculation), plus exhibitor booth and hotel partnership income.
  • 50,000+ attendance expected in 2026.

Confidence and gaps

  • Established offline event site, written from scratch. The founding story comes from the chairman's open letter in the 2007 site archive (first-hand), and attendance figures come from Wikipedia and the site.
  • Hoodline says Square Egg founded it and Monkey Paw acquired it in 2010, which is consistent with the open letter signed by Matt Solberg (Solberg was then Phoenix Comicon director), but no first-hand source for the acquisition details was seen.
  • Gaps: attendance for 2019 and 2022-2025, revenue, and the site's own online acquisition data were not found.
