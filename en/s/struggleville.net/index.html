Basics

  • Product: a daily tool and hardware deals blog (WordPress), organized by merchant (Amazon, Acme Tools, Ace, Harbor Freight, Home Depot, Lowe's, Menards, Walmart) and category; includes Amazon promo codes, warehouse deals, an HF coupon library, a daily email, the Struggleville Deals & Coupons app and a merch store.
  • Author: PJ (says "Helping you afford the tools you need since 2013"; he fixes his own cars and house on a tight budget, so he hunts for tool deals every day); email PJ@struggleville.net. Digital media management is provided by 66.media (SixtySix Media Inc.). https://struggleville.net/about/
  • Monetization: affiliate commission such as Amazon Associates + ads + paid deal posts from merchants (regular posts $5–7 each, pinned posts need pre-approval) + merch. https://struggleville.net/services/
  • Traffic mix (profile record): direct 65%, social 26.2% (YouTube, Facebook), email 5.2%; search is brand keywords only; referrals from xndeals.com and maijiahuideals.cn (both are intermediary platforms where sellers promote deals). 100% of visitors are in the United States.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Early long-tail search: the Harbor Freight coupon, free gift coupon and coupon policy pages from 2014–2017 are typical high-frequency search term pages.
  • Multi-channel matrix: website + YouTube (in-depth reviews and high-value deal alerts) + TikTok (store clearance) + Facebook page and Facebook group ("Invite your family and friends") + r/Struggleville + daily email + app. Social is now 26%, email 5% and direct 65%, showing that returning readers visit every day.
  • Merchant-side supply: intermediaries where sellers promote deals, such as xndeals.com and maijiahuideals.cn, send traffic (about 3.7% each), and the owner sells paid posts to Amazon sellers, so merchants pay to keep a steady flow of deal content.
  • User word of mouth: a smartcustomer comment, "I've gotten $60 tools delivered to my door for around $5 bucks many times strictly from following struggleville.net".

Speculation:

  • After 2019 Google demoted coupon sites and the site's search traffic fell, so there is almost no non-brand search now, and it relies on YouTube and Facebook to retain returning readers.
  • The Facebook group's "invite family and friends" is probably one of the main ways to bring in new people.

Revenue and scale clues

  • YouTube 56.6K subscribers, 13.11 million views.
  • Revenue comes from affiliate commission + paid posts ($5–7 each; at a few dozen a day, probably a few thousand dollars a month, Speculation) + ads + merch. No public revenue figures.

Confidence and gaps

  • The full name and home state of the owner PJ were not found; the YouTube channel was registered in 2011, but the content of the earliest videos was not verified.
  • "2019 takeoff" is judged by the time of the redesign into a daily deals site, with no traffic curve to back it up.
  • This is a new site, written from scratch this time; the search results were mostly irrelevant, and the main evidence came from the official About/Services pages, yearly Wayback archives, RDAP and the YouTube channel page. The Reddit interface was blocked, so the subreddit's subscriber count was not obtained.
