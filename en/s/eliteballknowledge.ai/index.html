Basics

  • Product: an AI short-video creation course — topic selection, visual consistency for AI images/video, script structure; course recordings, a prompt library, script frameworks, a private Skool community; early versions also promised "a possible future chance to join our team".
  • Pricing: $197 one-time, lifetime access, labeled "founding price, going up soon" (since 2026-09, per Wayback and the current site).
  • Who is behind it: the site is unsigned, saying only "The creator behind the method — publicly building with AI". Linking evidence: the Children Of Khan Facebook page bio reads "www.childrenofkhan.com / ai video course: www.eliteballknowledge.ai" (search snippet); Brandsearch shows childrenofkhan.com as a Shopify apparel store registered in Sheridan, Wyoming, with social accounts TikTok @santeluca and Instagram @lucamaxiim, 388 Meta ads, some landing on eliteballknowledge.ai. The founder may be named Luca (inferred from the handle, unverified).
  • Launch: domain registered 2026-05-05 (RDAP, GoDaddy); first Wayback capture 2026-05-12, already a course page ("Hurry, only limited spots left").
  • Site claims: 1B+ views, 6K+ videos, 13K+ customers ("customers" most likely refers to Children Of Khan apparel buyers, not course students — speculation).
  • Traffic (profile data): United States 27%, United Kingdom 10%, Brazil 9%; social 52.3% (Instagram, Facebook), paid social 30.3%, search 10.5% (brand keywords); two months ago 519 → last month 6,030.

Where the first users came from: Children Of Khan's own AI short-video audience (TikTok/IG/FB), via course links in the bio and posts; the homepage testimonial mentions a "first cohort", so it was first sold to followers as a small limited-seat cohort, then converted into a recorded course to scale.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • The main account keeps producing viral AI videos (self-reported 1B+ views) → course link in the bio → course site (FB bio, social traffic 52%).
  • Paid Meta campaigns: paid social 30.3% (profile); Brandsearch logs 388 Meta ads, some landing on the course site.
  • Student case study (Jake Walsh, 10M views) used as social proof.

Speculation:

  • Students making AI videos with the same method may be encouraged to credit the source, creating second-order spread; no evidence.
  • The course and the apparel store share an audience and ad account; the course is a high-margin second monetization layer.

Revenue and scale clues

  • $197 × unknown student count; if the site's "13K+ customers" were course students that would be about $2.5M, but they are more likely apparel buyers (speculation), so the figure cannot be used directly.
  • Brandsearch estimates the Children Of Khan main site at 463K monthly visits and $1.2M–$2.2M monthly revenue (third-party estimate, unverified).
  • The course site itself gets about 6K monthly visits (2026-09).

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. Most search results were the "Elite Ball Knowledge" sports meme and a same-named mini-game, unrelated to this site; used the current course page, three Wayback versions, RDAP, Brandsearch and the Children Of Khan Facebook snippet.
  • Gaps: the founder's real name and background, actual course sales, how the Children Of Khan account took off (when, and which video went viral).
