Basics

  • Product: DiskBuddy, a native Mac app (macOS 14+), later joined by a Windows 10/11 version. One scan, 8 ways to visualize disk usage; finds duplicate files, shows real app sizes, compresses videos and photos, and deletion only moves items to the Trash. One license covers both platforms. The site claims a scan of about 3 million files takes 20 seconds on Apple silicon.
  • Developer: Mohd Danish (India), company MVP STACK LLC, solo. No funding.
  • Pricing: one-time purchase. Price history: launch price $39 limited to 400 copies → $19 (crossed out $49) → now $9 (crossed out $49, "goes up to $49 once 400 copies sell out").

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • The author launched by building in public on X; social traffic (49%) comes entirely from X.
  • Buyers post their purchases on X, and the site embeds those posts directly as social proof.
  • A limited early-bird price creates scarcity, with repeated price cuts.
  • Revenue is published on TrustMRR, drawing attention from the open-revenue crowd.
  • Speculation:
  • The first buyers were mainly the author's indie-developer followers and mutuals, partly out of goodwill.
  • The product has no sharing or invite mechanism, so once launch buzz passed there was no new acquisition source, hence only $321 in the last 30 days.
  • The "37 left" counter does not match revenue: at historical prices, $2,248 is roughly one to two hundred copies, so the counter may be a static marketing number.

Revenue and scale clues

  • TrustMRR: $2,248 lifetime, $321 in the last 30 days, no subscriptions (one-time purchase).
  • Buyer count on the order of one to two hundred people (inferred from the $9–$39 price range).

Confidence and gaps

  • Revenue comes from TrustMRR (Stripe-verified), so credibility is fairly high.
  • The author's follower count and engagement on the launch posts were not obtained (X pages unreadable). Search results were all noise; no Reddit or Product Hunt records.
  • Our data shows 8,494 three months ago, which predates the domain registration; the series is identical to usecutai.com and listadum.com, so it is a data-source filler value and not trusted.
