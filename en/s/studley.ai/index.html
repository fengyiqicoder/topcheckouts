It first blew up on the founder's own TikTok parody videos (one with 49.4 million views, another with about 3 million that brought the first users), then scaled that playbook into a "StudyTok ambassador army": students are paid to open 110+ unbranded new TikTok accounts, $20 per video plus up to a $2,000 view bonus, using a report-card slideshow format of "failed the exam, used ChatGPT / aced it, used Studley" to rack up 150 million views. Basics

  • Product: upload any material (PDFs, lecture recordings, YouTube videos and so on) to generate notes, flashcards, quizzes, AI podcasts and mind maps, plus homework solving and chat. Web, iOS, Android.
  • Company: Studley AI Inc., headquartered in Nova Scotia, Canada (Trailblazer case study). Founder Emran Hassan (the Trailblazer case study quotes him directly); the team describes itself as students "building it in dorm rooms and late-night Discord calls". Funding not found.
  • Pricing: free tier with weekly limits; Unlimited $14.99/month, or $8.17/month equivalent when billed annually.

First users: the founder's own viral TikTok videos (evidence; Trailblazer says "growth initially came from one video with about 3 million views"). Source of new users: a paid student-ambassador TikTok matrix (evidence), with SEO and competitor-term search layered on from the second half of 2025 (evidence; the founder says "it now mainly comes from organic search").

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • Paid UGC matrix: students open new accounts with no links and no brand name so it looks like organic content; pay per video plus a view bonus; the team tests formats, copies hits and replicates in bulk (report-card slideshows, cynical-motivation study content, faceless study vlogs).
  • SEO/AI search: landing pages for competitor terms, category terms and subject terms, with backlinks grown from DR 5 to 21.
  • The app onboarding "how did you hear about us" options are, in order, TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, friends and family, search; the site carries a Google Ads conversion tag.
  • Study sets and folders can be shared by link.
  • Speculation: classmates sharing study sets spreads it within classes; the Philippine traffic comes from local student creators or cheap ad buys.

Revenue and scale clues

  • MRR: about $20K (before SEO) → $50K (2025-11, SGE headline) → $100K-150K+ (2026 Trailblazer case study, the agency's own claim, figures inconsistent).
  • Users: 2 million (2026-03), 4 million (self-claimed 2026-10).
  • Our data: average monthly checkout-page visits of 36,233 in April-June, 12,432 in July, 35,626 in August, with clear school-term seasonality.

Confidence and gaps

  • The MRR figures come from marketing agencies and third-party growth blogs, with mutually contradictory bases; the founder's name appears only in the Trailblazer case study. Whether the founder's TikTok parody video predates the product launch, and what it contained, is unverified.
  • Second-pass check: the takeoff method was changed from "New-platform window" to "Creator or press spark" (evidence: the founder's viral TikTok brought the first users), the growth engine from "Paid ads + Search ranking" to "Creator supply" (the paid student TikTok matrix is the main force), and pure product spread from "Mixed" to "No"; added founder Emran Hassan, the Nova Scotia headquarters, the creator program terms, view counts, MRR and the SEO second curve.
