In the second half of 2026 it launched 16 languages and about 1,770 pages (blog tutorials for each country's carriers); nearly 60% of traffic is paid advertising, plus a bit of "airtime top up" type search. Basics

  • Product: remote top-up for prepaid numbers across 42 countries and 230+ carriers (airtime, data, voice/SMS bundles), done in three steps; also prepaid SIM and eSIM/data pages.
  • Who built it: the About page says CODIXERA LIMITED handles "user access, payments and customer service", with airtime supplied by Ding. No company of that name found in UK Companies House (may be registered in another jurisdiction). Founder not found.
  • Pricing: per top-up $5/$10/$20/$50 or €5-€50 (profile data); revenue is the top-up margin/fee.
  • Domain: Verisign RDAP registration 2007-05-01, last changed 2026-08-17; Wayback snapshots from 2001-2006 show a Chinese mobile ringtone/wallpaper site (the literal meaning of "mobilerings"), 404 in 2023-06, no content in 2025-07, so presumably taken over by the current operator in mid-2026.
  • Traffic (profile data): Norway 16.5%, United Kingdom 16.3%, France 13.2%; paid 58.8%, search 21.6%, direct 11.8%; non-brand search terms "top up international phone", "buy airtime online", "airtime top up"; referrals show booking.com 6.9%, thepointsguy.com 6.4%, hostelworld.com 5.7% (weakly related to top-ups, possibly ad network attribution or data noise).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Mainly paid ads.
  • Multilingual programmatic SEO: one set of "carrier name + how to top up" tutorial pages per language, covering Lyca, Lebara, Jio, Vodafone, etc.
  • Aged domain reuse (2007 domain).

Speculation:

  • Ads target countries with large immigrant populations in Europe (Norway, UK, France), with language pages matching immigrants' native languages.
  • No evidence of repeat-purchase incentives or referral mechanisms; growth basically depends on ad ROI.

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public figures; revenue from top-up fees/margins, order value $5-$50.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Search results were almost entirely irrelevant (dictionaries, space launches, YouTube homepage), with only the site's own About and blog pages; evidence mainly from the site's About page, sitemap, RDAP, Wayback.
  • Operator background, exact launch date, and revenue all not found; the anomalous referral sources (booking.com etc.) could not be explained.
