In 2011 a small team in Kemnath, a small town in Bavaria, Germany, built an "Amazon price tracker" web app, and the key step was turning it into a browser extension that inserts the price history curve straight into Amazon product pages. Amazon's daily shopping traffic became Keepa's display slot: people with the extension use it every time they browse Amazon, and when posting in deals communities (mydealz, chollometro, slickdeals and the like) they screenshot the Keepa curve to prove "it's a real price drop", which brings in new users. It covers more than ten Amazon sites beyond camelcamelcamel (Germany, Japan, Italy, Spain, India and more), and is especially strong in Europe and Japan. Free for consumers, it built up 5M extension users and a full price dataset, then sells that data monthly to Amazon sellers (Keepa Pro/Data, €19 → €29/month) and to API customers, which is the part that makes money.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: Amazon price history charts, price-drop and back-in-stock alerts, daily deals (Deals), browser extensions (Chrome, Firefox and others) and a mobile app; paid Keepa Pro (Product Finder for product research, Product Viewer for bulk export, more charts and alerts) at €29/month, plus an API billed by tokens. Covers.com,.co.uk,.de,.co.jp,.fr,.ca,.it,.es,.in,.mx and more, claiming 7B+ products. The free version needs no sign-up.
  • Company: Keepa GmbH, Kemnath, Germany (Berndorfer Str. 10), Weiden district court HRB 5942; North Data shows registry notices since 2017, managing directors Sascha Arthur and Julian Johann, share capital around €25,000, with annual reports for 2018-2024 (figures need a paid account).
  • Scale: Chrome extension about 5M users, 5,100 ratings at 3.4 stars; third-party estimate of about 7M monthly visits (milonic, summary only).
  • Traffic (our records): direct 76.7%, search 17.4%, brand keywords 80.2%; United States 28.9%, Japan 16.5%, Italy 7.5%; referrals include the Spanish deals community chollometro.com 5.8%, amazon.in 3.9% and the Canadian consumer magazine protegez-vous.ca 3.8%.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Piggybacking on Amazon: the extension embeds the price curve in Amazon product pages, so users see Keepa every time they shop on Amazon, and usage frequency is set by Amazon's traffic; 5M extension users.
  • Deals communities: the top referral source in our data is the Spanish deals community chollometro (5.8%), and the consumer magazine protegez-vous.ca also recommends it.
  • Multi-site coverage: supports more than ten Amazon sites, and Japan and Italy account for a much higher share of traffic than for a typical US tool.
  • Free, no sign-up: lowers the trial barrier; direct visits 76.7% and brand keywords 80.2% show users return through the extension and bookmarks.
  • Free for consumers, paid for sellers: free users' tracking requests keep widening data coverage, and the data is then sold to sellers (Pro, API).

Speculation:

  • The first users may have come from the German deals community mydealz and Amazon seller forums; the developers are German and the German Amazon site was the early mainstay; no launch post was found.
  • Pasting Keepa curve screenshots (with a Keepa watermark on the image) into deal posts as proof of a price drop is a long-running passive source of spread (from memory, watermark details unverified).
  • Cutting the free quota and raising prices in 2026 is a shift from "building users" to "harvesting sellers", which may hurt consumer reputation (Trustpilot 1.6 stars).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Chrome extension about 5M users, third-party estimate of about 7M monthly visits.
  • Paid: Pro €29/month (about €19 before the 2026 price rise), API charged separately; in the seller-tools market Keepa data is widely cited by Helium 10, SellerAmp and others, and API customers are probably numerous (speculation).
  • If 20K-50K paying sellers x €25/month, annual revenue would be about €6M-€15M (pure speculation); the German annual report figures need a paid account.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch: there was no old report in the notes. Search results were mostly alternatives roundups and scam-checker sites; the work relied mainly on 2011 Wayback snapshots, the Chrome store, Trustpilot and North Data (German commercial register).
  • The launch timing (mid-2011 to September) and extension scale are credible; there is no first-hand evidence for early acquisition channels.
  • Gaps: founder interviews, the launch channel, paying-user count, annual report revenue.
