A solo mini-site registered on 2026-08-25 and launched about a week later: search any city on a 3D globe, pick a bomb (Little Boy, Tsar Bomba, W87, etc.) and a burst height, and watch the fireball, blast rings and WorldPop-based casualty estimates. It looks far better than the venerable NUKEMAP, and screenshots and recordings of "dropping a nuke on my own city" are naturally shareable. In early last month it got reposted across X, Threads and Facebook; WorldStar reshared a screen recording under the headline "new nuke simulator supposedly built from a single AI prompt has people wildin" (about 69K views), and from there it spread to the Korean community aagag.com, Spanish-language tech blog microsiervos, Brazil and elsewhere. Every simulation result is a share link carrying coordinates and weapon parameters (?lat=…&weapon=…), so each click is a new visit. The author then added SEO landing pages for cities, weapons and nuclear test sites to catch long-tail search, and monetizes with weekly/monthly/quarterly Pro passes sold via Polar (unlocking photorealistic 3D cities).One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: in-browser 3D globe nuclear blast simulation (Three.js; place search via OpenStreetMap/Komoot Photon; Pro uses Google photorealistic 3D tiles; population from WorldPop 2020), showing blast wave, thermal radiation, simplified fallout and casualty estimates; has a leaderboard API (/api/leaderboard); city pages /cities/, weapon pages /weapons/, test site pages /sites/, About, FAQ (all updated 2026-09-13).
  • Monetization: core free; Pro is a one-week / one-month / three-month time-limited pass, with Polar as merchant of record; prices not listed on the page.
  • Author: unnamed on the site, only support@nukesimulation.com. X user @Atamol_rc (bio: Peking University physics, reverse engineer, based in Hokkaido, Japan, 466 followers) posted "today's nukesimulation.com update: realistic, accurate mushroom clouds generated from each weapon's own parameters" (summary only), very likely the author or a core developer; unverified.
  • Domain: registered 2026-08-25 (RDAP); first Wayback snapshot 2026-09-02.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Repost/press spark: the WorldStar screen recording got 69K views; referral sources concentrate on Korea's aagag.com (an aggregator of hot posts from major communities), Threads and the Spanish-language tech blog microsiervos.
  • Branded output: every simulation generates a shareable parameter link (Wayback archived links like?lat=…&weapon=tsar-bomba), and screenshots/recordings show the site's interface.
  • Long-tail search: about 10 days after launch it mass-produced city, weapon and test-site landing pages; brand keywords currently make up 84.6% of the 46.3% search share, meaning most people hear about it and then search the name.

Speculation:

  • The first 1,000 users most likely came from "nuking my city" recordings posted on X/Threads by the author or early users, then amplified by WorldStar and Korean community reposts; no HN or Product Hunt record (0 results on HN Algolia).
  • Traffic will rise and fall with nuclear-war-related news (our profile notes it "often floods in around news events").
  • The leaderboard and Pro photorealistic 3D cities exist to turn one-off gawking into return visits and payments.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Pro pass price not public; revenue unknown.
  • Scale clues from the spike: a single WorldStar post with 69K views; our channel data shows traffic scattered across many countries.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch: no earlier report in the notes. Search results were polluted by similar competitors (nukesimulator.com, etc.) and rocket-launch sites; useful evidence comes from RDAP, Wayback CDX, the site's About/FAQ and the WorldStar page.
  • Launch timing (registered 08-25, first snapshot 09-02, spike around 09-04) is credible.
  • Gaps: author identity (@Atamol_rc inferred from a single tweet summary, unverified); original source and metrics of the first viral post; the "built from one AI prompt" claim has no source; Pro price and revenue.
