Basics

  • Product: Delivrd, a personal car-buying concierge. A dedicated person negotiates prices with dealers for the buyer, handles paperwork with remote signing, arranges test drives and delivery to your door, and also does out-of-area purchases and trade-ins; tagline "Skip the Dealership, Not the Deal". Source: https://www.delivrdto.me/
  • Pricing: new car "Insta-Pay Deal Negotiation" $1,000 (work starts 3–5 business days after payment); there is also a "Personalized Deal Negotiation" that requires a consultation first, price not listed; the homepage offers a free consultation. Source: https://www.delivrdto.me/insta-pay-service
  • Who runs it: founder Tomi Mikula. The About page says he was previously a "top 1%" auto finance manager in the US who long recruited and trained salespeople. The team page lists 22 people (director of operations, PR manager, a dozen-plus Deal Coordinators/Negotiators, design and architecture). The Facebook page's location is Charlotte, North Carolina. Sources: https://www.delivrdto.me/about-us, https://www.delivrdto.me/team
  • Launch timing: delivrdto.me registered 2023-03-15 (.me WHOIS); first Wayback capture 2023-10-31.
  • Traffic (profile data): brand keywords 77.7% (delivrd, tomi mikula, delivrd reviews), non-brand 11%; social 6.8% from YouTube; direct 33.8%; United States 92.3%, Canada 4.2%, United Kingdom 1.9%.

Where the first users came from: Reddit. The founding story is that in 2023 he negotiated prices for Reddit users for free (only a Facebook group summary; the original Reddit post was not found), building up cases and word of mouth first; he then used TikTok short videos (recording the haggling with dealers, exposing dealer tricks) to turn viewers into paying customers. Brand keywords make up nearly 80% of site traffic and social comes from YouTube, which shows new users mainly watched the content and then searched "delivrd / tomi mikula" to reach the site.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Founder content as the traffic source: TikTok 765K followers, and a weekly YouTube live that shows "this week's wins"; every negotiation is itself material for the next video.
  • Media amplification: WSJ, GMA and Entrepreneur covered him with angles like "the dealers' worst nightmare / $200K a month", bringing a new round of brand searches.
  • Social proof: 652 Google reviews, lots of five-star testimonials on the site, and the Facebook group "Delivrd Car Buying Tips" as a community.
  • Free consultation → $1,000 flat fee lowers the decision barrier.

Speculation:

  • The conflict angle of "dealers hate him" gets industry podcasts and dealer circles talking too, which in effect gives him more exposure.
  • Satisfied customers show off the money they saved on social media, creating word of mouth (speculation; no specific posts were found).

Revenue and scale clues

  • Self-reported 5,000+ deals × $1,000 ≈ cumulative revenue in the $5M range (speculation, calculated from unit price); the Entrepreneur headline says $200K a month (self-reported, not independently verified). A team of 22 fits this scale.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Google/Bing results include TikTok, YouTube and Facebook summaries; the evidence mainly comes from the site's About/Team/Press/pricing pages and.me WHOIS. The social pages and the original Facebook group posts could not be fetched, so only summaries were used.
  • All media coverage is relayed from the site's press page, with no original links or dates (except GMA 2026-03); the Reddit origin details and the specific video and date when TikTok took off were not found; the "takeoff year 2024" is speculation. Wayback was temporarily down during the investigation, so the 2023–2024 site snapshots could not be viewed.
