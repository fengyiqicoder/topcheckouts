Small Tokyo company 株式会社インターン (representative 半澤洋) started in 2018-11 with a sold-out 80-person "Tohoku Night" regional ingredients and sake collaboration event in a dining bar. From 2019 to 2021 it ran a regional food gathering for a different prefecture every month or two (Hiroshima, Nagano, Hokkaido, Okinawa, Yamanashi, Fukushima, Kochi); in 2022 it switched to the "ふるてらマルシェ" market format, and in 2023-03 moved to the Okachimachi Panda Plaza in front of JR Okachimachi Station for a 4-day open-air "Tohoku Local Sake Festival" that drew 20,453 visitors. Since then it has grown on prefecture/brewery exhibitors, natural foot traffic at the station plaza, ward government backing, plus traffic from sake event calendar sites and PR TIMES press releases; the 2026 summer sake festival drew about 35,000 people over 3 days. The site is an event information page, and visits pulse with event months.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: urban outdoor events themed on regional food + sake (free admission; local sake via a ticket system, food paid on site; Makuake pre-sales of a "full enjoyment set": 8 sake tickets + a 500 yen food ticket). The site posts events, vendor/brewery information, vendor recruitment and volunteer recruitment (students from high school age up welcome).
  • Who made it: 株式会社インターン, representative 半澤洋 (official site, tokyofesta summary); a sake calendar summary says it is run mainly by people in their 20s and 30s.
  • Launch date: WHOIS created 2020-12-06; first Wayback capture 2021-04-19; the earliest post on the site is dated 2018-11-19 (the first event, back-filled).
  • Traffic (profile file): roughly 3,920 visits in 2026-09 (event month; the preceding months were 0); referrals 50% (sake event calendars such as nihonshucalendar.com and sakebana.jp); search 33%, almost all non-brand event terms (秋酒まつり, 日本酒フェス 錦糸町, 錦糸公園 イベント); Japan 100%.

Where the first users came from: the first event was a small 80-person gathering at a dining bar, with attendees presumably regulars of the two venues and the organizers' personal network; the 2019-2021 prefecture-themed events presumably drew on partner municipalities/regional producers and the venues' customer base. No finer-grained evidence on the first cohort was found.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Exhibitors (prefectural breweries, regional food shops) are the supply, and events recruit vendors (vendor recruitment on the site, official LINE).
  • Sake event calendar sites (nihonshucalendar, tokyo-sake-calendar, sakebana) and event aggregators (tokyofesta, event.navy, jk-tokyo.tv) send traffic, accounting for half of the site's referrals (profile file).
  • PR TIMES press releases, SAKETIMES media releases, Makuake pre-sales (the site's news-prtimes series, SAKETIMES article).
  • A results report (visitor count, satisfaction) after every event is used to recruit the next round of exhibitors.

Speculation:

  • Choosing a plaza in front of a JR station or a park lets passing foot traffic + free admission inflate visitor counts, which then persuade municipalities and breweries to exhibit, forming a "visitors -> exhibitors -> more visitors" loop.
  • Student volunteers reduce labor costs while spreading the event into student circles.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Revenue from exhibitor fees, tickets (sake tickets), Makuake pre-sales, and possibly municipal commission fees (speculation).
  • Scale: 20,453 visitors over 4 days in 2023-03; about 35,000 over 3 days in 2026-07.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Evidence comes from the site's sitemap and results posts, WHOIS, Wayback, the SAKETIMES press release and event aggregator summaries.
  • Company size, revenue and the specific form of municipal backing were not found; the claim that the team skews young comes from a single calendar-site summary.
