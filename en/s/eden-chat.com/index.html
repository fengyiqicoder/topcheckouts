Basics

  • Product: an AI character chat/role-play platform. Users create characters and "works" (story settings with multiple illustrations) and publish them publicly; others browse by ranking, hall of fame and genre, then start chatting; the model can be switched (reviews mention GLM, DeepSeek, Gemini and others, billed by points); paid features include long-term memory, chat admins and output correction; the safety filter can be turned off (adult content).
  • Payment: prepaid points (deducted by token usage), payments processed in Korean won and converted at the Korea Eximbank exchange rate; promotions such as a "Hangul Day 10% discount"; new users who enter an invite code within 24 hours get 1,000 points (on-site copy).
  • Operator: Eden Creative Studio, LLC, 1111B S Governors Ave STE 29965, Dover, DE (homepage footer). The actual team is in Korea (won payments, Korean holiday discounts, a creator who is Korean per a Bahamut author). A Bahamut comment says the predecessor was called EveChat (unverified).
  • Domain: registered 2025-10-31 (RDAP); the eden-chat.com in Wayback from 2002-2003 is an unrelated old site with the same name.
  • Tech: Next.js + Supabase, hosted on Vercel.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Invite codes give points: the site has a "friend invite" feature and ready-made share copy (with the #R18 tag and an invite link?utmsource=ref), and most Taiwan promo posts follow this template.
  • Creator supply + tipping: users publish illustrated works, with rankings, a hall of fame, "buy me a coffee" tips and comment events (chat a set number of times + write a review to earn points), encouraging creators to keep producing and players to leave reviews.
  • Adult-circle referrals: Korean adult/anime sites such as arca.live, hitomi.la and dlsite bring referral traffic.
  • Brand search: direct 70%, brand keywords 79% of search; returning Korean users come back by remembering the name.

Speculation:

  • The first users were users and creators migrating from existing Korean AI character chat platforms (similar competitors or the rumored predecessor EveChat); no direct evidence.
  • The last month traffic crash may be related to Korean local regulation/payments/competitors, after which it turned to Taiwan and Japan for incremental growth; cause Not found.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Prepaid points, deducted by model and token usage; Bahamut comments generally find it expensive and say prices are rising.
  • Scale and revenue Not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The company, payments and invite mechanism come from the homepage HTML itself, credible; "Korean team" is a judgment combining won payments, Hangul Day discount, our data and the Bahamut author's description.
  • Search results had no Korean-language material, so the Korean-phase founding story is basically blank; Wayback was temporarily offline, so no 2025-2026 snapshots were obtained.
  • Gaps: the Korean launch channel and timing; team identity; why traffic crashed last month; paying users and revenue.
