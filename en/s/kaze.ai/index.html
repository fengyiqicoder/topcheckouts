Basics

  • Product: an online AI image/video generation and editing tool: watermark removal, logo removal, AI ID photos, old photo restoration/colorization, outpainting, white-background images, conversational editing (Chat Edit), body reshaping, AI short clip/TikTok video generation and more; integrates video models such as MiniMax (X post summary).
  • Pricing: monthly/annual subscriptions, credit tiers of 200/500/1,200 per month (profile file).
  • Who built it: the site has no About page (/about returns 404); LinkedIn says 2-10 people (and elsewhere 11 employees), with no address or founding year. Referral traffic comes from ahhhhfs.com (a Chinese resource blog), feishu.cn (Lark) and ailabtools.com, so it is probably a Chinese team going overseas (speculation).
  • Launch: kaze.ai was registered on 2020-02-27, but in 2021 it was just a default nginx page, and in 2024-08 it was listed for sale on Dan.com; RDAP shows a transfer on 2024-11-16 (i.e. bought by the current team); Wayback on 2025-01-01 already shows "AI-powered Free Online Removing Watermark and Logos Tool".
  • Current traffic (profile file): United States 18%, Russia 5.1%, Guatemala 4.2% (very scattered, consistent with multilingual SEO); search 61.5%, about 37% non-brand, with terms like "png maker", "bald filter", "remove logo from photo"; direct 38.5%; social X, Reddit, Pinterest, and a Discord.

Since the starting product was a "free online watermark remover" with titles written entirely around search terms, the first users were probably long-tail searchers who came in searching "remove watermark / watermark remover"; the referral from the Chinese resource blog ahhhhfs.com shows it has also been recommended by Chinese "useful tools" blogs (speculation).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Programmatic SEO: 259 single-function tool pages x 13 languages, about 37% non-brand search, with terms for specific small functions (sitemap, profile file).
  • Posts on X following new video model launches and a creator program (X summary, LinkedIn).

Speculation:

  • Free trial → logging in gives more credits (the /my-projects page says "log in to get more free credits") → subscription conversion.
  • Images users export carry no brand that sends traffic back, so spread comes mainly from search rather than the product itself.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Credit subscriptions in 200/500/1,200 tiers; prices and revenue not found. Team of 2-11 people (LinkedIn).
  • No traffic numbers found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Search results were mostly unrelated AI rankings and same-name sites (kazeai.art and kaze-ai.vercel.app are not it); the evidence comes mainly from RDAP, Wayback (including the Dan.com listing page), the sitemap and LinkedIn.
  • The team's identity, the source of the first users and revenue are blank; "Chinese team" is only inferred from referral traffic in Chinese-speaking circles.
