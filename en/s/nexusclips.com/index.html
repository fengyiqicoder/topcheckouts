Basics

  • Product: AI cuts livestreams, podcasts and long videos into vertical shorts with animated captions, with scheduled posting to TikTok/Reels/Shorts; connects to Twitch/YouTube. Free tier; Pro $12.49/month or $59.99/year (profile data).
  • Who is behind it: a company in Barcelona, Spain; co-founders Albert Osuna Vera (CEO) and Mohamed Said Benmousa (CTO), COO Randy Esperben; founded 2019; 4 employees (Grokipedia citing PitchBook/CB Insights).
  • Funding: equity crowdfunding round on 2021-09-21; early-stage VC of $621K on 2023-09-29; roughly $600K–$751K in total (the two databases differ); investors/accelerators Draper B1, 4Founders Capital, Lanzadera, Conector Startup Accelerator (Grokipedia, not individually verified).
  • Launch: domain registered 2021-01-30 (RDAP, GoDaddy); earliest Wayback capture 2021-02-08.
  • Current traffic (profile data): United States 40%, Brazil 7%, India 5%; direct 59.6%, search 32.3% (brand keywords 91.9%), social 8.1% (YouTube, TikTok).

Where the first users came from: the Spanish-speaking Twitch streamer scene. The 2022 homepage listed a group of Spanish-speaking streamers as user endorsements and used an Ibai Twitch clip as the example URL; a free tool where you just paste a Twitch link to get a vertical cut lowered the barrier to try. Trustpilot reviewers also write that they "signed up because several streamers recommended it".

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Streamer endorsements/recommendations: the homepage has long displayed a list of Spanish-speaking streamers; reviews mention being "recommended by multiple streamers" (Trustpilot).
  • Free editor + Pro removes the watermark (2023 homepage: "Editor Premium… sin marca de agua"), meaning free-tier clips carry a watermark.
  • From 2025, expansion through English long-tail SEO pages; search is 32% (but 92% brand keywords).
  • YouTube tutorial videos (e.g. "How To Create SHORTS in MINUTES using AI | Nexus Clips Tutorial").

Speculation:

  • Watermarked vertical clips posted to TikTok/Shorts get seen by other streamers → sign-ups, forming a branded-output loop.
  • Streamer recommendations very likely involve affiliate commissions or sponsorship, but the site FAQ does not mention an affiliate program; unconfirmed.
  • "200K+ creators" is probably a sign-up count; paying users are far fewer.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Self-reported 200K+ creators (homepage, 2024-06).
  • 4 employees, "generating revenue" (via PitchBook); total funding under $1M.
  • Pro annual plan $59.99; the volume of annual-renewal complaints on Trustpilot suggests annual plans are the main revenue source.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch for a new site. Many search results were unrelated (adult clip sites, launch tracking); used Wayback-archived homepages (5 versions, 2021–2025), RDAP, Grokipedia and Trustpilot. PitchBook returned 403 and was not opened; funding figures are via Grokipedia.
  • Gaps: whether streamer partnerships are paid, whether an affiliate program exists, paying user count and revenue, the specific 2022 takeoff event (which streamer set it off).

How did Nexusclips get started?

A game-recording tool from a small Barcelona team. In 2021 it pivoted from "press CTRL+8 on Windows to save a gaming highlight" to "turn Twitch streams into vertical TikToks", and grew with endorsements from the Spanish-speaking Twitch streamer scene (Tanizen, Recuerdop, Duodedos, D3stri, etc.) plus a free editor; watermarked vertical clips from the free tier posted to TikTok/Shorts pulled in new streamers. In 2024 it rode the AI-clipping wave, rebranding as "AI long-video clipping" and claiming 200K+ creators; in 2025 it switched to an English site and rolled out long-tail landing pages like "twitch clips for tiktok" to go global.
