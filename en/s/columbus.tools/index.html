Source: Wayback snapshot of the site from 2026-10-17.

Where the first users came from: the Chinese "AI tool sites for overseas markets" crowd on X. From launch day through week 6, at least 5 KOLs in that crowd (10k–70k followers) tweeted about it, 3 of them with?aff= referral codes, and the site's homepage "testimonials" section embeds those 5 tweets directly. The author's own account has only about 1,600 followers, so the cold start relied mainly on these KOLs relaying it.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Referral commissions: the?aff=DROIDHZ / JASONZHU / MEEPO codes in KOL tweets all say "30% off", showing the referral program existed at launch.
  • High-frequency founder updates: the official account posts "ranking update / new feature / new model discovery" almost daily, 500–12,000 views per post.
  • WeChat group with subscription: subscribers join the "Twitter Daily VIP group" and aren't removed when they lapse, using community to boost retention and word of mouth.
  • Public reports for traffic: /market-report publishes competitor negative-review breakdowns and ranking analyses without login, then the official account and KOLs reshare them.
  • Lower payment friction: Alipay, one-time pass.

Speculation:

  • The "wealth code" narrative fits the anxiety and curiosity of the Chinese go-global crowd well, so KOLs are willing to share voluntarily; the product itself is content material for KOLs.
  • Site owners whose backlinks are submitted or who get listed come to check their own rankings (outbound links in the backlink ranking carry utm_source=columbus.tools), giving some passive spread.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Official account: 2k+ UV in 3 days after launch (2026-08-06).
  • @meepodev's tweet says "$10k a month" (2026-09-08, third-party claim, unverified).
  • Pricing $29/month, $199/year. In the traffic profile, the 28% social share comes almost entirely from X.
  • No funding seen.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site written from scratch. Launch date, KOL tweets, and feature iterations all have original X posts (read via fxtwitter API) and a Wayback homepage snapshot; medium-high credibility.
  • Search results were all unrelated historical "Columbus" pages and weren't used; the live site is blocked by Cloudflare, so the Wayback snapshot from 2026-10-17 was used.
  • Gaps: the author's real identity, paying user count, and referral commission rate are all not found; can't tell whether the KOL tweets were paid partnerships or voluntary recommendations.
