Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Paid ads are the main channel (68% in our data), concentrated on the US.
  • Launches on new-product directories: ProductCool (2026-07), BetaList (2026-08), small in scale.
  • Search capture: tool landing pages such as ai-manga-generator; search terms include ai manga maker, manga generator and pixai (a competitor term).
  • Retention design: a daily check-in reward calendar, a credit system, and a public gallery to come back and see others' works.

Speculation:

  • The public gallery lets users' works be shared with on-site links, possibly forming a small "watch works -> make your own" loop, but there is no sharing data as evidence.
  • The Philippines at 6.5% of traffic may come from targeted ads or anime communities; not found.
  • No direct evidence on where the first 1,000 users came from; from the channel structure, speculated to be mainly bought through ads.

Revenue and scale clues

  • No public revenue data. Dual charging via subscriptions + gem top-ups; acquiring customers through paid ads suggests revenue per user covers the acquisition cost (speculation).
  • ProductCool has 55 upvotes, so the community is very small.

Confidence and gaps

  • A new site, written from scratch. Launch timing is inferred from RDAP, Wayback and directory listing dates, medium credibility; the channel structure comes from our data.
  • Most of the search results provided were irrelevant content like MakerVerse and Thingiverse; Reddit, the YouTube channel and store prices could not be fetched.
  • Gaps: founder/team, exact launch date, prices, ad platforms (Meta/TikTok/Google), revenue.
