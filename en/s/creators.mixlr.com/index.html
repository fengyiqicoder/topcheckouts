creators.mixlr.com is the streamer back end that London audio live-streaming platform Mixlr (founded 2010) split out in 2021-06; it is not itself the "takeoff" subject, and nearly all its traffic is existing streamers logging back in. The parent site Mixlr started in 2010 as a "DJ mixset distribution network", opened high-quality live streaming in 2010-12, and through the DJ/electronic music scene (Josh Wink, John Digweed, Sundae Philly, Space Ibiza), Ableton/Traktor streaming tutorials, independent radio stations and fan radio, turned every stream into a public listening page and embeddable player branded "on Mixlr", so streamers brought their own listeners in. By 2013 it had 1.7M users, and Obama's OFA used it for a "conference call" that broke a record with 140K listens. In 2017 it dropped the free Basic plan in favor of paid + trial and gradually shifted toward institutional customers with stable willingness to pay: churches, sports and schools.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: audio live-streaming SaaS; streamers go live with Mixlr Studio (Win/Mac) or Mixlr for Creators (iOS/Android, 50K+ Android downloads), listeners tune in and chat at mixlr.com/<channel> or via an embedded player; includes recordings/replays, events and analytics. creators.mixlr.com is the streamer-only workspace split out on 2021-06-10 (login, channel settings, recording management).
  • Who made it: Mixlr Ltd, London (the Obama blog post says "London-based, founded in 2010"); founder names not found in this research.
  • Launch date: mixlr.com domain registered 2010-03-14; Wayback 2010-04-13 homepage title "Mixlr | Mixset distribution network"; a 2010-12-02 blog post announced the live-streaming service opening to everyone; earliest Wayback capture of the creators subdomain is 2021-06-12.
  • Pricing (profile file): free tier available; Intermediate $49/month, Advanced $150/month, Enterprise from $199/month. In 2013 premium was $9.99/month (Obama blog post).
  • Traffic (profile file): the creators subdomain had roughly 3,989 visits in 2026-09; direct 49.5%, search 37.9%, brand keywords 87% (mixlr login/download/pricing); United States 33%, Indonesia 18%, Argentina 16%; industry category is religion and faith.

Where the first users came from: judging by the first version's positioning and the 2011 blog posts, DJs, electronic music parties and independent internet radio circles (Sundae Philly, Josh Wink, Space Ibiza, Bleep Radio, etc.), supported by DJ-software streaming tutorials; no figures for the first cohort were found.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Every stream has a public listening page/embeddable player, and streamers send the link to their own listeners -> listeners see the Mixlr brand (the "X is on Mixlr" page structure; many mixlr.com/<channel> pages in search results).
  • Big-customer stories create a demonstration effect: OFA/Obama, sports teams, university radio, churches (customers page).
  • Dropping the free tier in 2017 shifted growth toward paying institutional customers.

Speculation:

  • During COVID churches moved services online, and audio streaming has a low barrier, bringing church users in Indonesia, Argentina and elsewhere (the profile's industry category is religion and the country mix supports it, but there is no direct evidence).
  • Traffic on the creators subdomain is existing streamers logging in and does not represent new user acquisition.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 1.7M users and $9.99/month premium in 2013; plans at $49-$199+/month in 2026.
  • Claims 1.7B+ listeners and 22M+ live streams (customers/about pages, definition unverified).
  • Company funding and revenue not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Evidence comes from the official Mixlr blog (2010 launch, 2013 Obama event, 2017 free-tier removal, 2021 creators space), Wayback, WHOIS and the customers page.
  • Founders, funding and the timing of church customer growth were not found; "COVID drove church users" is speculation.
  • The classification follows the parent site Mixlr's origin story; the creators subdomain itself has no takeoff event.
