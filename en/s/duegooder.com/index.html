University of Louisville student Nate Royal failed every class his freshman year (he says he has ADHD) and noticed a classmate was charging money to 'turn syllabus deadlines into a spreadsheet'. So in late 2024 he built Due Gooder: upload a syllabus or connect an LMS like Canvas, AI extracts every assignment/exam date, syncs your calendar and reminds you daily. It launched on Product Hunt in January 2025 (#15 for the day), with 160 users, 75 paying and roughly $150 MRR in the first week. The first 300 users came from 'walking into sororities and asking people to download it'; printed flyers produced zero sign-ups. The real turning point was a single comment the co-founder left under a TikTok video, which brought thousands of sign-ups overnight. The team then bet all of its growth on TikTok UGC: 20 signed creators producing about 1,200 videos and 2.8 million views in a month, timed to back-to-school season. In January 2026 alone it added roughly 23,000 students, reaching 35,000 users across 4,000+ schools, with about 40% choosing annual plans.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: syllabus/LMS → AI deadline extraction → Google Calendar sync + SMS/push reminders + AI study assistant; V2 supports multiple LMSs, later added a parent view; iOS app (listed 2026-01-08, developer Nathan Royal).
  • Pricing: cheap early on, later raised to about $8.99/month (from an auto-transcribed podcast, figure may be off); about 40% of users pick annual; the TikTok bio carries the promo code 'Fall25'.
  • Team: CEO Nate Royal (UofL); Ethan Havertape (UofL, met at a hackathon, first to join); Finn McCormack (University of Kentucky, business); developers Ron Kommoji (Georgia Tech), Anh Hoang (University of Kansas, ML), Rishi Reshwant Borra (high school student); plus content creators and campus ambassadors. All students, enrolled in the UofL Sandbox program (startup work counts for credit).
  • Funding: Vogt Invention and Innovation Award from the Community Foundation of Louisville, $25k non-dilutive grant + 10-week accelerator; later another $50k prize and a SAFE-based pre-seed (per the podcast, Keyhorse-related, amount undisclosed).
  • Domain: WHOIS created 2024-10-30 (GoDaddy); Wayback captures from 2013–14 belong to a previous owner.
  • Traffic (profile): direct 49.6%, search 37.8% (brand keywords 87%: 'due gooder'), referral 7.6%; United States 78%, Canada 18%; social from YouTube, WhatsApp, Instagram.

Where the first users came from: (1) the Product Hunt launch (2025-01, 160 people in week one); (2) the founder pitching in person at University of Louisville sororities for the first 300; (3) the real volume came from the co-founder's comment under a TikTok video — thousands of sign-ups overnight — which made the team realize TikTok was the channel.

Where new users come from: a paid/incentivized TikTok UGC creator network (20 people, 1,200 videos a month) concentrated around semester starts (January, August–September); campus ambassadors; word of mouth among classmates (social sources include WhatsApp and Instagram); then users search the brand term 'due gooder' to reach the site (brand keywords 87%).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Creator supply: contracted UGC creators mass-produce videos (1,200/month, 2.8M views) across several official TikTok accounts (@due.gooder, @letsduegooder, @duegooderadmin).
  • Comment-section marketing: the co-founder commenting under relevant TikToks drove thousands of sign-ups overnight.
  • Back-to-school cadence: 23,000 new users in January alone.
  • Campus ambassadors and on-the-ground outreach (sororities).
  • 40% annual plans; daily push reminders throughout the semester sustain retention.

Speculation:

  • Students in the same class share one syllabus, so one user recommends it to classmates, spreading within each class.
  • Canada's 18% share suggests the TikTok content works across North America without any Canada-specific effort.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 2025-01: about $150 MRR (first week).
  • 2026-03: 35,000 users, 4,000+ schools; about $8.99/month, 40% annual; 2026 goal of $0.5–1M ARR.
  • 2026-10: TikTok bio claims trusted by 100k+ users (self-reported).
  • iOS rating 3.26 (47 reviews).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The origin story (300 sorority users, the TikTok comment spark, 1,200 UGC videos a month, 23,000 in January) has two sources — the Middle Tech podcast and LinkedIn posts — so credibility is high; the first-week 160 users / 75 paying figure comes only from grokipedia citing r/SaaS, original post not seen.
  • Gaps: the exact date and video of the 'TikTok comment spark'; the Keyhorse pre-seed amount; actual paying-user count; the Reddit r/PlannerAddicts thread 'Is Due Gooder worth it?' would not load.
