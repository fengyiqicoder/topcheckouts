The UK tax authority HMRC announced it would shut down its free online CT600 corporation tax filing service in 2026-03 (while Companies House was also tightening manual filings), so hundreds of thousands of single-director micro limited companies suddenly "had to buy commercial software". Serial entrepreneur Tim Fouracre (who previously founded the UK accounting software Clear Books and the business account Countingup) registered the domain in 2025-07 and launched a beta in 2025-11 as "free CT600 & accounts software, the alternative to HMRC's free service" to grab this forced-migration crowd. At the same time he used Companies House open data to generate a page for each of about 650K companies and wrote guides like "HMRC CT600 alternatives compared" and "best CT600 software 2026" to feed on search. In early 2026 it went from free to paid (from £20–£59/year, far cheaper than the £45–200 per filing charged by peers), and by 2026-03 claimed 5k+ filings and 293 Trustpilot reviews at 4.9 within a year.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: online filing for UK micro companies: iXBRL annual accounts to Companies House and CT600 to HMRC, later adding MTD VAT, annual confirmation statements and personal Self Assessment; can import from Xero.
  • Founder: Tim Fouracre (site footer links to his LinkedIn/X/personal site; the personal site lists TinyTax in a "Tiny" series alongside TinyReg and TinyCalc; previously founded Clear Books and Countingup and built many SEO experiment sites).
  • Pricing: Dormant £20/year, Solo £59/year, Portfolio from £99/year (up to 1,000 companies at £999/year); MTD VAT £29–£263/year; confirmation statement £74/year (including the £50 government fee). No free tier now (preparing and previewing is free).
  • Domain: registered 2025-07-21 (Nominet WHOIS); first Wayback snapshot 2025-10-27, the 2025-11-05 page reads "TinyTax beta · Free CT600 & Accounts Software".
  • Traffic: United Kingdom 88%; search 44% (brand 60%, non-brand such as "file confirmation statement" and "best accounting software"), direct 42%, paid search 8.4%; social is Reddit only.

Where the first 1,000 users came from: the evidenced driver is the "HMRC closing its free service" event; in the 2026-03 homepage reviews several directors say they went looking for software because the government was closing the service, and one Trustpilot user says they "found it through Google". Speculation: during the free beta (2025-11 to early 2026) the first users came via searches for "HMRC CT600 free alternative", discussions on Reddit r/UKPersonalFinance / r/smallbusinessuk and the like, and the founder's network in UK accounting circles; the traffic profile's only social source is Reddit.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Forced external migration: HMRC's free service closes, so target users must find a replacement before the deadline.
  • Programmatic SEO: about 650K Companies House company pages + comparison guides + landing pages for each filing type.
  • Free beta for word of mouth → then paid: nearly 300 positive Trustpilot reviews in a year (presumably with a review-invitation flow).
  • Paid search 8.4% (traffic profile).
  • Accountant/multi-company plans: one accountant brings in dozens or hundreds of companies.

Speculation:

  • Tax filing is an annual must-do, so renewal is high and returning users come back directly every filing season.
  • The founder has built many SEO experiment sites (per his personal site); this company-page matrix is the same playbook.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Claims 5k+ filings (2026-03); pricing page says 1,000+ companies have filed.
  • Roughly estimating 2,000–5,000 paying companies at an average of about £60/year gives annual revenue of about £120–300K; speculation.
  • Solo founder / very small team (no team information seen).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site written from scratch. Registration, launch, free-to-paid switch and founder identity have WHOIS/Wayback/personal-site evidence, fairly reliable.
  • Gaps: the original date of HMRC's closure announcement, beta-period user numbers, and whether the founder posted on Reddit are not found; only the first page of Trustpilot was read.
