Josh Feinsilber, a Seattle high school student, felt the Kahoot he played in class wasn't fun enough. In summer 2017 he built Gimkit, where you "answer questions to earn money and buy power-ups", ran a closed test with a small group of teachers last month, and launched on Product Hunt on October 30 (#5 of the day). He then cut teachers' prep cost with "one-click convert a Quizlet set into a game" (Fusion). Growth since has come almost entirely from one teacher starting a game and bringing in a whole class of 30 students, who then ask other teachers to use it when they go home or change classes, so it spread by organic word of mouth among US K-12 teachers. The team has always been small and unfunded.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a live classroom quiz game. The teacher creates a "kit" (question set) and starts a game, students join by entering a game code at gimkit.com/join, earn game coins for correct answers and buy upgrade items; later it added homework mode, several 2D game modes, the Gimkit Creative map editor, and seasons (Season Ticket $5).
  • Who built it: founder Josh Feinsilber (Founder, Developer & Owner), co-founder Jeff Osborn (Community Support); Gimkit Inc. was formed in 2017-10 and became an official company in 2018-06; because Josh was under 18, his father and Jeff Osborn held it on his behalf from 2017-10 to 2019-01-07 (gimkit.wiki). Headquartered in Seattle. The profile file says about 7 people, unfunded.
  • Predecessor: Gimkit Ink (a Google Classroom-like site, now closed); the product was originally called Gimlet and was renamed to avoid confusion with Gimlet Media (gimkit.wiki, PH comments).
  • Pricing: Basic is free (limited modes/players); Pro is $14.99/month or $59.88/year after a 14-day trial (OneLaunch blog, 2023).
  • Launch: RDAP registration 2017-11-22 (the new domain after the rename); PH launch 2017-10-30; earliest Wayback 2018-05-13.
  • Current traffic (profile file): United States 89%; direct 55.9%, search 35.6% (91.5% brand keywords, "gimkit join/login/host"); referrals from school platforms such as padlet, classlink and myschoolapp.

Where the first users came from: the founder said on PH that in 2017-09 he first tested with "a small group of teachers"; combined with the site's "made by high school students from Seattle", the first teachers were most likely teachers at the founder's own school and nearby (speculation, no list seen); the PH launch then brought the first wave of attention from the tech crowd (186 votes).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • One teacher starting a game = a class of 20-30 students signing up/joining; the bulk of search terms is "gimkit join", and direct traffic is 55.9% (profile file).
  • Fusion imports existing Quizlet sets in one click, so teachers can try it at zero cost (Wayback 2018 homepage).
  • Referral traffic from school platforms (padlet, classlink, myschoolapp) shows teachers embedding it in school portals (profile file).

Speculation:

  • Students like playing → they ask other teachers to "play Gimkit" → teachers tell each other by word of mouth, the typical spread pattern for Kahoot/Blooket-style products; the r/Teachers thread is supporting evidence.
  • Pandemic remote teaching in 2020 very likely brought a wave of growth, but no Gimkit figures were found.
  • Later, student-made wikis, Creative maps and the season pass turn students into active players, forming a second loop.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Pro $14.99/month or $59.88/year + season pass $5 (OneLaunch, gimkit.wiki).
  • The wiki says "millions of students and educators" use it, with no specific numbers; the profile says a team of about 7, unfunded. Revenue figures not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. Search results were mostly comparison/tutorial SEO articles; the useful sources were the PH launch page (founder's own account), gimkit.wiki, the Wayback 2018 homepage and CDX, and RDAP.
  • On the PH page the maker's name shows as "Josh Foster (@joshdotjs)", which disagrees with the wiki's Josh Feinsilber; it may be a PH account display name, not verified.
  • User counts, revenue and pandemic-period growth data were not obtained; Reddit posts would not open, so only summaries were used.
