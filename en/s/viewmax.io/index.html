Basics

  • Product: AI short-video/ad generation: Shorts Studio (replicates hit short-video formats), Wizard (one sentence → script + voiceover + footage + music + captions), Marketing Studio (clones hit ads/UGC), image and video generators, auto captions, voiceover, MCP (callable inside Claude/ChatGPT). Billed by credits (blog example: one 3D explainer short costs about $4).
  • Company: Viewmax LLC, dba Everygen (https://everygen.ai/terms, revised 2026-10-08). The blog author and YouTube presenter is Luc Boulch (https://everygen.ai/blog/how-to-actually-make-viral-ai-videos), suspected founder or core member, not verified. Posted a developer-hiring post in 2025-11 (stack Next.js/Supabase/Stripe/Remotion), showing a very small team. Country: LLC form plus US traffic being first, so Speculation: a US company. Funding: Not found.
  • Launch: WHOIS registered 2025-03-11; the first 200 Wayback snapshot is 2025-09-25; from 2026-10-02 viewmax.io 301-redirects (the rename).
  • Self-reports: early on it said "Trusted by 5,000+ creators", now says "used by creators with 1.3B+ views / billions of views".

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • Content marketing: the official YouTube channel @EverygenAI plus the blog, all on side-hustle topics like "how to make money with AI Shorts, AI clipping, AI ads", each post with copyable prompts and finished videos, leading straight to signup.
  • Template library: Viral Presets and the Prompt Library turn hit formats (CCTV, bodycam, nursery rhymes, Zack D. Films-style 3D explainers) into one-click templates.
  • Ecosystem hooks: used as an MCP/plugin inside Claude and ChatGPT.
  • Pricing has a roughly $49/month "creator workshop" tier, including Sora 2 Pro, 1080p and a live workshop (relayed from the Kompozy review, quotes differ between sources), i.e. a bundle of "tool + side-hustle course" (https://kompozy.io/reviews/viewmax-io).
  • Speculation:
  • The most likely origin path is the "make money with faceless AI Shorts" tutorial circle: official tutorials plus live workshops, with third-party creators doing reviews ("Legit or SCAM"); 85% of search is brand keywords, which shows users hear about it elsewhere (YouTube) first and then come to search. Evidence is not enough, so the takeoff method is still marked Not found.
  • The Shorts users publish carry no brand themselves (generally no watermark), so the product spreads itself weakly; growth mainly relies on recommendations from "side-hustle money" YouTube creators and affiliates (no public affiliate page found).
  • The 2–3 months ago peak may relate to new model integrations (new Seedance/Kling versions) or a big creator's review, not verified.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Claims 5,000+ creators (2025–26 figures).
  • Billed by credits, with subscriptions and auto top-up, credits valid for 90 days; the specific monthly fees were not captured.
  • MRR: Not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • Company entity, the rename and the timeline come from the site and Wayback, credible.
  • Second follow-up check: most of the 5 sets of search results were unrelated (the PH query returned only StackOverflow), and no launch post, founder interview or PH page was found; only added the manifesto's origin sentence, the $49 "creator workshop" tier, early-2026 negative feedback and third-party "is it a scam" review videos. The conclusion was not overturned.
