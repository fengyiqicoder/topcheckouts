Miconex of Perth, Scotland brought the "town general-purpose gift card" already proven in the UK (one card works at all participating businesses in the town; underneath it is a Mastercard prepaid card - from memory, not verified) to Ireland in November 2020, in the pandemic "save the high street" window. Rather than finding consumers itself, it signs agreements with each town's chamber of commerce, BID (Business Improvement District) and county council, and these local bodies recruit businesses, promote the cards and upgrade the old paper vouchers to cards. The real explosion came from corporate benefits: Ireland's small benefit tax exemption was raised to €1,500 in January 2025, and employers bulk-buy local gift cards for staff. Ireland sales reached €17.4 million in 2025 (+65% year over year), about 65% of it corporate orders, with Letterkenny alone above €9 million.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: general-purpose gift cards for Irish towns (physical and digital cards); buyers pick a town and a value, and buy online to be mailed or sent straight to the recipient; corporate bulk orders go through corporate.townandcitygiftcards.ie (bank transfer and identity verification for orders over €2,000).
  • Operator: Miconex Ireland (footer "Copyright © Miconex Ireland 2026"); parent company Miconex Ltd, Perth, Scotland, CEO Colin Munro. Miconex overall has 200+ towns/areas, 27,000+ merchants and £69 million+ cumulative local spend, covering the UK, Ireland, Canada and the US (https://www.mi-cnx.com/).
  • Irish programmes (2026-01): 16 - Ballinrobe, Carndonagh, Cavan, Drogheda, Donegal Town, Dungarvan & West Waterford, Kilkenny, Limerick, Letterkenny, Monaghan, Mullingar, Macroom, Sligo, Tipperary, Waterford, Wexford.
  • Pricing: value of your choice plus a small purchase fee (profile file); income comes from fees, project fees from chambers/BIDs and unredeemed balances (the last two are speculation).
  • Domain:.ie does not support RDAP lookups; first Wayback snapshot 2020-11-12 (the homepage still had placeholder text from Elgin in the UK, showing the UK site template was copied directly). The corporate ordering subsite's first snapshot is 2023-06-09.
  • Traffic (profile file): Ireland 64%, UK 36%; search 43% (non-brand about 34%, "place name + gift card/voucher"), direct 42.7%, referral 8.6%.

Where the first users came from: not starting from consumers but from local institutions - chambers of commerce/BIDs such as Drogheda BID signed on in the 2020 pandemic "buy local" mood, and promoted the cards and recruited merchants locally (the Love Drogheda card now has 230+ retailers). Later growth mainly came from moving existing paper voucher users in each town over as a group (Donegal's 1997 vouchers, Mullingar's from 2012), and from companies buying in bulk for employees.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Institutional distribution: each town programme is promoted by the local chamber/BID/council, with Miconex providing the platform and website; the local bodies' own sites (lovedrogheda.ie, thinkwaterfordfirst.ie, macroom.ie) send corporate orders to townandcitygiftcards.ie.
  • The product is the ad: every card carries the town brand, and recipients return to the site when they spend locally or check balances; Miconex says 15% of redemptions come from new customers and over 50% of cards are bought by people from outside the town.
  • Policy tailwind: higher small benefit tax exemption → corporate orders are 65%.
  • Search ranking: one product page per town, capturing "place name + gift card/voucher" searches.
  • Campaign operations: an awards campaign for "favourite local business" draws in merchants and residents.

Speculation:

  • The Christmas season (year-end corporate benefits) is the annual sales peak.
  • The 36% share from the UK may be due to the shared brand with the UK site and search spillover.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Ireland 2025 sales €17.4 million (about €10.5 million in 2024, backed out from +65%).
  • Miconex overall has £69 million+ cumulative local spend and 200+ programmes.
  • Fee rate not found.

Confidence and gaps

  • New site, written from scratch. The search results were mostly unrelated pages; the key figures come from Miconex's official news (2026-01-14) and Wayback homepage archives, which are fairly reliable.
  • Gaps: Miconex's founding year and origin story (from memory: founded around the 2010s by Colin Munro in Perth, starting with the Perth Gift Card, not verified), the first Irish towns, and the fee and profitability were not found.
