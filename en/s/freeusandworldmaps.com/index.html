Basics

  • Product: free downloadable clip art maps of the US / states / counties / Canada / world / countries / globes / Bible lands / antique maps (JPG, printable PDF), licensed for personal, commercial and educational use; paid: editable map collections (Gumroad https://bjdesign.gumroad.com/l/Ocakh), clip art collections, map coloring books; also affiliate links and an "Advertise" ad slot.
  • Founder: J. Bruce Jones / Bruce Jones Design Inc. (the 2009 footer phone area code 781 = Boston suburbs; the current About page says Mystic, CT); site maintenance is handled by BKJ Productions. All maps are drawn by Jones himself from US government public-domain maps, copyright "2006-2024".
  • Sister sites: bjdesign.com (earliest Wayback 1996-10, selling editable PowerPoint/Illustrator maps), mapsfordesign.com (2007), antiquehistoricalmaps.com, clipartmaps.com.
  • Launch: domain registered 2009-08-15 (RDAP), first Wayback snapshot 2009-08-20.
  • Traffic (profile): US 67%, Canada 16%, India 4%; search 84%, direct 12%, referrals 2.4%; non-brand about 84% (philippine map, usa map, blank us map with state names free, blank world map with country borders printable); social from Pinterest.

Where the first users came from: no direct evidence found. Speculated to be Google searches for "free us map / blank world map" and similar (in 2009 it already did SEO with a keyword-stuffed description paragraph: "printable world map, plus blank World and US map, along with world atlas map and blank maps") + existing customers and cross-links from the parent site bjdesign.com.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Free maps → every page promotes the paid editable version (bjdesign / mapsfordesign / Gumroad) → revenue funds drawing new maps → more long-tail pages.
  • Permissive license (commercial use, T-shirt printing allowed, "giving credit is always appreciated") → the maps are used everywhere and credited back.
  • Pinterest social source (profile).

Speculation:

  • Teachers and parents use and share the maps in school assignments and PPTs, creating passive backlinks; the 16% Canadian traffic comes from the long tail of Canadian province maps.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Paid: editable map collections ($149.95 in the bjdesign era), coloring books, clip art collections, affiliate commissions, ad slots. No revenue figures.
  • A small business run by one person plus outsourced maintenance, going for 17 years.

Confidence and gaps

  • The site is written from scratch. The founder, launch date and the pattern of funneling traffic to the paid sites are backed by original Wayback pages and the About page, so credibility is high.
  • Gaps: the historical traffic peak, paid conversion, and the specific source of the first users; there is almost no external discussion in the search results.
