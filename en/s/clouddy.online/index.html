Basics

  • Product: ClouDDy Media Player, multi-platform across smart TVs/phones/TV boxes/car head units/computers; playlists are processed in the cloud, managed through a web panel and not tied to a device; provides no channels itself.
  • Operator: neither the official site nor the user agreement names a company, country or governing law, only leaving player@clouddy.online; the Play Store developer name is "ClouDDy Online" (as retold by the LDPlayer page). The site footer has X @clouddyonline, Vimeo (official tutorial videos), GitHub clouddyonline (2 repos, activity not public), and payments go through Stripe (the footer has a Stripe Climate link). There are also tutorial or mirror sites such as clouddy.tv, whose official status is doubtful.
  • Not only serving Brazil: the UK's Entbox (Pro Technologies Limited, selling "native-language TV" packages) wrote in a 2026-09 tutorial "works in players such as ClouDDy and Ottplayer" and pre-configures accounts for users; RaspBerry TV (English and German sites) also treats ClouDDy as a standard player, and its back office can generate ClouDDy-specific playlist and EPG links in one click. This shows it is a generic "white-label player" for IPTV providers in many countries, in the same kind of ecosystem as Ottplayer (speculation that its developers are tied to the Russian-speaking OTT scene, unverified).
  • Pricing: monthly, 3 devices $1/month; annual, 1 device $2/year; annual, 3 devices $6/year; accepts Visa/MasterCard. The site has a "Reseller" partnership entry and claims to be "trusted by dozens of providers and hundreds of thousands of users".
  • Launch: earliest Wayback snapshot 2021-05-10; latest 2026-06-17.

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • IPTV resellers write ClouDDy into the setup tutorials they give customers and resell activations: subscriber → must install ClouDDy → pays $2 to activate; the site has a reseller partnership entry.
  • Provider back-office integration: providers like RaspBerry TV directly generate "ClouDDy-specific links" in their own back office and write ClouDDy into the standard onboarding flow, which amounts to B2B2C pre-installed distribution.
  • Seller sites write "Clouddy IPTV" tutorials for SEO, which incidentally brings search traffic to clouddy.
  • Speculation:
  • Bulk activation discounts/revenue share for resellers (implied by the "reseller" page, specific terms not found).
  • Organic Social comes from installation tutorials posted on TikTok/YouTube/WhatsApp by Brazilian IPTV sellers.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Very low pricing ($1-6), claims hundreds of thousands of users; if last month's checkout page had about 26K visits with half converting, monthly revenue would be around tens of thousands of dollars (speculation).
  • No public MRR/team information.

Confidence and gaps

  • The company entity, country and founders are all Not found; deliberately anonymous in the gray-market IPTV space.
  • When it took off in Brazil and which reseller recommended it first were Not found. The first users most likely came from subscribers of the earliest IPTV provider to integrate it (speculation); there is no founder account.
  • Second follow-up check: confirmed it is also adopted by UK (Entbox) and English/German bilingual (RaspBerry TV) providers, with a back-office integration that generates ClouDDy links in one click, strengthening the judgment of "piggybacking on IPTV provider distribution"; added the official X, Vimeo, GitHub and Stripe payments. The operating entity is still anonymous. X and Reddit would not open this round, and the same-name TikTok/YouTube accounts in search results are all unrelated to the product.
  • Compliance risk: depends on a pirated IPTV distribution chain, so it may be removed from stores or have payment channels blocked.
