Salesteam S.r.l., a small Milan company doing IT, e-commerce and logistics integration since 2003, registered spediamo.it in 2010 and launched an "online courier broker" in mid-2011: users fill in an order online, pay with PayPal and print the label, and partner couriers such as SDA pick up at the door, from about €6 for 0–3kg anywhere in Italy with no monthly contract, selling courier contract rates that used to be reserved for big customers to individuals and small eBay sellers. Early volume came from search terms like "spedire pacco" (ship a parcel) and "corriere espresso" and word of mouth among eBay sellers and on Yahoo Answers; it then locked in small merchants with a permanent revenue-share referral program ("invite friends: you get €0.10 for every parcel a friend ships, and your friend gets 15% off their first order") plus a banner affiliate program, top-up bonus credit, and an e-commerce API/CSV bulk import. Ten-plus years later it is almost entirely returning users coming straight back to order (82% direct, search almost all brand keywords), with 1,287 Trustpilot reviews.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: Italian online shipping platform (broker/aggregator): choose couriers such as Poste Italiane, BRT and SDA, with door pickup or drop-off at a SpediamoPoint (post office, bar, tobacconist, locker), for envelopes/parcels/pallets, international shipments, cash on delivery, insurance and POD; for e-commerce it offers an API, CSV/XML import and "triangular shipping" (shipping directly from the supplier's warehouse to the end customer).
  • Company: Salesteam S.r.l. (Milan, P.IVA 04119480962, "doing IT, e-commerce and integrated logistics inside a business consortium since 2003"); the website was originally built by thewebcompany.it. The founders' personal information was not found.
  • Pricing: pay per item, no subscription; now from €3.97, 0–2kg €4.84 drop-off at a point, €6.78 door pickup; cash on delivery €5. In 2012, 0–3kg was €5.99–6.59.
  • Domain: WHOIS created 2010-10-07; Wayback shows "Under construction" in 2011-03 and a real launch in 2011-06.
  • Traffic: Italy 100%; direct 82%, search 17% (brand keywords 99%), email 1.2%.

Where the first 1,000 users came from: the evidenced leads are the 2012 Yahoo Answers user recommendation and the review site article, plus the large amount of "spedire pacco" and "corriere espresso" search copy and the eBay seller page on the homepage. Speculation: the ramp-up came from Google searches for terms like "ship a parcel / cheap courier" plus word of mouth among eBay and small e-commerce sellers; the founding company already did logistics integration and held courier contract rates, which was the supply-side precondition.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Permanent revenue-share referrals: the referrer earns €0.10 per parcel a friend ships, the friend gets 15% off the first order, and there is also a banner affiliate program (since 2012–2013).
  • Small e-commerce lock-in: API, CSV/XML import, triangular shipping, eBay seller page; once a merchant connects, they keep shipping.
  • Prepaid top-ups with bonus credit raise repeat purchases.
  • No subscription, pay per item, lowering the barrier for individual users.

Speculation:

  • New customers now come mainly from word of mouth and brand search (brand keywords 99%); the early SEO and referral program have settled into returning-customer repeat purchases, and the traffic profile's "Viral" classification is really the referral program plus repeat purchases.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 1,287 Trustpilot reviews; revenue and order volume are not public.
  • A low-ticket broker business at €4–7 per parcel, with profit coming from the spread on courier contract rates (speculation).

Confidence and gaps

  • New site written from scratch (actually an old site from 2011). Launch date, corporate entity, early pricing and the referral mechanism have hard evidence from Wayback + WHOIS.
  • Gaps: the founders, order volume/revenue, and which year it truly took off were not found; search results returned only the official site and tracking tool pages, with no media coverage.
