Basics

  • Product: 21-acre site: native botanic garden, one of South East Queensland's largest native plant nurseries, Gardens Café, Country Store, farm animals (miniature dwarf goats, chickens and ducks), Forest Fairy Tale Trail, Turtle Lake, Little Sprouts indoor play; divided into three "realms": Animal Farmyard / Garden Realm / Wild Nature Realm; website sells day tickets, annual/monthly memberships, private tours and event tickets; separate food sub-site at food.fairhill.com.au.
  • Operator: CEO Angela Wright, self-described "family custodian"; TripAdvisor says "formerly known as Fairhill Native Botanic Gardens… under new ownership" (summary only); acquisition date and previous owner not found.
  • History: opened 1980 as a specialist native plant nursery (Visit Noosa, website "Est. 1980"); fairhill.com.au has Wayback records from 2001 (old nursery site).
  • Pricing: adults enter the cafe, shop and nursery free; children need a day ticket or membership; annual pass is 12 months unlimited (price not shown on site); Christmas lunch $95/person.
  • Claims: 100,000+ visitors, 2,300+ native species, 500+ reviews at 4.8, Facebook 9.4K + Instagram 7.4K, 2026 Sunshine Coast Business Awards finalist; encourages visitors to post with #FairhillGardens.

Growth loop

  • Evidence:
  • Established brand: a 46-year-old nursery and botanic garden that local gardening enthusiasts and long-time customers already know; brand keyword search dominates.
  • Event-driven: almost one festival/new facility every month (naming contest, opening, spring festival, pumpkin festival, Christmas), each covered by the local paper and family-activity sites.
  • Social: encourages #FairhillGardens photo posts, about 16k followers across FB+IG; annual pass members drive repeat visits.
  • Listed in regional tourism directories (Visit Noosa, TripAdvisor, dog-friendly venue site Pupsy).
  • Speculation:
  • The new owners' playbook is to turn a "nursery" into a "family attraction", bringing in new families through mums' groups and local parenting media word of mouth; the website itself is just the ticketing entry point.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Site claims 100,000+ visitors and 500+ reviews; revenue comes from day tickets, annual passes, the cafe, the nursery, events and private functions; no public revenue.

Confidence and gaps

  • The 1980 opening, the rename, and the 2026 new facilities and events are all corroborated by the website and local press.
  • Ownership change date, new owners' background, and how "100,000 visitors" is counted not found; Wayback only confirms the old name was still in use as of 2025-11, so the rename should fall between late 2025 and mid-2026.
  • It is an offline attraction, not a typical online growth case.
