This is not a new site but the long-established UK pet chip registry identibase (founded in 1989 by veterinary company Animalcare PLC), renamed and moved to the identi.pet domain in 2026. Its users come from the default registration when vets, breeders and rescues implant chips (institutional distribution), and two pieces of legislation lifted the user pool: mandatory chipping for dogs in England in 2016 and for cats in 2024. The business today is upgrading "free registration" into annual protection plans (£15–£39/year, smart tags). The surge in September checkout visits is because traffic from the old domain identibase.co.uk was 301-redirected to the new domain.One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a UK pet microchip registration database plus lost-pet recovery service. Checking which database a chip belongs to is free; paid plans are Base+ £15/year (change contact details anytime, notification when the chip is scanned, official certificate), Alert £39/year or £4.99/month (pushes lost-pet alerts to vets, dog wardens, police and nearby pet owners, and auto-generates lost-pet posters and social posts), and Locator from £39/year (QR smart tag plus scan location); there are also Transfer of Keepership, a vet/professional portal, and the identi app (GPS, QR tags).
  • Company: Identicare Limited, registered in England and Wales under number 12960490, address York. Since 2024 private equity firm Bridgepoint Group has been the majority shareholder, and it merged with Pet Microchip Services.
  • Scale: Trustpilot has 5,748 reviews (4.3 rating, 1,513 in the last 12 months, with the company actively inviting reviews); it claims 70%+ of pets on paid plans get recovered.

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Institutional distribution: chips are registered into the database when implanted at vet clinics / by breeders / at rescues; partnerships with Cats Protection and the Royal Kennel Club bring registrations directly.
  • Regulation-driven: mandatory chipping for dogs in 2016 and cats in 2024.
  • Transfer feature: when a pet changes hands, the new owner is pulled into the platform.
  • The Alert plan auto-generates shareable lost-pet posters and social posts (branded output).
  • Changing details costs money: several Trustpilot reviewers say they were asked to subscribe/upgrade when they wanted to change their pet's details (one mentions £15/year). Free-registration users are pushed to Base+ when they move house, change numbers or transfer ownership, which is the main gate for paid conversion.
  • Database merger migration: reviews mention moving in from other registries and "database merger" emails, matching the wholesale user move after merging with Pet Microchip Services in 2024.
  • Rescue adoption: one reviewer adopted from a rescue and waited for a registration email, showing rescues are also a default distribution channel.

Speculation:

  • The core revenue is converting free-registration users into annual plans (a freemium upgrade), with renewals driven by the worry of "keeping contact details up to date".
  • The jump in checkout traffic comes from the rename and domain change, not new growth.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Prices are £15/£39/£39+ per year; user counts and revenue are not public; the Bridgepoint acquisition amount was not found.
  • September checkout visits were 46.9K.

Confidence and gaps

  • Second follow-up check: added the 2026-10 report date for the Perfect Storm-led rename, the latest Trustpilot number (5,748 reviews), and three acquisition/conversion pieces of evidence from reviews: paying to change details, migration in from the database merger, and rescue adoption.   • Takeoff method is filled in as "Institutional distribution" (the vet/breeder channel); the real "takeoff" was in 1989, outside our data window.
