NaturalReader started in 2003 under the name "Natural Voice Reader" as a Windows desktop text-to-speech program: the free version could read Word, PDF and web pages, and the paid versions ($29.5 / $39.5) unlocked high-quality "natural voices". It followed the classic 2000s shareware route: use "free download" to occupy "free text to speech" on download sites and in search, then convert users with paid voices. Each generation since has kept up with platform shifts: in 2013 the homepage embedded an online try-it box, in 2018 it split into three lines (Online web version, Software and commercial licensing), and later came a Chrome extension, mobile apps and an EDU edition, with Gemini and ChatGPT HD voices added in 2026. For more than twenty years it has held big terms like "text to speech free", and with word of mouth among students, users with dyslexia and teachers it claims "over 10 million users".One-line growth story, compiled by us from public sources; speculation is marked separately below.

Basics

  • Product: a text-to-speech reader that can read documents, PDFs, web pages, books and scans (OCR) and export MP3; available on web, Windows/Mac, iOS/Android and as a Chrome extension; split into personal, commercial (YouTube voiceover, course materials) and EDU school editions. In 2026 it launched HD Pro Voices (based on Gemini, ChatGPT and others).
  • Pricing: the free version has unlimited basic voices, about 20 minutes a day of premium voices and about 5 minutes of AI voices; Plus $20.90/month or $119/year, Pro $25.90/month or $159/year; commercial is charged separately (source: a competitor review by castreader, which is biased). It used to be about $60/year.
  • Company: the developer is NaturalSoft Ltd., in Vancouver, Canada (from memory, unverified; the Google Play package name com.naturalsoft.personalweb supports the company name). The founder was not found.
  • Domain: registered 2003-02-14 (RDAP); earliest Wayback snapshot 2003-04-16.
  • Users: US 45%, India 4.5%, Canada 4.2%; search 42.5% (brand keywords 60%, plus big terms like "text to speech" and "tts"), paid search 8.5%, referrals include canva.com (3%) (channel profile).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Free version plus paid voices: since 2003 it has always been "free to use, good voices cost money", now changed to daily limits.
  • Search squatting: the title has long been "Free Text to Speech...", non-brand search includes big terms like "text to speech free" and "tts"; it also buys 8.5% paid search.
  • Multi-platform entry points: Chrome extension, apps and Canva referrals; users who meet it inside other products flow back.
  • Tiered licensing for monetization: personal, commercial and EDU sold separately, with commercial voiceover pushed to YouTube creators since 2018.

Speculation:

  • The EDU edition lets teachers set up classes and students join groups, bringing campus word of mouth (the channel profile's "viral" judgment rests on this; no concrete data was found).
  • Users with dyslexia and students recommend it to each other across schools and support organizations.

Revenue and scale clues

  • Claims 10 million+ users. Plus and Pro are priced at $119-159/year. At a 1% payment rate, annual revenue would be in the millions of dollars (speculation, unverified).
  • An independent company from 2003 to today; no funding information found.

Confidence and gaps

  • Written from scratch (no earlier report). Search results contain almost no coverage of its history, and the origin timeline relies entirely on four Wayback snapshots from 2003/2008/2013/2018, so the evidence is fairly solid.
  • Gaps: the founder, the company's location (from memory only), the user growth curve, and when the EDU edition launched were not found. The service was temporarily offline when Wayback tried to read the About page.
