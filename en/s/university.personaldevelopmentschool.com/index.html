Toronto therapist Thais Gibson had a fully booked private practice. In 2017–2018 she first opened an offline "personal development school" in Toronto (small classes by semester), while hosting a podcast for the alternative media outlet Collective Evolution and opening a YouTube channel in 2017. At the end of 2019 she moved the courses to an online membership university (the university subdomain), and in 2020 narrowed the content to "attachment styles" (anxious, avoidant, fearful avoidant), with a free "attachment style test" on the homepage as a lead capture entry point. This coincided with relationship psychology content booming on YouTube during the pandemic (the channel reached 546K subscribers and 84 million views), and the funnel of test → email → $67/month membership + lifetime purchase + coach certification worked. Her 2018 About page says she hosts a podcast for Collective Evolution.

Where the first users came from: the evidence points to offline: the "overflow clients" of her private practice in Toronto and her Transformation/Life Mastery offline classes (2018 About page), plus the alternative/spiritual audience brought by hosting the Collective Evolution podcast. The first online members came from her 2020 YouTube attachment videos + the free attachment test (the video descriptions all link to the test).

Growth loop

Evidence:

  • Founder content flywheel: YouTube with 546K subscribers/84 million views + her own podcast + guesting on other people's podcasts, with video descriptions all pointing to the free attachment test.
  • Test lead capture: the most prominent items on the homepage and navigation are the free attachment test and the free ebook, which move people into marketing emails in exchange for an email address (klaviyo.com appears among referral sources).
  • Member retention: weekly live webinars, a community forum and support-group Zoom calls (students mention the community Zoom is led by a dedicated host).
  • Coach certification: the IAT certification trains coaches who then serve clients with this theory, widening its reach (the spread effect is Speculation; the certification itself is evidenced).
  • Search: long-tail searches for attachment style terms (dismissive avoidant, etc.).

Speculation:

  • Test results are naturally suited to being screenshotted and shared with partners/friends, bringing some self-propagation; no direct data was found.
  • The 38% of traffic from Indonesia may come from paid ads or anomalous traffic and does not represent real users.

Revenue and scale clues

  • 30,000+ students (official site); membership from $67/month, lifetime $1,399, certification charged separately. With several thousand active monthly members, annual revenue could reach the millions of dollars (Speculation).
  • Trustpilot has reviews complaining that the course pacing is designed to "keep you from leaving the membership", indicating that subscription retention is the core.

Confidence and gaps

  • A new site written from scratch. The evolution from offline school → online university → attachment test has Wayback evidence; the YouTube data was fetched directly in 2026-10.
  • Gaps: which YouTube video was the takeoff point, the cumulative number of test takers, member count and revenue were not found; the about subpage returns 404; most search results were generic articles.
